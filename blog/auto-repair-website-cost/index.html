Auto repair: what a website costs in 2026

Updated September 30, 2026

Short answer

An auto repair website can use a modest subscription or require a custom build costing several thousand dollars or more. The useful comparison is the complete scope: service information, contact or booking process, content, ownership, and ongoing changes. Start with what your shop needs customers to do, then compare written quotes for the same work.

How much should your auto repair shop spend on a website in 2026? A clear service website can cost much less than a custom site connected to your scheduling systems. Before comparing prices, decide whether you need customers to call, request an appointment, or reserve a real opening on your shop calendar.

Define the shop website you actually need

Start with the questions your service advisor answers. Do you work on the customer's vehicle? Can they arrange a diagnostic visit? Where do they leave the car, and when will someone confirm the appointment? A useful website should answer those questions before adding more complicated features.

Write down the essentials: services, vehicle coverage, hours, location, contact details, and a clear explanation of requesting service. Include practical policies such as after-hours drop-off only if you offer them. You may need separate information for fleet work or a specialty such as European vehicles.

Distinguish an appointment request from live booking. A form that emails your advisor is a smaller job than a connection that checks available bays, technician skills, and appointment types. Do not pay for a complex connection until you know what problem it solves for the shop.

Compare DIY builder costs with your own time

Using the prices checked for this comparison, Wix Light is $17 a month billed yearly, or $204 a year, as of September 2026. Squarespace Basic is $19 a month as of September 2026. These are starting price references, not complete quotes for every feature your shop might need.

Check the provider's current terms before buying, including the billing arrangement and what your intended setup requires. Do not assume a quoted website plan includes every booking connection, business email requirement, or future change. Ask for a complete list for your proposed site.

The work also includes writing service descriptions, choosing images, checking the phone layout, and keeping information current. A builder can suit a shop owner who likes handling that work and has time to maintain it. It is less attractive when the site remains unfinished because customer vehicles always take priority.

Compare the time commitment using your real schedule. Who will update holiday hours, remove an expired special, or change the vehicle brands you accept? A low subscription does not answer those operational questions.

Know when a freelancer or agency earns the extra cost

Typical custom-project costs can range broadly from a few thousand dollars to many thousands, depending on scope. Treat that as a broad planning range, not a quote or a claim that every freelancer or agency charges the same way. Get a written proposal for your specific shop.

A freelancer may fit when you need individual design work, help organizing a specialty service offering, or a particular technical connection. An agency may fit a larger project involving several locations, separate service markets, or coordinated marketing work. The right choice depends on the deliverables and who will maintain them.

Ask what the build includes: interviews, copywriting, photography, revisions, launch work, and support afterward. A cheaper proposal may require you to supply finished text and photos. A more expensive one should explain what additional work you receive.

A one-time build also needs an ongoing plan. Hosting, domain renewal, software upkeep where applicable, and future content changes may be separate. Compare those obligations before deciding that the initial invoice is the whole cost.

Identify the features that change the scope

Some additions mainly require content; others require development and ongoing testing. Be precise about what you mean when requesting a feature.

  • Vehicle coverage pages: Explain actual makes and service limits without creating empty pages for every brand.
  • Appointment requests: Gather contact details, vehicle information, and the customer's concern for an advisor to review.
  • Live scheduling: Confirm how availability and appointment types stay synchronized with shop operations.
  • Multiple locations: Give each real shop accurate hours, directions, services, and contact routing.
  • Customer system connections: Specify which system, which data, who supports it, and what happens when it fails.

Do not collect payment details or unnecessary vehicle records through an ordinary inquiry form. Keep the initial request focused on arranging the conversation. Ask the provider how any sensitive workflow is handled before including it in the scope.

Compare written proposals before choosing

Ask each provider to list the initial cost, recurring costs, included changes, ownership arrangements, and exit process. Confirm who supplies the writing and photos and who tests the appointment flow before launch.

Check that the domain is registered in your name and that you retain appropriate access. Find out what can be moved if you leave, because taking a domain and moving a complete website are different questions. The domain ownership guide explains the distinction.

Choose the smallest scope that answers customer questions and reliably reaches your advisor. Add complexity when you can identify the specific problem it will solve, not because a demonstration looks impressive.

Frequently asked questions

Does a repair shop need custom online booking?

Not always; a clear appointment request may fit your operation. Live booking is useful only when it accurately reflects the visit types and capacity you can offer.

Should I pick the cheapest quote?

Compare what each quote includes and what your team must do afterward. A low price can be sensible when it covers your needs, but missing content or maintenance can leave work unfinished.

Can I start small and add fleet pages later?

Yes, provided the provider can add useful pages under clear terms. Prepare the fleet service scope and contact process before paying for additional content.
