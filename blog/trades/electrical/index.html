  • Electrical: what your website needs

    An electrician website needs a clear phone number, an accurate service area, and plain explanations of the electrical work you take on. Separate troubleshooting calls from planned installation estimates. Show real credentials and project evidence, then explain what customers should expect when they contact you and how work is approved.

  • Electrical: what a website costs in 2026

    An electrician's website budget should reflect the services you need to explain and the help you want maintaining it. DIY plans, done-for-you services, and custom projects cover different amounts of work. Compare content, ongoing changes, domain ownership, and contact tools alongside price, especially if panel upgrades, EV charging, or commercial projects need separate explanations.

  • Electrical: get more calls from Google

    To get more suitable electrical calls from Google, keep your Business Profile accurate and explain the specific jobs your crew accepts. Build useful pages for services such as troubleshooting, panel work, and EV charger installation, then make estimates and callback steps clear. Ask customers for honest reviews and track which inquiries become booked work.

  • Electrical: Google Business Profile guide

    Your electrician Google Business Profile should make it clear whether you handle repairs, upgrades, installation projects, or commercial work. Use accurate business details, choose categories that fit your real work, and describe services in customer language. Keep availability current and link to a website that explains estimates, coverage, and how to request a visit.

  • Local SEO checklist for electricians

    For electrician local SEO, make your business details consistent and explain the specific work you do in the places you actually serve. Check your Google Business Profile, service pages, photos, reviews, and mobile contact options. Keep technical website facts accurate, and measure suitable inquiries rather than relying on a promised ranking.

  • Electrical website mistakes

    Electrical websites lose useful inquiries when they hide contact details, mix unrelated services, or make homeowners interpret technical language before calling. Clarify the jobs you accept, explain how assessments and estimates work, and show genuine project evidence. Keep forms simple, coverage specific, and claims about credentials or availability accurate.

  • What to write on an electrician homepage

    Your electrician homepage should name your main services and coverage area, then explain how customers arrange troubleshooting or a project estimate. Use concrete examples such as panel work, lighting, and EV charger installation. Support your claims with accurate credentials and real projects, and keep the contact process simple for people who cannot diagnose the problem.

  • Electrical: get more Google reviews

    Get more electrician reviews by asking consistently after a completed service visit or project handoff. Share a direct Google review link and let customers describe the experience in their own words. Never offer rewards, request a particular rating, or screen out unhappy customers; use feedback to improve communication, estimates, and job closeout.

  • Electrical: Facebook page vs. a website

    Facebook can help an electrician stay visible to local contacts and show recent projects. A website gives prospective customers a stable place to check services, credentials, coverage, and the estimate process. Use Facebook for timely updates and referrals, then direct interested customers to clear information and a reliable contact route.

  • Electrical: blog post ideas for customers

    An electrician's blog should help customers understand a proposed job and prepare for a professional assessment. Good topics include EV charger estimates, panel replacement questions, lighting plans, and the information you need about recurring electrical problems. Write around services you offer, use verified project details, and avoid publishing instructions for hazardous electrical work.
