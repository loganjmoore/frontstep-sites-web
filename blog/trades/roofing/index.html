  • Roofing: what your website needs

    A roofing website should distinguish repair requests from replacement estimates, show the roof types you work on, and explain what an inspection and proposal involve. Use real project photos, accurate credentials, and a clear service area. Make contacting you easy without promising storm coverage, insurance outcomes, or a repair price before assessment.

  • Roofing: what a website costs in 2026

    A roofing website can be a modest annual expense or a custom project costing several thousand dollars or more. The difference is usually scope: service copy, project photography, galleries, forms, and ongoing updates. Start with clear repair and replacement pages, then compare providers on the same deliverables and ownership terms rather than the headline price alone.

  • How roofers get more calls from Google

    Make your Google Business Profile accurate, give roof repairs and replacements clear website explanations, and invite honest reviews after completed work. Show real projects and explain how inspections and estimates are arranged. Track whether calls fit your materials, service area, and capacity instead of treating every inquiry as a useful lead.

  • Roofing: Google Business Profile guide

    A roofer's Google Business Profile should tell homeowners which roof types and jobs you handle, where you work, and how to request an inspection. Keep business details and hours accurate, use genuine project photos, and explain repairs separately from replacements. Review the profile after storms or scheduling changes so public information matches what your office can offer.

  • Local SEO checklist for roofers

    Roofing local SEO starts with accurate business listings and useful pages for the roof systems and services you actually handle. Add genuine local project evidence, keep contact details consistent, and ask customers for honest reviews. Check page access and mobile usability, then measure appropriate assessment requests rather than assuming more visibility means better jobs.

  • Roofing website mistakes that cost calls

    Roofing websites lose useful inquiries when they hide contact details, blur repairs and replacements together, or show impressive photos without explaining the work. Make your service area, roof types, estimate process, and next step clear. Replace vague promises with real project details, and check that a homeowner can call or request an inspection easily from a phone.

  • What to write on a roofer homepage

    Your roofing homepage should tell visitors what roof work you do, where you work, and how to request the right next step. Separate leak and repair enquiries from planned replacements. Support your claims with real project details, explain the estimate process, and make the phone number and contact form easy to find.

  • Roofing: how to get more Google reviews

    Ask for an honest Google review after the roofing job reaches its agreed closeout, including the walkthrough and outstanding items. Make the link easy to use, keep participation optional, and use the same approach across customers. Never buy reviews, reward ratings, or make service follow-up depend on changing a review.

  • Is a Facebook page enough for a roofer?

    A Facebook page can help a roofer share recent jobs and stay visible to people who already know the business. A website is useful for keeping repair services, roof types, coverage, and the estimate process easy to find in one place. Use both when possible, with social posts pointing to stable pages that answer the homeowner's next question.

  • Roofing: blog post ideas for customers

    The best roofing blog ideas answer questions homeowners ask before an assessment or estimate. Write about repair decisions, proposal details, roof materials, storm-related concerns, and preparing for work. Use your real process and documented projects, avoid remote diagnosis or insurance promises, and give each article a clear next step toward the relevant service.
