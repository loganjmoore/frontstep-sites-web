  • Painting: what your website needs

    A painting website needs a clear service area, separate explanations of the work you offer, real project photos, and an easy estimate request. Show how you handle preparation, protect occupied homes, and agree on the scope. Homeowners need to understand what your estimate includes before they can compare it fairly.

  • Painting: what a website costs in 2026

    A painting contractor website can be a modest yearly expense or a larger custom project, depending on who builds it and what it needs to do. Budget for service descriptions, useful project photos, estimate requests, and ongoing updates first. Compare the full recurring cost and ownership terms before paying for custom features.

  • Painting: get more calls from Google

    To get more suitable painting calls from Google, keep your Business Profile accurate, explain your main services on your website, and ask customers for honest reviews. Show real preparation and finished work, name the towns you serve, and make estimate requests simple. Track which inquiries become useful site visits rather than judging progress only by search positions.

  • Painting: Google Business Profile guide

    Your painting Google Business Profile should describe the work you actually take, the towns you serve, and how customers can request an estimate. Use your real business name, choose an accurate category, and show finished projects with permission. Keep hours, contact details, services, and replies current so a homeowner can take the next step confidently.

  • Painting: local SEO checklist

    Start local SEO for your painting business with accurate business details and clear pages for the painting work you actually do. Add useful project examples, request honest reviews, and test the estimate journey on a phone. Focus on your real service area rather than repeating town names or promising results you cannot control.

  • Painting website mistakes that cost calls

    Painting websites lose useful inquiries when homeowners cannot tell what you paint, how you prepare, where you work, or how to request an estimate. Replace vague promises with specific service details and real project photos. Make the phone number and estimate form easy to use, then test the entire process on a phone.

  • Painting: what to put on your homepage

    A painting contractor homepage should name the work you do, the area you serve, and the next step for an estimate. Follow that with clear service descriptions, real project examples, and an explanation of preparation and scheduling. Write about the customer's rooms, surfaces, and concerns instead of relying on broad claims about quality.

  • Painting: how to get more Google reviews

    Ask painting customers for an honest Google review as part of a consistent project handoff, after you have walked through the work and explained any remaining items. Share a direct review link without requesting a particular rating. Invite feedback fairly, never offer an incentive, and handle touch-ups or complaints separately from the review request.

  • Painting: Facebook page vs. a website

    A Facebook page can help a painter share recent work and stay visible to people who already know the business. A website gives new homeowners a clear place to check services, preparation, coverage, and estimate details. Facebook alone can suit a referral-led schedule, but using both makes the information easier to organize.

  • Painting: blog post ideas for customers

    Write painting blog posts around the questions homeowners ask before an estimate: preparation, room access, finish choices, scheduling, and what the quote includes. Use your actual process and relevant project examples. Connect each article to the service it explains, and avoid universal promises about price, drying time, or how long a finish lasts.
