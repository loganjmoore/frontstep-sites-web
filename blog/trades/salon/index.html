  • Salon: what your website needs

    A salon or barbershop website needs a clear service menu, useful price information, real work photos, and a booking process customers understand. Explain appointment lengths where practical, consultation needs, stylist choices, and walk-in arrangements. Put policies before confirmation and help uncertain customers ask which service to book rather than guessing at technical names.

  • Salon: what a website costs in 2026

    A salon or barbershop can start with a simple website subscription, while typical custom builds can range from a few thousand dollars to many thousands. Cost depends on service menus, staff pages, content, and booking requirements. Linking to a booking system you already use is a different project from building custom scheduling or account features.

  • Salon: get more calls from Google

    To get more useful salon and barbershop inquiries from Google, keep your business details current and explain the services customers can actually book. Show relevant work, identify stylist specialties accurately, and make booking or calling easy. Give consultations, walk-ins, pricing, and appointment requirements clear explanations so customers choose an appropriate next step.

  • Salon: Google Business Profile guide

    A salon or barbershop Google Business Profile should show the services you actually offer, where clients visit, and how they choose the right appointment. Keep hours and booking links accurate, explain consultation requirements, and share real work with permission. Match the profile to your website so clients receive consistent guidance before they book.

  • Salon: local SEO checklist

    Start salon local SEO with accurate business details, a clear service menu, and a working booking link. Show the haircuts, color services, or beard work you actually offer, with real photos and honest reviews. Make your location easy to find and help new clients choose the right appointment before they reach your calendar.

  • Salon website mistakes that cost calls

    A salon website should help someone choose the right service, understand the price, and book with the right person. Fix unclear service names, missing stylist specialties, and booking links that drop clients into the wrong calendar. Show your actual work, explain consultation requirements, and make your location and phone number easy to find.

  • Salon: what to put on your homepage

    Your salon homepage should tell a new client what you do, who each service suits, what it costs, and how to book. Lead with your location and main services, then explain appointment choices, introduce your team, and show real work. Clear expectations matter more than clever beauty slogans.

  • Salon: how to get more Google reviews

    Ask for an honest Google review as a normal part of checkout, then make the review link easy to find. Give haircut, color, and barber clients the same invitation without discounts or pressure. Keep requests separate from photo permission, and respond to criticism without sharing appointment details or private conversations.

  • Salon: Facebook page vs. a website

    A Facebook page can work for a salon or barbershop that gets enough repeat business and keeps its booking details current. A website becomes useful when new clients need a clear service menu, stylist information, prices, or directions. Use Facebook for fresh work and open appointments, then send people to a stable page that explains how to book.

  • Salon: blog post ideas for customers

    Write salon and barber articles that help a client choose an appointment, prepare for a visit, or understand upkeep. Start with questions your team already answers about color, curls, fades, and consultations. Use your own service details and approved photos, then direct the reader to the relevant booking option without promising a particular result.
