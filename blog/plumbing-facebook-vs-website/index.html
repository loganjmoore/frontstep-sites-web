Is a Facebook page enough for a plumber?

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

A Facebook page can support referrals and show recent plumbing work, but a website gives customers a clearer place to check services, coverage, and contact details. Use Facebook for updates and conversation, then send people to a simple website for lasting information. Keep emergency availability accurate on both.

Your plumbing business already has a Facebook page, and neighbors sometimes tag you in recommendation threads. Do you really need a website as well? The answer depends on whether a new customer can quickly find the details they need when a pipe leaks or the hot water stops.

Give Facebook credit for what it does well

Facebook can be useful when your plumbing work grows through local relationships. A customer can share your page after you replace a leaking kitchen faucet. You can post an opening in your schedule, introduce a technician, or show a tidy water heater replacement with permission.

It also gives you a place to participate in community conversations where allowed. Answer a general question helpfully without turning every comment into a sales pitch. A neighbor who has seen your name before may feel more comfortable calling when they need a plumber.

A Facebook-first approach can be reasonable for a part-time operation that wants a limited amount of referral work. If your schedule is full and your customers already know how to reach you, a large website project may not be your next priority. Still, test the experience from the perspective of someone who has never heard of you.

Think about the customer with water under the sink

An urgent plumbing customer is trying to settle practical questions. Do you come to this neighborhood? Do you handle supply leaks? Is someone answering now? What happens after the call?

A feed of completed jobs can help establish that you are active, but it asks the customer to piece together basic information. A post about an opening last Tuesday says little about tonight. A photograph of a water heater does not explain whether you also repair existing units.

Use a website to put stable answers together:

  • The main number, visible and easy to tap.
  • The plumbing services you actually provide.
  • Your regular service area and any outer-area restrictions.
  • Normal working hours and truthful after-hours instructions.
  • The information you need to arrange a visit.

The plumber website guide explains how to arrange those essentials. A homeowner should not need to scroll through holiday greetings to find them.

Use a website for explanations that deserve a permanent home

Some plumbing decisions take more thought than a quick call. A homeowner comparing tank and tankless water heater replacement may want to understand your assessment process. A landlord needs to know whether you coordinate tenant access and who receives the estimate.

Write those explanations once on the appropriate service pages. You can then link to them from social posts and send them when answering repeated questions. That is easier to maintain than giving slightly different answers in a string of private messages.

Useful pages do not need long technical lectures. A sewer inspection page might explain what the appointment covers, what information the customer receives, and when further work would need a separate estimate. A fixture replacement page can clarify whether customers may supply their own faucet and how you handle compatibility questions.

Keep prices tied to your actual policy. If you cannot quote a concealed leak before seeing it, explain the visit and approval process. Avoid a vague "starting at" figure that bears little resemblance to the situations you usually encounter.

Compare the work involved, not just the bill

Maintaining Facebook takes time even when there is no separate website bill. Someone still needs to check messages, correct holiday hours, remove expired availability claims, and respond when customers ask about a job. A website also needs attention, especially when your phone number, services, or coverage changes.

Choose the arrangement you can keep accurate. If you enjoy taking job photos but dislike updating page layouts, you may want help with the website while continuing to handle social posts yourself. If you prefer doing your own edits, factor that work into your weekly schedule.

The plumbing website cost guide can help you compare the scope of different approaches. Before paying anyone, ask who controls the domain, who makes changes, and what happens when you leave. A low initial price is not enough information to judge the arrangement.

Make the two channels work together

Use your website address in the page's business information and in relevant posts. Send a reader to the water heater page when a post discusses replacement appointments. Send someone asking about weekend availability to current contact information, not to an old announcement.

On your website, include a social link if your page is active and useful. Keep the main call button prominent. A customer who has already decided to book should not be sent on another tour of your social feed.

Choose a clear role for messages. If you check Facebook only after your last job, say that it is not the right channel for urgent scheduling. Do not invite a homeowner to send an emergency message to an inbox nobody monitors during the day.

The guide to showing emergency availability helps you write accurate contact instructions. Align the wording with what your crew can actually support during evenings, weekends, and busy periods.

Run a simple customer test before adding more content

Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to look you up on their phone. Give them a specific situation: a rental property has a blocked toilet, and they need to know whether you serve the address. Watch which page or post they open and where they hesitate.

Repeat the exercise with a planned job, such as replacing a worn-out water heater. Can they find enough information to make an informed call? If they cannot tell whether you perform the work, fix that gap before posting another promotional image.

Try these checks yourself too:

  1. Open your Facebook page in a browser where you are not signed in.
  2. Open the website on a phone and tap the number.
  3. Submit a test inquiry and confirm where it arrives.
  4. Compare services and hours across both places.

The phone-first website guide offers more practical checks. The aim is a short, clear path from a plumbing problem to a useful conversation.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with only a Facebook page?

Yes, especially if you are testing a small referral-based business and can keep the page current. Add a website when customers need clearer service information or you want one address to use across listings, vehicles, and invoices.

Do I need to post on Facebook every day?

Choose a schedule you can sustain with useful updates. A real project explanation or a truthful scheduling notice is more helpful than daily filler. Keep permanent service details on your website so your posting schedule does not control access to them.

Should all inquiries go through Facebook Messenger?

Only if you can reliably manage that channel and set accurate expectations. Urgent plumbing requests need clear instructions about the best way to contact you. Keep a direct phone option available wherever you advertise call-based service.
