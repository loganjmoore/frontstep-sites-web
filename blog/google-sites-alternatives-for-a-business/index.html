Google Sites alternatives for a business

Updated October 3, 2026

Short answer

Google Sites is free, but it can't set a custom meta description on each page, so Google writes the search snippet for you. Wix Light ($204 a year) and Squarespace Basic (about $228 a year) are the usual paid alternatives, and you do all the editing. Front Step Sites is $99 a year with the domain included, and you ask for changes in your account.

If you already have a Google Site, you're probably wondering whether it's good enough for a business or whether it's time to move. Google Sites is a free website builder that comes with a Google account. You want a site that looks like a real company, shows up when someone searches your trade, and doesn't eat your evenings. Here's how the three options stack up.

Why does Google Sites fall short for a business?

Google Sites costs nothing to build and host with a Google account, so price is never the problem. Control is. According to nDash, which tried the new builder, Google Sites does not offer a custom meta description for each page. Their words: "Google Sites does not have this option." Google writes the snippet from your page text instead, so the line a customer reads under your name in search is not one you choose.

That's fine for a club page or a wedding site. For a plumber, a dentist, or a landscaper, that snippet is free advertising every searcher sees, and you don't get to write it.

What does each option cost over three years?

A yearly price hides the real gap, since you pay it every year. Here are three years on each option. The DIY totals leave out domain fees, since those vary by registrar.

  • Google Sites: $0, plus your domain
  • Wix Light: $612 ($204 a year)
  • Squarespace Basic: $684 (about $228 a year)
  • Front Step Sites, $99 plan: $297, domain included
  • Front Step Sites, Grow plan: $870 ($290 a year), with unlimited changes the same business day and your Google reviews shown on the site

The $99 plan is the cheapest paid option here, but it caps changes at two a month. If you update your hours, menu, or service list often, the Grow plan's price is the one that matches how you work.

What should you ask any builder before you pay?

Ask these questions in writing, whichever option you pick. Get the answers before you put a card on file.

  • Can I set a page title and a description for each page, and where do I edit them?
  • Is the contact form on my plan, and where do the emails go?
  • Whose name is the domain registered in, and can I take it with me?
  • What does it cost to leave, and what happens to my pages when I do?

When is Google Sites still the right choice?

Google Sites is the right call for a temporary page, like a sign-up for a three-week fundraiser or a placeholder while you decide what you need. It's free, you can build it in an afternoon, and a volunteer can pick it up later. Once customers find you through search and you need control over what they read, move to a business site.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Sites free for a business website?

Yes, to build and host it with a Google account, there's no hosting fee. The domain name is the one cost you still pay if you want your own address. It's the cheapest option on paper. You still do all the work, though, and the snippet problem doesn't go away.

Can I connect my own domain to Google Sites?

Yes. Google's Workspace help describes mapping a custom web address to a Google Site. The steps differ depending on whether you use a Workspace account or a free Google account, so follow the current help page rather than an old tutorial.

Is a $99 site really better value than a free one?

It depends on what you value. A free Google Site costs less, but the $99 plan includes the domain, hosting, and a person who makes your changes. Over three years, that's $297 for the plan against your own hours, which add up faster than most owners expect.
