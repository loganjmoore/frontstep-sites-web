Google Business Profile for painting contractors: a setup and upkeep guide

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Your painting Google Business Profile should describe the work you actually take, the towns you serve, and how customers can request an estimate. Use your real business name, choose an accurate category, and show finished projects with permission. Keep hours, contact details, services, and replies current so a homeowner can take the next step confidently.

How do you set up a Google Business Profile that helps homeowners understand your painting business? Start with accurate contact information and a clear picture of the jobs you accept. Someone searching for "cabinet painter near me" needs different information from a property manager arranging an exterior repaint.

Establish an accurate business identity

Search for your business before creating a profile so you can check whether a listing already exists. Keep ownership under an account your business controls, and use Google's available access-sharing options when someone helps manage it. Follow the verification process Google presents rather than relying on an old tutorial's promised method.

Use the business name customers see on your estimates and vehicle. Do not append a list of towns or "best cheap house painter" to the name. Choose the available category that most accurately describes your main painting work, with additional categories only for genuine business activities. Google's business representation guidelines explain these requirements.

If you work from home and travel to customers without receiving them at your address, use the appropriate service-area setup and hide the residential address. An equipment storage unit is not a customer showroom. Keep your phone and website information accurate, with a number someone on your team checks during the stated hours.

Describe the jobs you actually want

Make your services more useful than a single entry saying "painting." A homeowner may be trying to distinguish cabinet refinishing from wall painting or wondering whether preparation is part of the job. Include the services you actually provide, using the choices and descriptions available in your profile.

For example, your list might include:

  • Interior wall and ceiling painting, with an explanation of how you assess repairs.
  • Exterior siding and trim painting, with surface condition reviewed during the estimate.
  • Cabinet painting or refinishing, if your shop performs that work.
  • Deck or fence staining, where that is part of your service.
  • Rental turnover painting, if your scheduling and scope suit property managers.

Do not add wallpaper removal, drywall work, or commercial coatings just because another painter lists them. Say what you handle yourself and discuss any separate work during the estimate.

Keep the profile description readable. Explain your core services and area without turning it into a string of search phrases. Put longer explanations on your website; the painting website guide covers the information a prospect needs after clicking through.

Match service areas and hours to real capacity

Choose towns where you genuinely take painting jobs. Think about travel for the estimate, preparation, painting, and any return visit. A distant cabinet inquiry may make sense for one business and be outside another company's practical service area.

Do not imply you maintain offices in every listed town. Your website can explain the areas you cover without inventing local branches. If you accept distant work only for larger projects, make that clear during the inquiry rather than listing everywhere you would theoretically drive.

Set hours that reflect when customers can reach your business, and update holiday changes. An unattended estimate form does not mean a painter is available around the clock. If crews start before the office answers calls, explain your contact arrangements on the website.

Check that profile hours, your contact page, and voicemail do not give conflicting instructions. A homeowner should know whether to call about a current project or submit information for a future estimate. The painting local SEO checklist helps you review those details together.

Show preparation as well as finished paint

Use photographs from your own jobs, with permission. Show complete rooms, exterior elevations, trim details, and cabinet finishes that represent the work you want to sell. A close-up can show finish quality, while a wider view explains the scope.

Preparation photos can answer doubts that a finished wall cannot. Floor protection, masking, and organized work areas help explain your process. Avoid customer faces, mail, valuables, house numbers, or anything else the customer did not agree to make public.

Use comparable lighting and angles for before-and-after pictures. Do not use edits that change the paint color or hide surface defects. If a picture shows cabinet doors finished off-site, describe that accurately rather than suggesting the whole project happened in the customer's kitchen.

Choose a manageable set of useful images. One clearly described project is better material than a pile of nearly identical wall photographs. Store permission notes with your project records so future uploads do not depend on someone's memory.

Maintain updates, questions, and review replies

Use profile updates, where available, for information a customer can act on. A recent exterior project can explain how you assessed peeling trim, while a cabinet project can point to your consultation process. Publish genuine scheduling information rather than a permanent "last chance" offer.

If your public profile offers a questions section, check it for unanswered customer questions. Answer only what you know, and move address-specific estimates into a private conversation. Do not assume that every profile has the same features or that a post will appear in a particular position.

Put recurring questions on your own website too. "Do I buy the paint?" and "Can we stay home during the work?" deserve explanations based on your actual process. Avoid universal answers when the project scope affects arrangements.

Reply calmly to reviews, thanking the customer without revealing private job details. If a review mentions missed touch-ups, acknowledge the concern and invite direct contact rather than arguing about the invoice. Follow the painting review guide for a consistent request process.

Set a regular reminder to inspect the public profile as a customer. Test the website destination and phone number, then check whether services, hours, and photos still represent your current business. Accurate information helps customers decide; it does not guarantee a particular ranking.

Frequently asked questions

Should cabinet painting have a separate profile?

A service alone is not a reason to create another business listing. Describe cabinet painting within your genuine business profile and give it a useful page on your website.

What if my phone number changes?

Update the profile and your website promptly, then check other places where customers find the old number. Test the new number from another phone so you know the call reaches the intended person or voicemail.

Should I publish an exact price for every job?

Only publish prices you can explain and honor for a defined scope. For variable work, describe the estimate process and the factors you assess, such as preparation, access, and surfaces.
