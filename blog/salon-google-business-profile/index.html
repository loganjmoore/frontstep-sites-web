Salon: Google Business Profile guide

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

A salon or barbershop Google Business Profile should show the services you actually offer, where clients visit, and how they choose the right appointment. Keep hours and booking links accurate, explain consultation requirements, and share real work with permission. Match the profile to your website so clients receive consistent guidance before they book.

A new client searches for "curly haircut near me," while someone else wants a barber who accepts walk-ins. Your Google Business Profile should help each person decide whether your shop is a fit. Start with the services and booking details that prevent an unsuitable appointment.

Establish the right listing for your shop

Search for your salon or barbershop before creating a profile. If a listing already exists, follow the appropriate ownership process rather than making a duplicate. Keep the business in control of its access, including when a receptionist or outside helper manages updates.

Use the name clients see on your signage and website. Enter the correct location details so someone can find your door, not merely the shopping center. A suite number and a clear entrance photo can be useful for a salon inside a larger building.

Booth rental and shared premises need care. Do not automatically create extra profiles for every chair or treat every professional as the same business. Check Google's business representation guidance for your actual arrangement before adding or combining listings.

Your salon website should make the relationship clear too. A client needs to understand whether they are contacting the salon, an independent professional, or a particular location before booking.

Match services to real client needs

Choose the available primary category that best describes your main work. Add other categories only where they reflect genuine services. A barbershop focused on cuts and beard grooming should not imply that it offers a full color menu if it does not.

Build service descriptions from your real appointment list. Depending on your business, examples might include:

  • Haircuts, with any consultation or styling included explained.
  • Clipper cuts, fades, and beard shaping where offered.
  • Root color, highlights, or other color services you actually perform.
  • Curly hair services with a clear description of your approach.
  • Blowouts or occasion styling with appropriate booking information.

Explain what a new client should do when the service is uncertain. Someone searching for a major color change may need a consultation before reserving a long appointment. State that process instead of inviting them to guess between technical color terms.

If prices depend on hair length, density, service scope, or stylist, explain your actual approach. Do not publish a misleading starting price without context. Google's service information guidance describes the service details profiles can support; keep the fuller explanation on your website.

Make the booking path match the appointment

Test the website and appointment links visible on your profile. They should take a new client to the correct shop and a useful next step. A link that opens the wrong branch's calendar creates extra work for both the client and the desk.

If different professionals manage their own appointments, explain how to choose the right person. Do not make a visitor work out your internal arrangement from a list of unexplained names. Include a way to ask for help when they are unsure which service fits.

Some services need a conversation first. Corrective color, an occasion booking for several people, or a service requiring particular preparation may not belong in a simple instant-booking path. Describe the actual process without giving universal instructions about treatments.

Google's business link guidance explains the kinds of action links profiles may display. Check your visible links as a customer, including any that lead to another service. A successful click is only useful if the destination gives the correct options.

Keep hours and walk-in expectations clear

Distinguish a staffed shop's opening hours from the availability of a particular stylist. An open salon does not necessarily have a color appointment available, and a barber working late one evening does not mean the whole business keeps those hours.

Publish accurate customer-facing hours where appropriate. If your business operates only by appointment, check Google's current hours guidance for that setup. Use your website to explain how appointments are requested and when someone handles inquiries.

State whether you accept walk-ins and how clients should check availability. Do not promise that a walk-in can always be seated immediately. A useful explanation describes your actual queue or inquiry process without inventing a waiting time.

Update holidays, temporary closures, and changes to your regular schedule. Review the website at the same time. The salon homepage guide can help you keep these practical details near the booking action.

Show real work and maintain helpful replies

Use photos of your shop, entrance, and completed work with permission. For hair photos, ask about the intended public use rather than assuming agreement because a client allowed a picture at the chair. Respect a refusal without making it awkward.

Show a range that reflects your actual work. Comparable lighting matters when showing a color result; heavy filters can misrepresent the shade. A useful caption describes the service without claiming that another person's hair will produce an identical result.

Where posts are available, share concrete updates such as a new service you are ready to book, holiday opening, or a consultation process. Keep availability claims current. Do not leave an old "appointments this week" message as the main instruction after that week has passed.

Review any visible questions and answers, and keep permanent answers on your own site as well. Useful topics include choosing a service, consultations, walk-ins, and how to find the entrance. You do not need a particular profile feature to make those answers available on your website.

Reply to reviews without revealing private consultations or personal information. If a client dislikes a result, acknowledge the concern and invite a private conversation about the appointment. The salon review guide explains how to request feedback consistently and keep replies professional.

Frequently asked questions

Should each stylist have a separate profile?

Do not assume that every team member needs one. Eligibility depends on the actual business and professional arrangement. Check the current guidelines, then make your website and booking information accurately explain who clients are contacting.

Can I list a color service without a fixed price?

Explain how the price is assessed and when a consultation is needed. A customer should understand the next step before reserving time. Avoid a low starting price that appears to cover work it does not include.

What if I have no client photos I can publish?

Start with your entrance, interior, and appropriate team photos. Add examples of work only when you have permission. A clear service menu and booking path remain useful even while your public portfolio is small.

Should I reply publicly to a complaint about a haircut?

Acknowledge the concern briefly and offer a private way to discuss the appointment. Avoid arguing about the client's appearance or publishing consultation notes. Use your normal service recovery process without making help conditional on changing the review.
