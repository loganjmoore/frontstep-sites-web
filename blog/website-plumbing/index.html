Plumbing: what your website needs

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

A plumber website needs a working call button, clear service hours, the towns you cover, and plain descriptions of the jobs you take. Put those details before company history or decoration. Add real job photos, honest pricing guidance, and a short request form so customers can decide whether you are the right plumber to call.

What does your plumbing website need so someone with a leaking water heater actually calls you? Start with your phone number, availability, service area, and the problems you fix. A customer should be able to find those answers before reading your company story or scrolling through job photos.

Put the phone number and availability first

The first screen should identify you as a plumber, name your main service area, and offer a clear way to call. Use a button labeled "Call for plumbing service" with the number visible beside it. Keep a call option on every service page, too.

Test the button on your own phone. Does it open the correct number in the dialer? If you have an old office line, a new mobile number, and a separate after-hours line floating around, decide which one customers should use and explain any difference.

Someone searching "emergency plumber near me" needs an honest answer about availability. Say whether you answer overnight, offer weekend appointments, or return messages during business hours. Do not advertise round-the-clock emergency service if the call goes to voicemail until morning.

A useful example to adapt is: "Drain clearing and water heater repairs in your town. Call to check today's availability." Replace the town and services with your own. Our guide to what to write on a plumber homepage helps you build the rest around that opening.

Name the plumbing problems you actually handle

"Full-service plumbing solutions" leaves the customer doing the work. A backed-up kitchen sink, a toilet that keeps running, and a water heater with no hot water are different reasons to call. Name them in words you hear on the phone.

Start with a short service list. Depending on your work, that might include:

  • Clogged sink and shower drains.
  • Toilet repairs and replacements.
  • Leaking pipes and fixture repairs.
  • Tank and tankless water heater service.
  • Sewer line inspections and repairs.
  • Sump pump repairs and replacements.

Only include work you perform. If you do residential service but not restaurant grease lines or commercial plumbing, say so. That helps both you and the caller avoid a wasted conversation.

Give your main services their own pages when you have enough useful detail. A water heater page can explain which types you service, what information to have ready, and how you assess repair versus replacement. Put a call button after those answers. Skip pages that repeat the same paragraph under a different service name.

Make your service area easy to check

List your core towns in plain text near the top of the homepage. Then give customers a fuller service-area list on the contact page or a dedicated page. A map can help, but people should not have to guess whether they fall inside a colored circle.

Explain boundaries that affect booking. Perhaps you offer routine appointments across the county but limit evening emergency visits to nearby towns. If travel charges apply outside your usual area, tell people before they request a visit.

Keep the business name, phone number, and hours accurate wherever customers find you. Use the Google Business Profile guide for plumbers when reviewing the details shown alongside your website.

Avoid creating a page for every surrounding town with only the place name changed. A town page is more useful when you can describe actual work there, show a permitted job photo, or explain a real scheduling difference.

Explain the visit and the pricing process

Customers want to know what happens after they call. Give them a short sequence: describe the problem, confirm the address and availability, arrange the visit, then discuss the proposed work and price. Adjust that sequence to match how your business operates.

You do not need to publish one price for every possible leak. You should explain whether you charge a service-call or diagnostic fee, when you provide an estimate, and whether after-hours visits have different charges. Publish actual fees where you can keep them current.

For a drain clearing page, explain that access and the condition of the line can affect the work. For a water heater replacement, explain that you need to assess the existing installation before confirming the scope. Specific explanations are more helpful than "Call for our best price."

Do not promise a fixed arrival time or same-day repair unless you can support it. If you are deciding what to disclose, should you put prices on your website covers ways to explain costs without guessing at a job you have not seen.

Show evidence from real plumbing work

Use clear photos of your team, service vehicle, and finished work. A tidy under-sink repair or completed water heater installation can give a customer something concrete to look at. Add a short caption describing the job and the problem you addressed.

Get permission before showing a customer's property. Keep addresses, family photos, and other identifying details out of the frame. You can describe a general service area without naming the homeowner or posting their street.

Display credentials accurately and keep them current. Avoid vague badges that imply an approval you do not hold. If you show customer reviews, use real wording and make the source clear rather than turning a few comments into an invented satisfaction score.

Choose useful evidence over a large gallery. A photo and explanation of a cramped replacement job can answer more questions than a row of stock pictures showing spotless bathrooms.

Give routine jobs a simple request form

Not every visitor has an emergency. Someone planning a faucet replacement may prefer to send a request after dinner. Offer a short form alongside the phone number, with only the details you need to respond.

Ask for the person's name, contact details, town, and a short description of the job. Make photos optional. Explain when you normally reply and that submitting the form does not confirm an appointment.

Before you call the site finished, check it like a customer:

  1. Open it on your phone using a cellular connection.
  2. Find a service and check whether your town is covered.
  3. Tap the phone number and confirm the digits.
  4. Send a test request and verify that it reaches you.
  5. Check that the confirmation message explains what happens next.

Remove oversized video or pop-ups if they get in the way. A customer should be able to read and act without waiting for decoration.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with a one-page plumbing website?

Yes, if it clearly covers your services, towns, hours, proof, and contact options. Add separate service pages when you have useful questions to answer about specific work. You do not need a large site to explain a small, focused business.

Should I say I offer emergency plumbing?

Only if you actually offer it, and explain the hours and area covered. If availability varies, ask customers to call to check. An unanswered promise creates a poor first impression.

Do I need a blog before launching?

Get the main service information and contact process right first. A blog can come later, using questions your customers regularly ask. It should not delay fixing a broken call button or missing service area.

What should I fix first on an existing site?

Check the phone number, form delivery, hours, and service area first. Then review whether the page names the jobs you want. Fix anything that prevents a suitable customer from reaching you before adding more content.

The short version

Build your plumber website around the caller's immediate questions: can you do this job, do you come here, and how do I reach you? Support those answers with a clear pricing process and real examples of your work. Test the phone and form yourself before sending people to the site.
