Pressure washing: Google Business Profile

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Build your pressure washing Google Business Profile around the services you actually provide and the area your crew can reliably reach. Keep your business name, phone, website, and hours accurate. Add genuine job photos and useful service descriptions, then check customer questions and reviews as part of your regular office routine.

How do you set up a Google Business Profile that brings suitable pressure washing inquiries? Start by making it clear what you clean, where you travel, and how someone can reach you. A homeowner searching for driveway cleaning needs a different answer from a restaurant manager arranging recurring walkway cleaning.

Claim the right business and retain access

Search for your business before creating a new listing. If a profile already represents your company, use Google's process to claim or request access to it. Avoid creating another version simply because the old one contains the wrong phone number.

Keep owner access connected to an account your business controls. Give a marketing helper appropriate access without making their personal login your only way in. Save account recovery information where the owner can retrieve it.

Use the name customers see on your invoices and vehicle lettering. Do not add a string of towns or services to the name just to match searches. Put driveway washing and house washing in the appropriate service information instead.

Follow the verification steps offered for your business and provide accurate evidence if requested. There is no universal verification shortcut to promise. The guide to getting on Google Maps explains the broader setup process.

Describe the operating location and coverage honestly

If you travel to customers and do not receive them at your base, use a service-area setup and hide the address. A storage yard or home office is not automatically a public storefront. Google's business representation guidelines explain the distinction.

Choose coverage based on jobs you can actually service. Consider travel, setup, water arrangements, and whether a small patio clean makes sense at the edge of your area. Listing every distant town can create inquiries that your crew cannot accept.

Do not create a separate profile for each neighborhood you wash. Keep your website's coverage consistent with the profile and explain any project-dependent travel arrangements directly to customers.

Check what the public sees after edits. A homeowner should not drive to your house expecting a staffed counter, and a commercial customer should not have to guess whether you work in their part of town. Clear coverage saves unnecessary calls on both sides.

Match categories and services to the work you perform

Choose the most accurate available primary category for your main business. Review additional categories only for services you genuinely offer. Category choices can change, so select from the options Google currently provides rather than copying a list from an old tutorial.

Build service descriptions around actual jobs:

  • Driveway and concrete walkway cleaning.
  • House washing appropriate to the surfaces you handle.
  • Patio and pool-deck cleaning, where offered.
  • Fence or deck cleaning, if included in your services.
  • Commercial entrances and recurring exterior cleaning.
  • Gutter or roof cleaning only if you actually provide it.

Explain relevant boundaries. A customer may use "pressure washing" to describe several cleaning methods. Describe how you assess the material and choose your approach without implying high pressure is suitable for every surface.

Do not promise that every oil mark, rust stain, or discoloration will disappear. Explain that you assess stains and surface condition before confirming expectations. Google's service management guidance describes how service information can be added to a profile.

Use photos that explain the job instead of exaggerating it

Photograph actual work with permission. A driveway, shaded siding, a small patio, and a commercial entrance show different services. Include enough context that a reader can tell what was cleaned.

For comparison photos, use similar angles and avoid presenting wet concrete as if it were the final dry result. Label the situation accurately. Water and lighting can change the appearance, so do not edit images to make the contrast look more dramatic.

Add a genuine crew or equipment photo if it helps customers recognize who will arrive. Keep license plates, house numbers, and people out of the frame when permission does not cover them. Skip borrowed images that suggest you completed another contractor's work.

Link the profile to a website that expands on the same services. The pressure washing website guide explains how service descriptions, project examples, and estimate requests can work together.

Keep hours, updates, and answers practical

Publish hours that reflect when the business operates and customers can reasonably contact you. Do not claim round-the-clock service because an online form accepts messages overnight. Explain your response expectations on the linked website.

Check hours around holidays and planned closures. If weather disrupts the schedule, communicate with affected customers directly. A general profile update does not replace telling a homeowner that their booked house wash has moved.

Where your profile supports updates, share useful information such as a newly offered service or a seasonal scheduling note. Remove or replace expired offers. Avoid treating frequent posting as a guaranteed route to better placement.

Where customer questions are available, answer them accurately. Common topics include water access, gates, moving vehicles, and whether someone needs to be home. Also answer those questions on your own site so customers can find them regardless of how Google displays the profile.

Handle reviews and check the route to an estimate

Invite honest reviews after completed work without discounts or a requested rating. Keep the invitation open to customers with concerns too. The pressure washing review guide explains timing around the walkthrough and finished result.

Reply calmly to feedback. If someone raises a concern about siding, plants, or a remaining stain, arrange a private discussion. Do not diagnose the issue from a public comment or publish their address to defend your work.

Set a recurring reminder to check the public profile. Open the website link, confirm the phone number, and review the displayed services and hours. Ask an office colleague to follow the same path a customer would take.

Then submit a test estimate request on your website. Confirm that the message arrives with the service and location details. Profile activity is only useful if an interested customer can reach a real person and understand the next step.

Frequently asked questions

Can I show my home address if I store equipment there?

Equipment storage alone does not make it a customer-facing location. If you travel to customers and do not serve them there, use the appropriate service-area arrangement and hide the address.

Should I list roof cleaning to get more inquiries?

Only list services you genuinely provide. A misleading service listing wastes customers' time and can create expectations your crew cannot meet.

Do I need to post an update every day?

There is no useful reason to publish filler just to maintain a daily streak. Keep essential information correct and share an update when you have something relevant to tell customers.

Should I answer questions about cleaning chemicals publicly?

Give an accurate general explanation of your process and how customers can discuss concerns. Avoid universal claims such as completely harmless, and do not publish technical instructions you cannot support.
