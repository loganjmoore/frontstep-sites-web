Auto detailing: Google Business Profile

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

An auto detailer's Google Business Profile should clearly explain whether customers visit a studio or you travel to their vehicle. Keep your service area, detailing packages, photos, hours, and website link accurate. Use the profile to answer practical booking questions about vehicle condition, access, and service scope rather than promising a particular search position.

How do you set up a Google Business Profile that helps people book the right detailing service? Make it clear where the work happens and what each service includes. Someone searching for "mobile interior detailing near me" should not have to call just to discover that you only accept vehicles at a studio.

Start with your real business and working location

Look for an existing profile before creating another one. If your business already appears, follow Google's process for managing it and keep ownership under an account you control. A former helper should not be the only person who can update your hours or phone number.

Use your real business name. If you travel to customers and do not receive them at your address, keep that address hidden and use an accurate service area. Google explains these requirements in its business representation guidelines.

For your own planning, distinguish a detailing studio from a garage used only for storing equipment. Think about what happens when someone follows directions: can they actually check in there? If not, your profile and website need to explain the mobile appointment process clearly.

Choose the towns you can realistically serve while allowing time for setup and travel. Explain any travel considerations on your website, particularly for customers outside your regular route.

Choose categories and describe actual detailing services

Choose the closest available category for your main detailing business. Add another only when it describes a substantial service you actually provide. Browse Google's current choices rather than assuming a phrase from another detailer's profile is available or appropriate.

Build your service descriptions around customer decisions. "Premium package" means little until you explain whether it covers seat cleaning, exterior washing, paint correction, or protection. A customer with a minivan full of pet hair needs different information from someone booking a recently purchased sports car.

Useful service descriptions might cover:

  • Interior detailing: Specify the areas cleaned and explain how you assess stains, pet hair, and unusually heavy soil.
  • Exterior detailing: Describe the wash and finishing work your package actually contains.
  • Paint correction: Explain that you inspect paint condition before agreeing on the work and expected result.
  • Protective coatings: Describe your consultation and preparation process without making unsupported durability promises.

Only include services you offer. Keep the fuller explanations on your site using the auto detailing website guide.

Make hours and appointment expectations agree

Publish hours that match how customers can actually deal with your business. If a studio works by appointment, make that expectation clear wherever appropriate. A visitor should not assume an open listing means you can accept an immediate walk-in while another vehicle occupies the bay.

Mobile work needs additional explanation. State whether you need access to water or power, where the vehicle must be parked, and how you handle unsuitable weather. These are your operating policies, so write what you actually do rather than copying another company's setup.

Keep a short answer ready for "Can you detail my car in my apartment parking lot?" Explain that the customer should confirm property permission and suitable access before you finalize the appointment. Do not assume every parking space works for your equipment.

Update holiday changes and planned closures. Check the website at the same time so the profile does not invite a booking that the site says you cannot take.

Use photos that help customers judge the work

Show the work people are hiring you to do: cleaned seat stitching, a finished cargo area, wheels, or a vehicle ready for pickup. Include an exterior studio photo if customers visit, or your mobile setup if that helps explain an appointment.

Photograph before-and-after work from similar angles and in comparable light. Heavy filters, wet surfaces, and dramatic lighting changes can make the result difficult to judge. Explain the service involved so a simple wash is not mistaken for paint correction.

Get permission for customer vehicles and remove visible personal items from the frame. Avoid exposing addresses, paperwork, faces, or registration information. You can show a cleaned rear seat without showing where its owner lives.

Use a mix of work examples that reflects your real bookings. If your profile only shows spotless luxury cars, a parent with an everyday SUV may wonder whether you accept ordinary family vehicles.

Connect the profile to a useful booking page

Test the website link on your phone. It should lead to clear package information and a practical next step, such as calling or requesting a quote. A link that opens an unrelated social feed leaves the customer to assemble the details themselves.

For a quote request, collect the vehicle type, requested service, general location, and a brief condition description. Ask for photos only when they help you assess the work. Do not make someone create an account merely to ask whether you handle pet hair removal.

Explain whether the form requests an appointment or confirms one. Mobile routes, vehicle size, and condition may affect the time you need. A customer should not assume a requested afternoon is reserved before your team responds.

Maintain posts, questions, and review replies

Where posting is available, use it for concrete updates such as revised winter arrangements or a newly offered interior service. Google's post guidance describes this feature. Keep availability claims current rather than leaving an old "appointments this weekend" message untouched.

Check any customer questions visible on your profile and answer them directly. Also keep common questions on your website, since profile features can vary. Water access, drying expectations, vehicle pickup, and whether you remove child seats are useful subjects to clarify according to your own procedures.

Reply to reviews without adding private details about a customer's vehicle or habits. If someone says an odor returned, invite them to contact you so you can review the service and circumstances. Do not argue that a photo proves their complaint impossible.

The auto detailing review guide explains how to make honest feedback requests part of handover. Give one person responsibility for checking the profile regularly and after any operating change.

Frequently asked questions

Should a mobile detailer show a home address?

If customers do not visit that address for service, use the appropriate service-area arrangement under Google's guidance. Explain your actual travel coverage on the website so customers can check whether you reach them.

Should I list exact package prices?

Use your actual prices if they accurately describe the work. If condition or vehicle size changes the quote, explain that clearly instead of advertising a starting price that few of your customers can use.

Can profile photos replace a website gallery?

They can introduce your work, but your website gives you room to explain what each service involved. Keep both useful and current without assuming the customer will look at every photo.
