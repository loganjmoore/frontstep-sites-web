How to get more Google reviews as a landscaper

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Ask landscaping customers for an honest Google review after a meaningful service milestone, such as a cleanup, project walkthrough, or established mowing routine. Make the request short, optional, and easy to follow. Ask consistently, never reward reviews or filter customers by satisfaction, and respond to concerns without sharing private property details.

How do you get more Google reviews for landscaping work without bothering customers after every mow? Choose a sensible point in the relationship, ask briefly, and make the link easy to use. A one-time cleanup and an ongoing lawn care account need different timing, but both deserve an honest, pressure-free request.

Choose a milestone the customer can judge

For a spring cleanup, ask after the agreed work is finished and the customer has had a chance to see it. If the quote included debris hauling, wait until that part is complete too. Asking while bags are still waiting at the curb can create confusion about whether the job is done.

For a planting project, the walkthrough can be a useful moment to explain what was installed and discuss care information within your scope. The customer can review the service experience without being asked to predict how the garden will look years later.

Recurring mowing needs a different rhythm. Let the customer experience your normal service before asking, then choose a single appropriate moment rather than adding a request to every visit. There is no need to turn weekly lawn care into a weekly review campaign.

Do not tie the request to a satisfaction test. Use ordinary service milestones consistently, including for customers who have raised concerns. Resolving a concern is good service; making public feedback depend on a positive private response is a different practice.

Keep the request short and easy to use

Google lets businesses share a review link or QR code with customers. Use the link provided for your business and test it before adding it to your job closeout message. Google's review request guidance explains those options.

Choose a channel the customer already uses appropriately for service communication. A brief follow-up email may fit a design project. A printed card can work when you finish a cleanup in person. Avoid starting a new messaging relationship solely to chase a review.

The request should identify your business, thank the customer, and invite honest feedback. Do not make them search for your profile or complete a private rating form first. One direct route is enough.

If you use a QR code, include a plain-language label explaining where it goes. Test the printed version on a phone before producing more cards. A code that cannot be scanned is a small problem you can catch before asking customers to use it.

Adapt these scripts to the job

Use these as sample requests, not as customer testimonials. Replace the service description with the work you actually completed, and send through your normal customer communication process.

  • After a cleanup: "Thanks for having us handle the yard cleanup. If you'd like to share your experience, you can leave an honest Google review using this link. It's optional, and we appreciate your time."
  • After a planting project: "Thank you for trusting us with the planting work. If you'd like to describe your experience working with our crew, here's our Google review link."
  • For an established mowing customer: "Thanks for having us care for your lawn. If you have a moment and would like to share your experience, here's a link to review our business on Google."
  • During a walkthrough: "If you ever want to leave feedback about working with us, the card has our Google review link. There's no obligation."

Let customers choose what to discuss. They might mention communication, cleanup, scheduling, or how the crew handled a gate or garden bed. Do not tell them to use "best landscaper" or repeat a town name for search purposes.

The general review request guide has additional ways to keep the request simple. Pick a script your crew can say naturally without turning it into a sales pitch.

Keep rewards and review filtering out of the process

Do not offer a mowing discount, free mulch, prize entry, or other benefit in exchange for a review. Do not buy reviews or ask people who have not used the service to pose as customers. Google's rules prohibit incentivized reviews and selective solicitation of positive feedback. See Google's contribution policies.

Avoid a private survey that sends only happy respondents to Google. You can ask for private feedback, but the opportunity to leave a public review should not depend on the customer's answer. Keep problem reporting available to everyone.

Do not stand over someone while they write or ask to use their phone. A customer should feel free to decline or leave a mixed review. Your role is to make feedback possible, not to direct the outcome.

Give employees a consistent process instead of a target that pressures them to secure praise. If the crew is rewarded only for positive ratings, the request can become uncomfortable for both staff and customers.

Reply without exposing the property or arguing

For a positive review, thank the customer and keep the reply brief. You can acknowledge the service when the customer has already made it public, but avoid adding private details. Never publish gate codes, access arrangements, or the days a property is unattended.

For a complaint, acknowledge the concern and offer a direct route to discuss it. A sample reply is: "We're sorry to hear about the concern with the cleanup. Please contact our office so we can review the work and discuss the next step." Do not imply you checked records if you have not.

A missed mowing visit, a damaged sprinkler head, or disagreement over leaf removal needs facts and a practical response. Those details are better handled directly than debated in public. The bad review reply guide offers a straightforward approach.

Give the routine an owner

Choose who sends the request, who checks that links work, and who replies to feedback. Keep a simple note that a request was sent so the office and crew do not repeatedly ask the same customer.

Review feedback for patterns you can act on. If customers keep misunderstanding whether clippings are removed, clarify the service description and quote. If they praise reliable communication, protect that habit as the route grows.

Keep your landscaping Google Business Profile accurate so reviews appear alongside current services and contact information. A review process works best as part of good service, not as a substitute for it.

Frequently asked questions

Should I ask after every mowing visit?

No, use a sensible milestone once the customer has experienced your service. Repeated requests can turn a routine visit into an unwanted reminder.

Can I ask a customer who made a complaint?

Use the same optional review process rather than excluding them because of their feedback. Address the complaint on its merits without making a review part of the resolution.

Can I reuse a Google review on my website?

Get the needed permission and reproduce the customer's words accurately. Do not rewrite a mixed review into praise or imply it describes a different service.
