Is a $99 website worth it?

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

A $99 website can be worth it when it covers your actual business needs, has clear ongoing costs, and gives customers a useful way to understand your services. Front Step Sites charges $99 a year with the domain included and no setup fee. Check the change allowance, ownership terms, and required features before deciding that the low price is enough.

A $99 website sounds inexpensive, but what are you actually buying? The answer depends on whether that price covers the work you need and whether customers can use the result. Start by checking the offer, then judge it against a normal day in your business.

Get clear about what the price means

For Front Step Sites, $99 means $99 a year, with the domain included and no setup fee. It is an annual service price, not a one-time purchase that removes every future website responsibility.

The $99 plan includes 2 change requests a month, made within 2 business days. You ask for changes by writing in your account. New pages, blog posts, and local SEO setup are included.

The domain is registered in your own name. You can leave any time and take your domain, with no transfer fee. Keep those specific facts in mind when comparing offers that use a similar headline price but may describe a different purchase.

Do not fill gaps in an offer with assumptions. Write down the features you need and confirm that they are covered before choosing a provider.

Judge whether the site answers real customer questions

A low price has little value if the website leaves customers confused. Imagine a homeowner looking for someone to repair a garage door that will not open. They need to understand whether you handle repairs, where you work, and how to reach you. A gallery of attractive new doors does not answer all of that.

For a fence company, the useful details may be different: repair versus replacement, materials you install, and how an estimate begins. Your website should reflect the work you actually want rather than collecting every possible service phrase.

Use this basic check when reviewing a proposed site:

  • Can a stranger tell what you do without guessing?
  • Are your service area and contact details clear?
  • Do the service descriptions match the jobs you accept?
  • Can someone comfortably read and use it on a phone?
  • Does an inquiry reach the right person?

Match the change allowance to your working year

Look at the updates you expect to need, including seasonal ones. A painter may add photographs after finishing an exterior project, while a lawn care company may need to explain spring cleanups and fall leaf removal at different times.

The relevant question is whether 2 change requests a month, made within 2 business days, fit your pattern. Keep a running note of likely updates. Include changes to hours, services, service areas, photographs, and any dated offers.

If you need more frequent updates, the Grow plan costs $29 a month or $290 a year. It includes unlimited changes the same business day and Google reviews shown on the site.

Choose based on the work you expect to request. Ask how a larger set of edits would be handled before assuming it fits inside a particular allowance. Our guide to how often to update your business website can help you identify useful changes instead of changing things just to keep busy.

Know when another option fits better

A straightforward service website and a custom business tool are different purchases. If you need customers to configure a product, sign into a private account, or complete a complicated booking process, describe that process before deciding on a plan.

Do not assume those functions are included in the $99 offer. A freelancer with relevant experience may be a better fit when you need specialist development or want a detailed custom design process. Typical custom freelancer projects often cost a few thousand dollars, with more complex work costing more, but only a scoped quote can price your project.

Building it yourself may suit you if you enjoy editing pages and want to make changes directly. Account for the time you will spend writing, arranging content, and checking the result. The comparison of Front Step Sites and hiring a freelancer explains where each approach fits.

Check ownership and leaving before you join

The domain matters because customers may already know it from your truck, business cards, or email. Having it registered in your own name and being able to take it elsewhere gives you clarity about that address.

That is separate from whether a complete website can be exported. Front Step Sites' domain terms do not establish that you can take its website design, files, or software to another host. Ask about anything you would need to keep before signing up.

Keep your original photographs, logo files, and business descriptions somewhere you control. If you already have a site, make a list of important page addresses and any connected services before replacing it.

Use the same ownership questions with any provider, regardless of price. Our guide to what to ask before hiring someone to build your website covers the practical conversation.

Measure usefulness instead of assuming a return

Neither a low price nor an expensive custom build guarantees customers. A website can explain your business well and still need accurate listings, a good local reputation, and prompt replies to inquiries around it.

Decide what you will watch after launch. Keep a simple record of website inquiries, what people needed, and whether those requests matched your services. Ask callers how they found you without making the conversation awkward.

Also test the basics yourself. Send an inquiry, follow the contact links, and check your main pages on a phone. Repeat those checks when important details change.

Do not judge value by raw message count alone. Several inquiries for work outside your area can reveal unclear wording. A smaller number of appropriate requests may tell you more about whether the website is doing its intended job.

The Front Step Sites price is $99 a year, with the domain included and no setup fee. The Grow plan is separate and costs $29 a month or $290 a year. Compare the plan you actually need, rather than mixing their allowances together.

Are changes included in the $99 plan?

Yes, the plan includes 2 change requests a month, made within 2 business days. You request them by writing in your account. Ask how your particular request fits if you are planning a larger revision.

Will the website put my business first on Google?

No particular ranking, traffic level, or lead count is promised. Local SEO setup is included, but setup work does not guarantee search results. Treat anyone's ranking guarantee cautiously.

Can I keep my domain if I leave?

Yes, it is registered in your own name, and you can leave any time and take it with no transfer fee. That answers the domain question, not every question about moving the website itself. Clarify those separately if export matters to you.

The short version

A $99 website is worth considering when the included work matches your needs and the resulting pages help customers understand your business. Check update timing, required features, and what you can take if you leave. Judge value by usefulness and clear responsibilities, not by price alone.
