Comparisons

12 articles

Front Step Sites is a done-for-you website service for small businesses, priced at $99 a year with the domain included and no setup fee. The Comparisons hub is a section of the Front Step Sites blog with 12 articles of practical, specific guidance, with 2 change requests a month included on the Launch plan.

  • Front Step Sites vs. Wix

    Front Step Sites fits owners who want to request website changes instead of building and editing the site themselves. Wix fits owners who want to work directly on their website and have time to maintain it. Compare the ongoing work as well as the price, then check that your chosen option supports the specific things your business needs.

  • Front Step Sites vs. Squarespace

    Front Step Sites fits a local business owner who wants to request website changes rather than make them personally. Squarespace fits an owner who wants to build and edit their own site. Compare the price, your available time, and how often you need updates, then check that your chosen option handles your actual requirements.

  • Front Step Sites vs hiring a freelancer

    Front Step Sites fits owners who want a business website with a stated annual price and a defined process for ongoing changes. A freelancer can be the better choice when you need custom design or specialist functionality and want to agree on a detailed project scope. Compare responsibilities, future edits, and ownership as carefully as the initial price.

  • Front Step Sites vs a marketing agency

    Front Step Sites fits an owner who wants a business website with ongoing changes at a clear recurring price. A marketing agency may fit better when you need someone to plan and run advertising, coordinate larger campaigns, or handle a complex project. Compare the work each proposal includes before comparing its price.

  • Done-for-you website vs. DIY

    Build your website yourself if you want direct editing control and can make time to learn, write, publish, and maintain it. Choose a done-for-you service if you would rather supply business details and review someone else's work. Compare the ongoing responsibilities, change process, and ownership terms, not just the starting price.

  • Website subscription vs. one-time build

    A website subscription can spread the work and cost into an ongoing service, while a one-time build pays for an agreed project. Neither label tells you the full cost. Compare domain renewal, hosting, future changes, and support, then confirm who handles each task and what you can take if you leave.

  • Is a $99 website worth it?

    A $99 website can be worth it when it covers your actual business needs, has clear ongoing costs, and gives customers a useful way to understand your services. Front Step Sites charges $99 a year with the domain included and no setup fee. Check the change allowance, ownership terms, and required features before deciding that the low price is enough.

  • What to ask before you hire a web builder

    The best web builder depends on whether you want to run the site yourself. A builder like Squarespace or Wix suits an owner who will do the work, and Front Step Sites, a done-for-you service at $99 a year with the domain included, suits an owner who wants the site built and kept running. Before hiring anyone, ask who controls the domain, exactly what the price covers, how future changes work, and what happens if you leave, and get the answers in writing.

  • Leaving a web designer: keep your domain

    Before leaving a web designer, confirm who controls your domain, what your agreement lets you take, and which services depend on the current provider. Secure business-owned accounts, request usable copies or exports of permitted material, and prepare the replacement site before cancelling. Domain transfer rights do not automatically mean your entire website can move unchanged.

  • Website contracts: red flags to avoid

    The biggest website contract red flags are unclear domain control, vague deliverables, charges you cannot predict, and an exit process the provider will not explain. Ask for those details in writing before paying. A setup fee or subscription is not automatically a bad deal, but you should understand what you receive, what keeps costing money, and what happens when you leave.

  • Google Sites alternatives for a business

    Google Sites is free, but it can't set a custom meta description on each page, so Google writes the search snippet for you. Wix Light ($204 a year) and Squarespace Basic (about $228 a year) are the usual paid alternatives, and you do all the editing. Front Step Sites is $99 a year with the domain included, and you ask for changes in your account.

  • Can you run a business website on Canva?

    Yes for a one-page brochure, no for most of what a local business needs from a site. Canva Websites builds one page per site, so a business with a home page, service pages, an about page, and a contact page ends up linking several separate sites together. You buy the domain separately, and the blogging and SEO tools are limited.
