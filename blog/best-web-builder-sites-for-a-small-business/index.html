Best web builder sites, ranked by small-business fit

  1. Wix: Best for owners who want the most freedom to arrange pages themselves.
  2. Squarespace: Best for owners who prefer structured, polished templates.
  3. Google Sites: Best for publishing a few simple pages quickly.
  4. Done-for-you site: Best alternative when you want the finished site and future edits handled for you.

The ranking favors self-serve builders because that is what this list compares. If your priority is spending less time in an editor, move the done-for-you option to the top.

Compare the options side by side

Start with who will make the edits. A builder puts that work on you: writing the text, uploading photos, and checking the page on a phone after every change. A done-for-you site moves most of that work to the provider, and you supply the facts and approve the result.

Which website builder do most websites use?

According to W3Techs, which tracks the software behind websites, WordPress powers 40.2% of all websites as of October 4, 2026. Shopify has 5.4%, Wix has 4.2%, and Squarespace has 2.4%. Those numbers measure popularity. They say little about fit. A platform with a big share can still be a poor match for a plumber who needs a service area page and a button that calls the shop.

What does Squarespace cost in the first year?

Squarespace's pricing page says the annual plan includes one year of free domain registration on available domains. That makes the first year easy to price. Paying annually comes to $228. Paying month by month comes to $300. The $72 difference is what you pay for the monthly option.

The free domain covers year one only. Check the renewal price before year two, because it is not part of the $228. A small business that plans to stay three years should count that renewal before comparing the totals.

When does a done-for-you site make more sense?

A done-for-you site makes more sense when edits pile up faster than you can make them. Front Step Sites is a done-for-you website service for small businesses. Three checks before you pay

  1. Is the domain included in the price, and what does it cost to renew?
  2. Who makes the edits, and how long does each one take to go live?
  3. What do you keep when you leave: your domain, your files, or both?

Frequently asked questions

Is Wix or Squarespace the better builder for a small business?

Neither is better for every business. Pick the one whose editor you can use without help in an afternoon. Build one test page with your services, a photo, and your phone number in each builder before you pay for either.

Do I need a builder if I already have a Google Site?

Not right away. A Google Site can carry a basic brochure site. Google says only the owner of a site can connect a custom domain to it, so confirm that you are the owner before you try. If the site has grown beyond a few pages, compare it with a builder before you move customers to a new address.

Is the domain included in a builder plan?

Check before you pay. Squarespace includes one year of domain registration on plans bought for an annual term, according to its pricing page. For any builder, read the terms for renewal, because the domain may cost extra after the first year.

How often will I need to edit my site?

Count your real updates for one month before you choose. Include new photos, changed hours, new services, and seasonal pages. If that comes to more than a couple of hours a month, a done-for-you service is worth comparing. No platform guarantees a ranking, so keep your service pages accurate and current whichever route you take.
