Wix Website Creation: DIY, Freelancer, or Managed?

Wix website creation has three practical routes: build it yourself, hire someone to build a Wix site you maintain, or choose a managed website service that handles the build and later edits. The right route depends less on templates and more on who will write, check, publish, and update the site after launch.

By Logan Moore · Updated October 9, 2026

Short answer

Choose DIY Wix website creation when you want direct editor control and will maintain the pages yourself. Hire a Wix freelancer when you want a custom initial build but can agree on handoff and future-edit terms. Choose a managed website service when you prefer to send business information and change requests while a provider handles the website work.

What are the three website creation routes?

The three website creation routes divide the work in different ways: DIY gives the owner the editor, a freelancer completes an agreed project, and a managed service retains ongoing website responsibilities. All three still need accurate information and timely approvals from the business.

When does DIY Wix website creation fit?

DIY Wix website creation fits when the owner wants hands-on control and can reserve time for copy, layout, domain setup, testing, and later changes. Wix combines an editor and hosting, so the owner can work without writing the underlying code.

Wix's pricing page listed the Light plan at $17 per month on a yearly subscription when checked on October 9, 2026, with prices and currency varying by location and the yearly amount paid in full. That works out to $204 for the listed year before tax. Wix also says its plans include hosting and that select yearly plans include a one-year domain voucher; confirm the exact checkout and renewal terms for your location.

The subscription buys the tool and included services. You still write the service boundaries, choose truthful claims, collect approved photos, and notice when holiday hours change. DIY is a strong match when that control sounds useful.

When does hiring a Wix freelancer fit?

Hiring a Wix freelancer fits when the business wants a specialist to turn its information into a finished Wix site while preserving a familiar editor for handoff. The agreement should state the page scope, revision process, content responsibilities, account ownership, launch checks, training, and cost of work after launch.

Ask whether the freelancer builds in an account owned by the business or transfers the site at completion. Wix Help Center documents roles and permissions for inviting collaborators, which lets an owner provide access without sharing the main password. Use the smallest role that lets the person do the agreed work.

A project quote needs an upkeep answer. If the owner will handle every later edit, include training and a tested handoff. If the freelancer will remain available, get the response time and pricing method in writing. A low build quote can become awkward when neither side expected six months of small requests.

When does a managed website service fit?

A managed website service fits when the business wants to provide facts and approvals while someone else handles page construction, publishing, technical upkeep, and routine edits. Direct drag-and-drop control is usually lower because requests replace editor work.

Front Step Sites is one managed option. Its Launch plan is $99 a year with the domain included and no setup fee, and it includes two change requests a month with completion within two business days. Those terms make sense for an owner who prefers describing a change in plain English over rearranging the page personally at midnight.

Check the limits in any managed plan. Ask what counts as a request, who owns the domain, how quickly changes happen, what services are excluded, and what you keep if you leave. The domain ownership guide shows how to verify control instead of relying on a verbal promise.

How do the three routes compare on owner workload?

The three website creation routes create different kinds of owner work even when the finished page count is identical. DIY turns most website tasks into owner tasks, a freelancer concentrates owner effort around the brief and review, and a managed service uses short approvals and requests over time.

Consider a 90-day example for a five-page service site. During that period, the business needs an opening-hours correction, one new project photo, a service-description change, a staff update, and a new financing note. That is five changes.

Which route gives the most control?

DIY Wix gives the most immediate control over visual edits, while a business-controlled account and domain provide the important ownership control in any route. A freelancer or managed provider can still work under permissions and written terms instead of owning the business identity.

Control also has an operational side. The ability to change a heading instantly matters only if someone will notice the need and make the change correctly. A managed route trades some editor access for a request process. A freelancer arrangement sits between them and depends heavily on the contract.

Choose the kind of control you will use. Then document account recovery, domain renewal, collaborator access, files, content rights, and the departure process.

How do you choose among DIY, freelancer, and managed?

Choose among DIY, freelancer, and managed website creation by testing your preferred weekly behavior. If opening the editor and making a careful change sounds normal, try DIY Wix. If you want a defined launch project and can own the site afterward, price a freelancer. If you want to send requests and review results, compare managed services.

Ask each option the same five questions:

  1. Who writes and verifies the first version?
  2. Who owns the domain and main account?
  3. Who makes the five example changes above?
  4. What is the complete first-year payment?
  5. What can the business take away if the arrangement ends?

Written answers make the tradeoff visible. A template demo or sales call cannot substitute for them.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to create a Wix website yourself?

DIY Wix may require the lowest direct labor payment because the owner supplies the labor. Compare the current Wix checkout, extra tools, domain renewal, and your editing time with quotes that include build and maintenance work.

Can I hire someone to create a Wix website in my account?

Yes. Wix supports collaborator roles and permissions. Keep the main account and recovery details under business control, invite the specialist with an appropriate role, and document what happens at handoff.

Is a managed website the same as Wix?

No. Wix is a self-serve website platform that can also be used by hired professionals. A managed website is a service arrangement in which a provider handles specified website work and may use a different platform.

Which route is best for a small local business?

DIY is best for an owner who wants editor control, a freelancer fits a defined build with a clear handoff, and managed service fits an owner who wants to delegate ongoing website work. The business should compare ownership, updates, full cost, and exit terms before choosing.
