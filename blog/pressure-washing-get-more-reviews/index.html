Pressure washing: get more Google reviews

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Ask for an honest review after the pressure washing job is complete and the customer has had a fair chance to inspect the result. Use a direct link in your normal follow-up, without rewards or a requested rating. Keep the same invitation available to all customers, and handle surface or damage concerns separately.

When should you ask a pressure washing customer for a Google review? Wait until the work and handoff are complete, with a fair chance to see the result. A dripping driveway, a locked gate, or a customer who has not returned home can make an immediate request feel premature.

Start with a clear walkthrough and handoff

Review the agreed work before you ask for feedback. Point out the cleaned areas, anything excluded from the scope, and any remaining marks already discussed. Do not use a review request to rush past questions about an oil stain or a patch of discoloration.

If the customer is present, offer a walkthrough when conditions allow. If they are away, send the job update you agreed to provide and explain how they can raise questions. Do not assume photos substitute for an opportunity to inspect the property.

The handoff should also cover practical matters from the job: gates closed, equipment removed, and access restored. Give any relevant aftercare instructions based on the actual service and products used. Avoid blanket promises about when every surface will be ready for use.

This makes the review invitation a normal final step. It should not feel like the condition for getting your crew to finish tidying up or return an item moved during cleaning.

Choose timing that suits the surface and customer

A house-wash customer may want to walk around the siding. Someone who booked driveway cleaning may want to see the surface after it dries. A commercial property manager may need to hear from the person who inspected the entrance.

Choose a consistent follow-up process that allows for those situations. You might include the invitation in the completed-job message or in a later follow-up after inspection. Do not claim there is one perfect hour that produces reviews for every business.

For recurring commercial work, pick a reasonable milestone, such as the first completed service cycle. Repeatedly asking the same facilities contact after every visit can make your messages easy to ignore. Check whether they have already reviewed the business before sending another invitation.

If you need to make the profile itself clearer before sharing the link, use the pressure washing profile guide. Confirm the customer will land on the correct business, not an old or duplicate listing.

Use a short message that leaves the words to them

An example for a homeowner is: "Thanks for choosing [business name] for your driveway cleaning. If you would like to share your experience, you can leave an honest Google review here: [review link]. Please contact us if you have any questions about the completed work."

For a commercial contact, adapt the job reference: "Thank you for arranging the entrance cleaning at your property. If you would like to review your experience working with us, here is the link." Keep tenant names and property details out of messages that may be forwarded.

Do not supply a paragraph for the customer to copy. Avoid instructions to mention a town, say a stain disappeared, or praise a particular method. The review should reflect what they actually observed.

A direct link removes unnecessary searching. Google's review request guidance describes links and QR codes. Test yours on a phone before adding it to invoices, completion emails, or printed care cards.

Avoid rewards, filtering, and repeated pressure

Keep discounts, referral rewards, and review requests separate. Do not offer a free patio clean, money off the next visit, or entry into a drawing in exchange for a review. Buying reviews or asking friends who were not customers defeats the purpose.

Do not send satisfied customers to Google while routing dissatisfied customers only to a private survey. Google's review policies prohibit incentives and selective solicitation of positive reviews. Offer the same honest invitation after comparable completed jobs.

A private feedback option can still help customers contact you. It should sit alongside the review option, not act as a test someone must pass before seeing the link. A low satisfaction score should trigger attention to the service issue, not removal from your review process.

Use communication channels the customer has agreed to use. Consider a single reminder if appropriate, then stop. Good work does not create an obligation for the customer to promote your business.

Respond carefully to stains and damage concerns

Pressure washing reviews can involve claims about landscaping, window spotting, siding, or damage to a surface. A public reply is not the place to decide what happened. Acknowledge the concern and arrange to review the job privately.

For example: "We're sorry you have a concern about the completed work. Please contact our office so we can review the details and arrange the next step." That leaves room to investigate without blaming the customer or accepting an unverified account as fact.

Use your own job records, scope notes, and photographs during that conversation. Do not publish a customer's address, private messages, or images of their property in an argument. Keep any remedy separate from whether they update the review.

The bad-review response guide can help you write a calm public answer. If several reviews mention unclear expectations about stains, improve your estimate conversation and service descriptions as well.

Give one person responsibility for the routine

Decide who sends the request and who checks replies. If the crew leader and office both message the customer, the business can look disorganized. Record that an invitation was sent so you can avoid duplicates.

Review a small set of recent completed jobs to see whether the process is consistent. Include routine driveway work, larger house washes, and commercial visits. Do not evaluate the process only by counting five-star ratings.

Look for useful patterns in the feedback. Customers may mention clear arrival messages, careful work around planters, or confusion about what the quote included. Share those observations with the crew and improve the underlying service.

Keep related information on your website current too. The pressure washing website essentials help you explain services and estimate expectations before a customer books, reducing preventable surprises later.

Frequently asked questions

Should I ask while the concrete is still wet?

Avoid rushing the customer to judge a result they cannot yet assess fairly. Complete the handoff and let them inspect when appropriate before expecting a review.

Can I give a discount for a review with any rating?

No, the reward still makes it an incentivized review. Run any ordinary promotion independently of whether someone leaves feedback.

What if the customer was not home?

Send the agreed completion update and a way to raise questions. Let them inspect the work before following up with an optional review invitation.

Should I ask a customer to remove a complaint after a revisit?

Resolve the concern without making removal a condition. The customer can decide whether to update their review after the service issue is addressed.
