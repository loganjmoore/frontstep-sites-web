How do you set up a Google Business Profile for a moving company?

Search your business name on Google Maps before creating a new profile. A past dispatcher, previous owner, or old directory listing may have already generated one. Claim the correct profile instead of creating a duplicate, and keep ownership under an account the business controls.

Use the real business name, not a version padded with search phrases. Choose Moving company as the primary category when it accurately describes the main work. Add another category only when it represents a service customers can actually book. If customers do not visit an eligible staffed office, use the service-area setup and hide the address.

What service area should a moving company list?

Google does not let a service-area business draw a radius around a pin. According to Google's own service-area guidance, build coverage from up to 20 cities, postal codes, or other named areas. The overall boundary should stay within about two hours of driving time from the business base, although Google notes that some businesses may have a larger appropriate area.

List the towns and zip codes you can genuinely staff a crew and truck for on short notice, not every town within range on a straight line. A mover who lists 20 towns across three counties but only ever answers quickly within a tighter radius generates calls they can't service well, and a customer who waited days for a callback from a "nearby" mover will say so in a review.

How should a moving company verify applicable credentials?

First determine which federal or state credentials apply to the operation. Interstate household-goods movers must have applicable federal registration, according to the FMCSA. An in-state operation is not automatically exempt because FMCSA says some states require intrastate carriers to obtain a USDOT number. Check the responsible state agency as well.

Do not treat the Google profile description as a universal credential field or claim that every mover must put a USDOT number there. Instead, make sure the legal business name and any applicable credential match the appropriate official public lookup, then present verified credential information consistently on the website and quote path. Never invent a number or imply an authorization the company does not hold.

What hours actually matter for a moving company profile?

Use Google's business-hours guidance to decide whether hours apply to your business: it lists exceptions for appointment-only businesses and certain other types, including transportation services. Where hours apply, show your actual customer-facing booking availability. Include weekends only if your office or dispatcher really answers then; voicemail until Monday is not weekend availability.

Accurate hours reduce the chance that a customer expects an immediate response when nobody is available. Keep the website and quote-route wording consistent with the public profile, and update the information when dispatcher or office availability changes.

What belongs on the profile beyond the basics?

Upload current photos of the company's trucks, crew, and equipment rather than stock imagery of an unmarked van. Obtain permission, check every frame for customer addresses and belongings, and avoid presenting a photo as work the company did when it did not.

List only real moving and storage services. Explain important estimate or quote distinctions on the website and quote route where there is enough room for accurate terms. Reply to reviews without disclosing a customer's address, inventory, access instructions, price dispute, or other private move details.

Frequently asked questions

Can I set a radius instead of listing cities for my moving company?

No. Google removed the radius option for service-area businesses, so you need to list specific cities, postal codes, or similar areas instead, up to 20 of them.

Do I need a USDOT number on my profile if I only move people locally?

It depends on your state. Some states require intrastate carriers to obtain a USDOT number even when their vehicle and cargo never cross state lines. Check with your state Department of Transportation or responsible state agency rather than assuming a local operation is exempt.

Should I set special hours for weekends?

Not for a recurring schedule. A regular weekend schedule belongs in regular hours, when hours apply to your business. Include it only if you actually serve customers or handle bookings then. Special hours are for applicable holidays or exceptional dates; check Google's guidance for businesses that should not provide hours.

What happens if my moving company profile gets suspended?

Suspensions are usually tied to service-area or duplicate-listing issues. Review Google's guidelines, fix whatever triggered the flag, and request reinstatement through the business profile help center rather than creating a new listing.

Do I need separate photos for local and long-distance moves?

It helps if you offer both, since the trucks, crew size, and logistics often look different between a same-day local move and a multi-day interstate one.

The short version
