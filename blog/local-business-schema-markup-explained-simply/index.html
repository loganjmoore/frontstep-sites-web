Local business schema markup, explained simply

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Local business schema markup is a set of labels in your website that helps search engines understand your business details. It can describe your name, phone number, location, and opening hours. Start with accurate information customers can also read on the page, then have your website provider add and test the matching markup.

Someone told you that your website needs schema markup, but what are you actually buying? Think of it as a labeled business information card that a search engine can read. It helps describe your business, but it cannot make up for an incorrect phone number or a page that never explains your services.

Know what the labels are for

A customer recognizes that the number beside “Call us” is your phone number. Structured data makes that relationship explicit for software. Schema.org supplies a shared vocabulary, including a LocalBusiness type and more specific business types. You can see the available descriptions in the Schema.org LocalBusiness reference.

For an auto repair shop, the important facts might be its shop address, customer phone line, and weekday opening hours. For a plumber, the difference between normal office hours and emergency call availability needs particular care. The labels should describe how the business actually operates.

Your Google Business Profile and your website's structured data are separate things to maintain. Do not assume changing hours in one place updates the other. Make a short list of every place your hours appear before a holiday closure, including the website footer and appointment page.

Prepare a clean business information sheet

Before asking someone to add markup, give them facts you have checked. This takes less technical knowledge than reviewing code and prevents avoidable errors.

  • Your normal public business name, without extra search phrases.
  • Your main website address and the phone number customers should call.
  • A public business address, when appropriate, with the correct suite and ZIP code.
  • Regular opening hours and any different seasonal schedule.
  • The areas you actually serve and the services you actually provide.
  • Links to profiles that belong to your business.

Schema.org includes a way to describe the area served. That is useful context, but listing a town does not create an office there. A garage door company that travels to neighboring towns should not invent storefront addresses for those towns.

Use this same information sheet when checking your business name, address, and phone across listings. Resolve an old phone number before giving it to a developer. Copying inconsistent details into another location only adds another correction later.

Match the markup to the page and your privacy choices

Ask your provider to use a business type that accurately describes your work. Schema.org includes types such as Plumber and Electrician. A general category can be appropriate when a narrower one does not fit; accuracy matters more than filling every possible field.

Google requires a name and physical address for its LocalBusiness rich result eligibility. That requirement can be awkward for an owner who operates from home and keeps the street address private. A service area is not a substitute for that required address. See Google's LocalBusiness requirements.

Do not put a private home address into markup on the assumption that customers cannot find it. Markup sent with a public page is public information. Tell your provider that privacy comes first and ask them to explain the resulting eligibility limits.

The marked information should also agree with what readers can see. Google says structured data must represent the page and must not describe hidden, misleading content. Keep that rule in mind if someone offers to add claims that never appear on your site. Google's structured data policies explain the requirement.

Give your provider a small, specific job

You do not need to learn programming to request this work. Send the information sheet and ask your provider to check whether your website already produces LocalBusiness markup. A website builder, theme, or existing tool may already be doing it.

A useful request is: “Please check the business details already in our structured data, correct anything outdated, and show me the test results for the published page.” This is clearer than buying an undefined “schema package.”

Ask these questions before approving the work:

  1. Which page contains the business information?
  2. Where will future changes to hours or phone numbers be made?
  3. Does another website feature already add the same information?
  4. How will you confirm that the published details match my information sheet?

For a business with several real locations, check each location individually. A tire shop's second branch may have different Saturday hours and its own appointment number. One shared set of details would send some customers to the wrong place.

Test the live page, then check the facts yourself

Use the Schema Markup Validator to inspect the structured data on a page. The Google Rich Results Test checks supported Google search features. They answer related questions, so their results do not have to look identical.

Paste the public page address into each tool or ask your provider to walk you through the results. Check the detected business name and phone number yourself. A test can accept a correctly formatted number that still reaches your old office.

Keep a short record of the page checked, the date, and any issues the provider needs to fix. Ask them to explain warnings rather than dismissing them or inventing data to remove them. A field that does not apply to your business should not be filled with a guess.

You can also use Google Search Console to inspect your website. Testing a saved draft is useful, but the published page is what matters. Recheck after a redesign or a change of website provider.

Keep expectations realistic and maintain the details

Google does not guarantee a special search appearance even when markup passes its tests. Treat the work as accurate labeling, not a promise of first place or a particular number of calls. That distinction also matters when considering structured data for AI search.

Be careful with promises of review stars. Google excludes self-serving LocalBusiness review snippets, including reviews about your own business shown through an embedded third-party widget. Its review snippet rules explain this limitation.

Put markup on your regular website update checklist. When your shop stops opening on Saturdays, update the visible hours, relevant listings, and structured data together. The best handoff is a clear answer about who maintains those facts after launch.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need schema markup before launching my website?

You can launch a useful website without it. Make sure customers can find your services, service area, phone number, and contact form first, then add accurate structured data as part of the website setup.

Can I copy another business's markup?

Do not paste another company's markup into your site. It can include their address, profile links, ratings, and identifiers, even when you remember to replace the visible business name.

Will schema markup put my business first on Google?

There is no placement guarantee. A passing test confirms aspects of the markup, not that Google will feature your business or send customers.

How often should I check it?

Check it whenever the underlying business details change or the site is rebuilt. Add it to your existing review of opening hours and contact information so it does not become a forgotten task.

The short version

Local business schema labels facts that should already be clear on your website. Prepare accurate details, respect private addresses, and test the published page. Keep those labels current whenever the business changes.
