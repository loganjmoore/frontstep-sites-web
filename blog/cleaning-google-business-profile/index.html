Cleaning: Google Business Profile guide

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Your cleaning company Google Business Profile should explain which homes or workplaces you clean, where you travel, and how a customer requests an estimate. Use accurate business details, list services you actually provide, and add photos with permission. Keep hours, website information, and replies current so customers know what to expect.

A homeowner searches for "move out cleaning near me," while an office manager needs cleaners after the staff leaves. Does your Google Business Profile make clear whether you handle either job? Set it up around the work you actually want, then maintain it as carefully as your cleaning schedule.

Establish the correct business and location

Search for your company before creating a profile so you can deal with an existing listing rather than make an unnecessary duplicate. Keep ownership under an account the business controls and complete the verification Google requests. Do not rely on a former employee's personal access as your only way in.

Use your real business name, not a string of cleaning keywords. Keep your phone number and website consistent with the details customers find on invoices and your van. Google's business representation guidelines explain the underlying requirements.

If your team travels to customers and does not receive them at your base, use the appropriate service-area setup and hide the address from public view. A home office where you store supplies is not automatically a customer-facing storefront. Read the Google Maps setup guide if this is your first listing.

Match categories and services to your cleaning work

Choose the available primary category that best describes your main business. A company focused on recurring house cleaning should not present itself mainly as a specialist carpet business unless that is truly its work. Add other relevant categories only when they describe services you actually provide.

Then describe the cleaning services in plain language. A useful working list might include:

  • Recurring home cleaning, with the tasks your normal visit covers.
  • First-time or deep cleaning, with an explanation of the added scope.
  • Move-in and move-out cleaning for empty properties.
  • Office cleaning, including the types of premises you accept.
  • Specific extras, such as an oven interior, if your team offers them.

Do not list every task as though it were included in every visit. Inside cabinets, high windows, carpet extraction, and post-construction dust may need a different quote or fall outside your work entirely. That distinction belongs on your cleaning website as well as in your customer conversations.

If your profile offers service descriptions, use them to explain the customer situation and scope. Keep descriptions factual instead of filling them with repeated city names.

Make your coverage and hours believable

List places your team can realistically reach. A recurring route that works well for nearby homes may not support a distant one-time clean. Explain any meaningful coverage differences on your website, where there is room for detail.

Think through the questions customers actually ask: Do you serve the apartment buildings downtown? Can you clean a small office in the next town? Are rural properties outside your usual route considered individually? Give honest answers rather than advertising everywhere within driving distance.

Keep published hours accurate and explain scheduling on the website. If you operate only by appointment, check Google's current hours guidance for that setup instead of inventing walk-in opening times. If office cleaning happens after normal business hours but estimates are handled during the day, make that difference clear. Do not suggest a person answers the phone all night just because a crew works evenings.

Update holiday availability and temporary closures when your schedule changes. An owner searching during a move needs to know whether a request will be handled, not simply that your company exists.

Choose photos that show work without exposing customers

Photograph your team, equipment, and finished spaces with permission. A tidy supply setup or a clearly cleaned kitchen can show how you work without revealing a customer's private life. Use your own images rather than presenting stock rooms as completed jobs.

Check every frame for addresses, family photos, papers, alarm panels, and screens. Office images may reveal staff information or customer records. A room that looks anonymous to you may still be recognizable to the person who works there.

Before-and-after photos should explain the service, not embarrass the client. Ask permission for the actual intended use. If you cannot obtain it, photograph your tools, your team in an appropriate setting, or a space you control.

Captions can describe the task, such as cleaning empty cabinets as part of an agreed move-out service. Avoid guarantees about deposit refunds or claims about health outcomes. Those promises are not needed to show careful cleaning work.

Keep updates, questions, and reviews useful

Where profile posts are available, share concrete updates: a change in service coverage, holiday scheduling, or what is included in your move-out clean. Do not publish an offer you cannot honor or leave an expired seasonal message as your only recent update.

Review any public questions and answers visible on your listing. Correct misunderstandings where you can, but do not depend on a particular question feature being available. Put enduring answers on your own website: whether you bring supplies, how estimates work, and what customers should prepare.

Reply to reviews without disclosing private arrangements. Thank a reviewer for feedback on communication or the cleaning visit, but do not add their address, access instructions, or the fact that their home was vacant. If a complaint concerns a missed task, acknowledge it and move the job details to a private conversation.

Use the cleaning review guide to build a consistent request process. Check the listing regularly for changed details, broken website links, and unanswered inquiries. Keep a brief record of corrections so another team member can take over when you are busy.

Frequently asked questions

Can I display my home address?

The setup should reflect whether customers are actually received at a qualifying business location. If you travel to homes and offices and do not receive customers at your base, use the service-area arrangement. Check Google's current guidance before presenting a residential address as a storefront.

Should house and office cleaning have separate profiles?

Different services alone are not a reason to create duplicate profiles for the same business. Describe your services within the appropriate listing and explain them on separate website pages if useful. Check Google's eligibility guidance if you genuinely operate distinct businesses or locations.

What should I post when I have no customer photos?

Use appropriate photos of your own team, supplies, or equipment, and write useful service updates. You can explain how to prepare for a move-out clean without showing a customer's home. Never treat access to a property as permission to publish it.

Will adding more towns guarantee more calls?

No, listing additional areas is not a promise of visibility or inquiries. Choose coverage that reflects your actual operations. Accurate information helps people judge whether you can serve them, which is the purpose of the profile.
