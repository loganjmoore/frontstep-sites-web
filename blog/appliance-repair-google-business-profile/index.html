Appliance Repair Google Business Profile

Updated October 2, 2026

Short answer

An appliance repair Google Business Profile needs four fields set correctly: a service area built from cities or zip codes, a services list naming the actual appliance types and brands you fix, honest same-day or emergency availability, and hours that match when a tech really answers. Get those right and someone with a dead fridge can tell in seconds whether you're a fit.

An appliance repair Google Business Profile needs four fields set correctly to turn a search into a call: a service area built from cities or zip codes instead of a radius, a services list that names the actual appliance types and brands you fix, honest same-day or emergency availability, and hours that match when someone really answers the phone. Most appliance repair profiles get the category right and stop there, which leaves a customer guessing whether you touch their brand of dryer or just show up tomorrow when their fridge is leaking today.

What service area should an appliance repair business list?

Your service area should be a list of the specific cities or zip codes you can realistically reach, not a wide circle drawn around your shop. Google removed the option to set a plain radius years ago. According to Google's own service-area documentation, you build coverage from up to 20 cities, zip codes, or similarly named areas, and the whole area shouldn't stretch much more than about two hours of driving time from where your business is based.

That rule plays out differently for an appliance repair truck than it does for a trade that drives long distances between jobs. A locksmith might cover 100 highway miles in two hours. An appliance tech working mostly in-town stops, with parking, unloading a dolly, and city traffic between calls, realistically covers closer to 30 miles an hour on average. Two hours of that pace works out to roughly 60 miles of actual reach from your base, not 100. List the towns inside that real driving pattern, in the direction you actually run routes, rather than every zip code that falls within 60 miles on a straight line.

A customer whose dishwasher is flooding the kitchen floor doesn't want a tech who's two counties away and "on the way eventually." List the towns you can hit the same day, and let the rest go to a referral.

Which appliance brands and types belong in the services section?

The services section should list the individual appliance types you repair, each as its own entry, not a single line that says "appliance repair." Break it out: refrigerator repair, washer repair, dryer repair, dishwasher repair, oven and range repair, and microwave repair if you handle it, each with a short description of what that covers. A customer searching "Whirlpool fridge not cooling" is more likely to click a profile that visibly lists refrigerator repair than one that just has a category name.

Name the brands you actually service, somewhere in your business description or within the service descriptions themselves, such as Whirlpool, GE, Samsung, LG, and Maytag. Don't list a brand you can't get parts for quickly; a customer who books you expecting same-day Sub-Zero service and gets told parts are three weeks out will leave the exact review you don't want showing up when the next person searches your name.

Keep each service description within Google's working limits. Service names top out around 58 characters and descriptions around 300, according to thestacc.com's 2026 Google Business Profile services guide. That's roughly two sentences, so lead with the appliance type and brand scope, then the one detail that matters most, like "same-day diagnosis available."

How do you show same-day or emergency availability honestly?

Same-day availability belongs in two places: your special hours and your business description, and it should only say what's actually true on a normal week. If you run one truck and same-day service depends on how the schedule looks that morning, say "same-day service when available" rather than promising it outright. If you genuinely hold a slot open for urgent calls, like a flooding washer or a fridge that stopped cooling overnight, say that specifically instead of a generic "fast service" line.

Google doesn't have a dedicated "emergency repair" toggle for appliance businesses the way it does for some other categories, so this detail lives in your description and your posts. Use a post when you're intentionally holding extra same-day capacity, for example during a heat wave when refrigerator calls spike, and remove it once that's no longer true. A profile with a six-month-old "extra techs standing by this week" post reads as abandoned, not busy.

What hours actually matter for an appliance repair profile?

Set your hours to when a real person answers the phone and can schedule a visit, not when your trucks happen to be on the road. If calls after 5pm go to voicemail until morning, your hours should reflect that, even if a tech is technically still finishing a job at 6:30. A customer deciding between you and the next listed business is often checking "Open now" on their phone standing in front of a broken appliance, and a profile that says open but doesn't pick up costs you more trust than one that's honestly closed.

Review hours after anything changes: a new tech added, a schedule that shifted, a holiday closure. The appliance repair website mistakes guide covers the matching mistake on the website side, where outdated hours or a missing service list cost calls the same way a wrong Google profile does.

How does this compare to other trades on Google Business Profile?

Appliance repair shares the service-area and category basics with most home service trades, but the brand-and-type breakdown is specific to this trade. A plumber doesn't need to list manufacturer names; an appliance tech does, because the search itself often includes the brand. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a separate Google Business Profile for each appliance type I repair?

No. Use one profile and list each appliance type as its own entry in the services section. Separate profiles for the same business violate Google's guidelines and risk suspension of all of them.

Should I list every appliance brand that exists, just in case?

No. List only the brands you can realistically service and get parts for quickly. A long brand list that doesn't match your actual turnaround time creates the exact bad review you're trying to avoid.

What if I only do refrigerator and washer repair, not full-service appliance work?

Say so clearly in your category and services section instead of choosing a general "appliance repair" category that overstates your scope. A specific profile that matches what you do converts better than a broad one that disappoints half the people who call.

Can I set my service area as a simple radius around my shop?

No. Google requires a list of specific cities, zip codes, or similarly named areas for service-area businesses, up to 20 of them, instead of a radius.

How often should I update the services section?

Check it whenever your brand list, parts availability, or appliance types change, and at least once a season. An outdated services section is one of the quieter ways a profile stops matching real searches.
