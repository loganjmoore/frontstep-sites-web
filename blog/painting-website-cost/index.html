How much a website costs for a painting contractor in 2026

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

A painting contractor website can be a modest yearly expense or a larger custom project, depending on who builds it and what it needs to do. Budget for service descriptions, useful project photos, estimate requests, and ongoing updates first. Compare the full recurring cost and ownership terms before paying for custom features.

How much should you spend on a website for your painting business? Start with the work you want to book, not the number of design effects in a proposal. A residential repaint crew needs clear project examples and an easy estimate request; a company bidding occupied commercial properties may need a different set of pages.

Price the website around the painting work you sell

Write down your actual services before requesting quotes. Interior repainting, exterior painting, cabinet refinishing, and deck staining involve different customer questions. A useful site helps a visitor recognize the right service without calling to ask whether you do that work.

For interior jobs, customers may want to know about furniture, wall repairs, and whether the home can stay occupied. Exterior prospects may ask about siding condition, access, and scheduling around weather. Cabinet customers need to understand your process and whether doors leave the home.

Your initial website brief might include a homepage, service pages, a project gallery, information about the crew, and an estimate form. Page count alone does not tell you how much work is involved. Writing honest service descriptions and sorting real job photographs can take more effort than arranging a page.

Use the painting website essentials to decide what deserves space before you compare proposals.

Understand the DIY cost and your part of the job

With a website builder, you pay for the platform and do the setup yourself. You choose the layout, write the text, prepare photographs, connect the domain, and test the result. That can suit an owner who enjoys this work and has time between jobs.

As of September 2026, Wix Light is $17 a month billed yearly, or $204 a year, and Squarespace Basic is $19 a month. These are subscription reference prices, not a complete quote for your project. Confirm your intended setup directly before buying, without assuming everything you need comes with the advertised amount.

The real question is who will finish the site. A half-built gallery waiting for captions does not help a homeowner choose between you and another painter. Count the time to select photos, explain preparation, and check the estimate form.

For a solo painter with simple needs, that work may be manageable. If your evenings already disappear into estimates and ordering materials, compare the DIY and done-for-you approaches before choosing by subscription price alone.

Compare done-for-you, freelancer, and agency scopes

A done-for-you service can make sense when you need a straightforward website and prefer to send changes to someone else. Check the exact update allowance, delivery terms, domain arrangement, and recurring bill. A lower starting cost means little if routine edits are unclear.

A typical custom freelancer or agency project often costs a few thousand dollars, with more extensive work costing more. Treat that as a broad planning range, not a quote. Ask each provider what their proposal actually covers.

Custom help may fit a painting company that needs a substantial photo shoot, separate commercial bid resources, or a carefully planned connection to existing office software. Ask for those tasks by name. Do not assume a more expensive site automatically includes better copy, maintenance, or advertising.

Spend first on proof and a useful estimate request

Your project gallery should explain the work, not simply display pretty rooms. A caption could identify an occupied-home repaint, describe patching included in the scope, and note which surfaces were finished. Use only accurate details and photos you have permission to publish.

Ask a builder to make before-and-after photos easy to view on a phone. Similar angles help customers judge a result. Heavy image files and complicated sliders can make the gallery harder to use, especially when someone opens it from a yard sign.

The estimate form should collect information you will actually use:

  • The project town and a reliable contact method.
  • Whether the work is interior, exterior, cabinets, or another offered service.
  • A short description of the rooms or surfaces.
  • The customer's preferred timing.
  • Optional photos, if your process can receive them reliably.

Do not buy an instant price calculator just because it sounds convenient. If preparation, access, and surface condition require a visit, explain that. A clear estimate process is more useful than a precise-looking number you will immediately revise.

Separate launch costs from ownership and upkeep

Request a written first-year total and a recurring total. Ask what happens when you add a new service, replace job photos, change your phone number, or need help with a broken form. Confirm whether charges are recurring, optional, or tied to individual requests.

Also ask who controls the domain and who receives renewal notices. Your truck graphics, estimate sheets, and customer emails may all depend on that address. Read the domain ownership guide before handing responsibility to a new provider.

Keep a copy of your original photographs and approved website text. Ask how leaving works and what you can take with you. Domain control and website portability are separate questions, so get a clear answer to both.

Build routine updates into the decision. A new cabinet refinishing service should not remain absent simply because nobody remembers how to edit the website. Choose an arrangement your business can maintain during its busy season.

Compare proposals using the same painting project brief

Send each provider the same service list, towns, sample jobs, and contact requirements. Ask them to explain what you must supply and what they will create. That gives you a fairer comparison than collecting prices for vaguely defined websites.

Before accepting delivery, open the site on your phone. Find cabinet painting, identify the service area, and submit a test estimate request. Confirm that the message reaches the right inbox and includes the information you entered.

Check claims as carefully as buttons. Do not publish insurance, warranty, or preparation promises that your business has not approved. The finished website should describe the job you can deliver, not the biggest promise someone can fit into a headline.

Frequently asked questions

Is a one-page painting website enough?

It can work for a narrow service offering in a clearly defined area. Separate pages become useful when customers need different explanations for interior work, exterior preparation, and cabinet finishing.

Should I pay for a custom quote calculator?

Only if it matches how you actually price work and you can maintain it. For site-dependent jobs, a short request form followed by an assessment may be more useful.

Do I need professional photos before launching?

You need clear, accurate photos with permission to use them. Well-lit phone pictures can explain real work, and you can replace weaker images as you document more projects.

Does a larger website budget guarantee more leads?

No website price guarantees rankings, traffic, or jobs. Judge the proposal by its usefulness to customers, the accuracy of its content, and the support you will receive.
