Appliance Repair: what your website needs

Updated October 2, 2026

Short answer

An appliance repair website needs a clear list of the appliance types and brands you actually service, an honest statement of same-day or next-day availability, and a visible service area, since customers search by brand and symptom and want to know fast whether you can help today. Add real photos and reviews, then a simple quote-request form.

Someone's refrigerator stopped cooling an hour ago and everything in it is at risk. They're searching on a phone, standing in the kitchen, trying to figure out who fixes their brand and can come today. What does your website need to answer in those first few seconds?

Name the appliance types and brands you actually fix

"We repair all major appliances" tells a worried customer nothing about whether you've worked on their specific machine. List the appliance types you service:

  • Refrigerators and freezers.
  • Washers and dryers.
  • Dishwashers.
  • Ranges, ovens, and cooktops.

If you specialize in certain brands, or have real gaps, say so plainly. A customer who learns in thirty seconds that you don't service their brand can search elsewhere immediately instead of calling and finding out the hard way. Appliance repair customers often search by brand and symptom, something like a specific model that won't spin or cool, so a site that only has one generic repair page is invisible to that more specific, higher-intent search.

Say honestly whether you can come today

A broken refrigerator or a flooding washer usually feels like an emergency to the person calling, even if it's routine work for you. If you offer same-day or next-day service, say so on the homepage in plain language, not buried on an about page. If same-day isn't realistic for every job, explain what typically qualifies, for example refrigerators often jump the queue while a dryer that still mostly works usually doesn't need to.

Give a realistic picture of availability by appliance type rather than an unqualified "we're always available" claim that falls apart the first time someone calls on a Saturday.

Make your service area obvious

A homepage that says "serving the greater metro area" leaves someone in a specific suburb guessing. List the cities, towns, or zip codes you actually dispatch technicians to, and if coverage has real limits, like no same-day service more than 45 minutes from your shop, say that directly instead of letting someone wait on a callback that doesn't come. Keep this list matching whatever you've set in your Google Business Profile so the two don't contradict each other.

Show evidence that your shop is real

A page with no reviews, no real photos, and no sense of how long you've been in business reads as a risk to someone letting a stranger work on an expensive machine in their home. Show real reviews, even a handful, and real photos: a technician actually working, a marked service vehicle, or your shop if you have one. Skip stock photography of a generic repair person in a uniform that doesn't match your own.

A short, true line about how long you've been repairing appliances in the area does more than a generic "trusted by homeowners" claim with nothing behind it.

Give the repair-versus-replace decision a fair shot

Plenty of visitors are quietly deciding between calling you and just buying a new appliance. Your site can tip that decision toward repair with a rough price range, a note about how often repairs actually fix the problem, and clear same-day availability. Leaving pricing and availability vague pushes people toward the easier-feeling choice of just replacing the thing.

Give every visitor a simple way to request service

Not everyone calling has an active emergency; some are planning ahead for a repair that can wait a day or two. Offer a short form next to the phone number asking for name, contact details, appliance type and brand, and a short description of the problem, with photos optional.

Before calling the site done, check it like a customer would:

  1. Open it on your phone over cellular data, not wifi.
  2. Confirm your town or zip shows up as covered.
  3. Tap the phone number and check it dials correctly.
  4. Send a test request and confirm it reaches you.
  5. Read the confirmation message and make sure it explains what happens next.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a separate page for every appliance type I repair?

Not necessarily a full page each, but the appliance types and brands you service need to be named somewhere a visitor will actually see, usually the homepage or a dedicated services section, rather than left implied by a photo gallery.

Should I list my prices?

A rough range or starting service-call fee helps more than hiding pricing entirely, since customers already have an expectation in mind before they call. You don't need an exact price for every possible repair.

Should I put same-day availability on my homepage?

Only if it's true for your actual scheduling. State it specifically, for example same-day service in your core area, rather than a vague promise you can't back up on a busy day.

What should I fix first on an existing appliance repair site?

Whether the brands and appliance types you service are clearly named, and whether your service area and availability are honest and specific. Fix those before adding more pages or photos.

The short version

Build your appliance repair website around what a worried customer with a specific broken machine actually needs: do you fix this brand, do you cover my area, and can you come today. Be honest about availability, show real evidence the business is legitimate, and test the phone and request form yourself before sending anyone else to the site.
