How much a website costs for an auto detailer in 2026

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

An auto detailing website can start at $99 a year with a done-for-you service, while a typical custom freelancer or agency project can run from a few thousand dollars to much more. Your biggest cost decisions are whether customers request quotes or book appointments, how packages are explained, and who keeps the site current.

You want a website for your detailing business, but you need to know what you are actually paying for. A mobile detailer offering interior cleanups needs something different from a shop selling paint correction and ceramic coatings. Start with the customer's buying decision, then compare prices for the same work.

Price the website around your detailing services

A useful detailing site explains what happens to the car, what affects the quote, and how the owner takes the next step. It should distinguish an exterior maintenance wash from decontamination, polishing, or a full interior detail. Otherwise, you spend your evenings explaining why a quick wash price does not cover a neglected family SUV.

Write down your actual services before requesting proposals. Include package descriptions, vehicle size differences, mobile service towns, shop directions if applicable, and contact information. Add original photos that show the kinds of vehicles you regularly handle.

Your first version can be straightforward. A homepage, clear service information, a small gallery, and a quote request may cover the job. Use the auto detailing website guide to decide what deserves space before paying for extras.

Compare DIY, done-for-you, and custom work

As of September 2026, Wix Light costs $17/month billed yearly, or $204/year, and Squarespace Basic costs $19/month. Those are starting points for comparing website spending, not assurances that a particular booking or payment setup is included. Check the current quote for every feature you intend to use.

DIY fits when you want direct control and have time to write package descriptions, arrange images, and check the site on your phone. Your subscription does not do those jobs for you. Count the evenings you will spend maintaining the site alongside the money you pay.

Typical freelancer and agency projects can range from a few thousand dollars to substantially more as custom design, content, and connections to other systems expand. Booking choices can change the scope

An appointment request is not the same as a confirmed booking. For a mobile detailer, travel time, access to the vehicle, weather, and the condition of the interior can all change the schedule. An open calendar slot alone may not mean the job fits.

A simple request form could ask for:

  • Vehicle make, model, and approximate condition.
  • The desired service, such as pet hair removal or exterior detailing.
  • The customer's town and preferred contact method.
  • Whether the work would happen at home, work, or your shop.

You can review that information and confirm the price and appointment yourself. A system that confirms jobs automatically needs more thought about service durations, buffers, deposits, cancellations, and exceptional cases. Ask who configures and tests those rules.

If you already use a booking service, ask whether a clear link will solve the problem. Do not commission a replacement just to make everything appear on one screen. Explain on the site whether submitting information requests a quote or reserves an appointment.

Spend on package clarity and believable photos

Package names such as Silver and Platinum mean little until you list the work. Say whether a package includes seat extraction, door jambs, trunk cleaning, or paint polishing. Explain when a vehicle inspection is needed and how heavy soil or excessive pet hair affects the estimate.

A ceramic coating page needs different content from an interior cleanup page. Customers may need to understand preparation, vehicle drop-off, care instructions, and the difference between coating and correcting existing defects. Use your actual process without promising that every scratch will disappear.

Budget for sorting and preparing photos, even if you take them yourself. Consistent before-and-after angles help a customer judge the work. Avoid a gallery that consists entirely of exotic cars if your regular customers drive pickups and family crossovers.

When comparing proposals, check whether copywriting and image preparation are included or whether you must supply finished material. The common detailing website mistakes can help you spot gaps in a cheap-looking scope.

Ask what happens after launch

A site becomes expensive when ordinary updates turn into surprises. You might adjust a package, stop serving a distant town, or change your rain policy. Ask how changes are requested, who makes them, and how the bill is calculated.

Get written answers about the domain, hosting, renewals, and access to your content. Ask what happens if you switch providers. Ownership of your web address matters when it is printed on your van, invoices, and appointment cards; the domain ownership guide explains the questions to ask.

Before accepting the finished site, use your phone to request an interior detail for a heavily used SUV. Check that the form reaches you and that the customer sees what happens next. Then try the phone button and booking link. This checks whether you bought a working inquiry path, not simply a nice gallery.

Compare the first year and a normal following year separately. A proposal should make recurring costs and optional services understandable without a sales call.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need online payments at launch?

You may not need them if you inspect vehicles before confirming a price and already have a way to collect payment. Start with a quote or appointment request if that matches your process. Add payment steps when you can explain exactly how deposits, balances, and cancellations should work.

Should every detailing package have a separate page?

Separate pages help when the customer questions are meaningfully different, such as paint correction versus interior cleaning. Closely related maintenance packages can share a clear comparison in ordinary text. Avoid buying pages that repeat the same description with a different package name.

Is a custom site worth several thousand dollars?

It can be when you need original branding, extensive content, or carefully configured booking workflows. Ask the provider to connect each part of the proposal to a real business need. A higher price alone does not establish that the site will generate bookings.
