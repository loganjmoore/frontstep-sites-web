Local SEO for cleaning companies: the short version

Local SEO for a cleaning company starts with accurate business details, clearly described services, and honest local coverage. Check your Google profile, website, and other listings for contradictions. Then improve pages for recurring, deep, move-out, or office cleaning, make inquiries easy, and ask customers for feedback without exposing private household or workplace details.

What should you check first if your cleaning company is hard to find in local searches? Start with whether people can tell what you clean and where you work. This checklist puts the basic business facts ahead of adding more pages or chasing search tricks.

Check your business details and Google profile

Write down the current version of your business name, phone number, website, hours, and coverage area. Compare that information with your Google Business Profile and the contact details on your site. Fix old numbers, outdated hours, and links that lead to an unfinished page.

Use your real business name rather than adding phrases such as "best house cleaners near me." Choose categories that describe the business you actually operate. Follow Google's representation guidelines when deciding how to display an address for a business that travels to customers.

A cleaning company run from home should not invite customers to an address where it does not receive them. Keep the public explanation focused on how to request service and which places you cover.

For a more detailed review of the listing itself, use the cleaning business profile guide.

Match service pages to the cleaning request

Check whether your site explains the services people can actually buy. "Cleaning services" is a broad label. A renter searching for "move-out cleaning" needs different information from an office manager looking for evening janitorial work.

For each main service, answer these questions:

  • What rooms, surfaces, or tasks are included?
  • What is excluded or priced separately?
  • What condition or access information affects the estimate?
  • Which locations can you serve?
  • How does the customer request the next step?

Make recurring home cleaning distinct from an initial deep clean. Explain whether a move-out property needs to be empty and whether inside appliances are part of the scope. On office pages, discuss walkthroughs, work schedules, and who provides consumable supplies if that is relevant.

Give each page a descriptive title and heading. Write for the person comparing services instead of repeating the same search phrase in every paragraph.

Review local coverage and other listings

Read your service area page against your actual routes. Remove towns you no longer serve and clarify any scheduling limitations. If a distant area is available only for larger jobs, explain how customers can check before requesting an estimate.

Separate town pages are useful when they contain meaningful local information. For example, you might explain actual route availability or appointment arrangements for buildings you regularly serve. Do not imply that you have an office in each town or invent local experience.

Use the service area page guide before producing pages that differ only by a place name.

Check the other business listings where customers already encounter you, including relevant directories and map services. Correct outdated business facts. A directory entry should help someone reach the right company, not just add another link to a spreadsheet.

Check the technical basics without making them a project

Open important pages on your phone using a normal mobile connection. Can you read the scope, tap the phone number, and finish the estimate form? Large photographs, awkward menus, and forms that require constant zooming deserve attention before decorative changes.

Ask whoever maintains the site to confirm that important pages are available to search engines and not accidentally blocked. If you use Google Search Console, review whether your main service pages have indexing problems. A submitted page is not a promise that it will appear for every search.

Business structured data is another item for the maintainer to check. It should describe the same company details visitors can read, without invented offices or ratings. Treat it as accurate supporting information rather than a ranking shortcut.

The plain-English schema guide explains what to ask for without requiring you to write code.

Refresh evidence without exposing private spaces

Review the photos on your site and business listings. Do they show the kind of work you currently do? If your commercial work has grown, an entire gallery of residential kitchens may give office managers the wrong impression.

Publish customer property photos only with permission. Inspect images for mail, staff schedules, alarm panels, family portraits, or other details that should remain private. Never use an untidy home as a joke or turn a difficult cleaning situation into a spectacle.

Build review requests into your normal follow-up after a completed service. Invite honest feedback without asking for a particular rating or providing a ready-made review. Do not ask only customers who first passed a satisfaction screen.

Reply politely, and move disputes about access, billing, or property condition into a private conversation. Public replies should not reveal details about a customer's home or workplace to prove a point.

Check whether searches become suitable work

Keep a small record of inquiries and outcomes. Track the requested cleaning type, general service area, and whether you arranged an estimate or booking. This can show whether your pages attract work you actually want.

A company seeking recurring housekeeping might receive mostly last-minute move-out requests. That is a reason to review its page emphasis and availability language. A commercial cleaner might find that office managers need a clearer explanation of the walkthrough process.

Do not judge results only by searching your own name from your own phone. Google describes local results as dependent on relevance, distance, and prominence in its local ranking guidance. Your position can differ between searches.

Repeat the practical checks when your routes, phone number, services, or hours change. Maintain a short list of problems and fix the ones preventing real inquiries first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I rank in a town where I have no office?

You can truthfully explain that you serve the town, but no one can promise a particular ranking there. Do not invent a business address to appear local. Publish useful coverage information and let customers check whether their property fits your route.

Should every cleaning service have its own page?

Separate pages make sense when the service has distinct scope and customer questions. Deep cleaning and office cleaning usually need different explanations. Avoid creating thin pages for every individual task, such as wiping one type of surface.

How often should I ask for reviews?

Make an honest request part of a sensible customer follow-up, without repeatedly chasing the same person. Give customers a simple way to respond and let them decide what to say. Keep requesting feedback separate from resolving a complaint or offering compensation.
