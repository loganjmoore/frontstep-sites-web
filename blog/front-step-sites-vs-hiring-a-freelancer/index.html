Front Step Sites vs hiring a freelancer

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Front Step Sites fits owners who want a business website with a stated annual price and a defined process for ongoing changes. A freelancer can be the better choice when you need custom design or specialist functionality and want to agree on a detailed project scope. Compare responsibilities, future edits, and ownership as carefully as the initial price.

Front Step Sites is a done-for-you website service for small businesses, priced at $99 a year with the domain included, while a freelancer is an independent contractor you hire for a custom, scoped project. Should you pay a freelancer to build your website, or use Front Step Sites? Both can make sense, but they are not interchangeable purchases. The useful comparison is what your business needs built, who will keep it accurate, and what happens when those needs change.

Start with the job your website must do

Picture the next person who visits your site. A homeowner with a blocked drain wants to know whether you handle the problem, cover their address, and answer the phone. A commercial property manager comparing electrical contractors may need details about maintenance work, project experience, and how to request a site visit.

Write down those needs before choosing a provider. For a straightforward business, useful service pages and a clear contact path may cover the main work. Another business may need a customer login, a parts catalog, or a booking process tied to existing software.

Do not assume that either choice includes specialist features because the word "website" appears in the offer. Describe the exact process and ask whether it is supported. Our guide to questions to ask before hiring a website provider gives you a starting checklist.

Compare a defined subscription with a scoped project

Front Step Sites costs $99 a year, with the domain included and no setup fee. That is the stated price for the plan, so the comparison begins with an ongoing service rather than an upfront custom build.

Freelancers set their own prices and agreements. As a wide, typical planning range, custom projects often cost a few thousand dollars, with more complex work costing more. An individual quote may differ substantially because the work, experience, and responsibilities differ.

Ask a freelancer whether the proposal covers copy, photographs, development, revisions, launch, and later maintenance. Ask which renewals you will pay separately and which accounts will be yours to manage.

Compare what you expect to pay during the first year and what you expect to pay afterward. Also account for your own time: gathering service details, answering questions, and reviewing drafts are real tasks whichever route you choose. Our subscription versus one-time build guide explains that distinction further.

Decide how you want everyday changes handled

The first website is only part of the purchase. A pressure washing business may add gutter cleaning, change its coverage area, or need to remove a seasonal offer. Decide who will handle those edits before you sign up.

The Front Step Sites $99 plan includes 2 change requests a month, made within 2 business days. Customers ask for changes by writing in their account. New pages, blog posts, and local SEO setup are included.

The Grow plan costs $29 a month or $290 a year. It includes unlimited changes the same business day and Google reviews shown on the site.

A freelancer may offer ongoing support, teach you to make edits, or finish the agreed project and hand it over. None of those arrangements is automatically wrong. Ask how requests are submitted, how they are priced, and what response time is actually agreed. Choose an arrangement that fits your normal workload, including busy seasons.

Keep domain ownership separate from website portability

Your domain is the address customers type. The website itself includes the pages, design, files, and any tools that make it work. Being able to take the address elsewhere does not by itself mean you can move the complete site unchanged.

Front Step Sites registers the domain in the customer's own name. Customers can leave any time and take their domain, with no transfer fee. Those facts concern the domain; they do not establish a promise to export the website's design, files, or software.

With a freelancer, ask who will register the domain, control the relevant accounts, and provide access when the project ends. Also ask what rights and copies you receive for custom design, text, photographs, and any purchased components.

Get answers before paying, particularly if a future move matters to you. Read who owns your domain name if the account details are unclear. Keep your original business photographs and text in a place you control.

Choose a freelancer when the project needs specialist work

A good fit for a freelancer is a business with requirements that need individual investigation and design. For example, a cabinet shop may want customers to explore finish combinations and send selected options with an inquiry. A service company may need a connection to an existing dispatch system.

Those projects need more than a general promise of a website. A suitable freelancer can assess the requirements, explain the tradeoffs, and quote for the agreed work. Look for evidence of relevant projects and ask to see how the finished features behave.

A freelancer may also suit you when you have a strong creative direction and want a close working relationship through design reviews. Be ready to make decisions and supply feedback. A higher project price can be reasonable when you are buying specialist work you will actually use.

Choose the ongoing service when its scope fits

Front Step Sites is worth considering if the stated plans and change process match what you need. An owner who wants to request changes through an account may prefer that arrangement to learning an editor or organizing separate maintenance work.

For example, a small cleaning company might need clear descriptions of recurring cleaning, move-out cleans, and the neighborhoods it serves. Its owner should focus on whether the proposed site explains those differences and makes inquiries straightforward.

Before choosing, list your expected updates and any required features. Confirm that the offer covers them. Do not choose on the annual price alone if a missing capability would force you to change providers soon afterward. The guide to whether a $99 website is worth it walks through that check.

Frequently asked questions

Is a freelancer always more expensive?

No universal comparison covers every freelancer and every project. Compare an actual written quote with the plan you would buy, including renewals and expected changes. A different scope can explain a large price difference.

Can a freelancer provide ongoing updates too?

Yes, if that is part of the arrangement you agree on. Ask whether updates are included, billed separately, or covered by a maintenance agreement. Confirm availability instead of assuming the person who built the site will always maintain it.

Does domain ownership mean I own everything on the site?

No. The domain and the website are separate, and photographs or software may have their own terms. Ask what you can take, copy, or keep using if you leave either provider.

Which option will get me more leads?

Neither choice guarantees a particular number of leads. Judge the proposed pages against your customers' questions, and check how inquiries will reach you. The quality of your follow-up matters after someone makes contact.

The short version

Choose a freelancer when you need a scoped custom project, specialist features, or a design process built around your requirements. Choose an ongoing service when its included work, change process, and costs fit your business. Get clear answers about future edits and ownership before committing.
