What to put in your website footer

Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Put your business name, main phone number, honest hours, service area or customer-facing address, and useful navigation in your website footer. Add links to relevant policies and a clear contact option. Keep it readable on a phone, and make sure its details agree with your contact page and business listings.

What should go at the bottom of your business website? Your footer should help someone who has finished reading and still needs your number, location, hours, or next step. Treat it as a useful closing set of details, not a storage space for everything that did not fit elsewhere.

Start with your name and a working phone number

Use the business name customers know from your sign, estimates, and listings. If your logo is hard to read at a small size, put the name beside it as ordinary text. A visitor should not have to enlarge an image to confirm which company they found.

Show your main customer phone number and make it tappable. Test the link on a phone and check the number it opens. A correct-looking number can still have an old destination behind the link.

Use a short description only if it adds useful context. "Residential painting in Fort Collins" helps someone who landed on a project page. "Quality, integrity, excellence" does not explain the work or location.

Keep these facts consistent with your contact page and listings. Our guide to name, address, and phone consistency explains how to find old details that can confuse customers.

Show the right location for your type of business

A shop that welcomes customers should show its actual street address, including a suite when needed. Add a directions link if it helps people find the entrance. An auto repair shop in a business park might also explain that vehicle drop-off is behind the building.

A contractor who works at customers' homes has a different need. You can name your base town and the places you serve without inviting people to your private home. Do not label a mailbox or borrowed office as a walk-in location.

Google's service area guidance says businesses that do not serve customers at their address should remove that address from their Business Profile. Keep your website's directions and visit instructions consistent with how you actually operate.

For a mobile detailer, "Serving Tempe and nearby East Valley neighborhoods by appointment" is a useful kind of statement, if accurate. For multiple customer-facing locations, link to a locations page rather than squeezing every address into tiny text. See our map guide for more detail.

Separate office hours from service availability

State when customers can expect to reach you. If the office answers calls on weekdays but crews sometimes work Saturdays, explain the difference. A form being available all night does not mean someone is reading it all night.

Use language that matches the business:

  • An appointment-only salon can say visits require an appointment.
  • A cleaning company can distinguish office hours from scheduled cleaning visits.
  • A plumber can explain whether after-hours calls reach a person or voicemail.
  • A repair shop can link to separate vehicle drop-off instructions.

Do not put "Open 24/7" in the footer just because customers can submit a form. Only advertise round-the-clock service when the business actually provides it.

Give someone responsibility for updating holiday or seasonal information. If your hours change often, a clear link to the current hours page can be easier to maintain than several conflicting schedules. Check that the linked page answers the question without another search.

Keep navigation short and useful

Choose links that help visitors finish a task. Services, service area, About, Contact, and relevant policies are a reasonable starting point. Use labels customers understand, such as "Request an estimate" or "Our service area."

Your footer does not need every article you have published. A long wall of links makes the important ones harder to find. If you have several services, link to a services overview or include a small selection of the work customers request most often.

Link to social accounts only when they belong to your business and remain useful. An abandoned page with a disconnected number can undo the clarity you just created. Remove empty icon links and test every destination.

Include a privacy policy link when your site collects information, and make the policy match what you actually do. Our privacy policy guide explains the questions to gather before getting appropriate advice. Ask a lawyer about requirements that apply to your business.

Give the visitor one sensible next step

Someone who reaches the footer may be ready to contact you. Offer a main action suited to your work instead of several equally loud buttons. For a tree service, that could be requesting an assessment; for a barbershop, it could be checking appointment availability.

Keep the wording honest. "Request an appointment" and "Book a confirmed appointment" describe different outcomes. Do not make a basic inquiry form sound like a reserved time slot.

You can include an email address if you regularly monitor it. If you prefer a form, link directly to that form and explain any important response expectations there. Avoid asking visitors to copy a long address from an image.

The footer number is a backup contact route, not a reason to hide contact details elsewhere. Put a suitable call or inquiry option near the main service information too. Our click-to-call guide covers making the phone option work throughout the site.

Read the footer on a real phone

Open your homepage, a service page, and your contact page on a phone. Scroll to the bottom of each one. The footer should be readable without zooming, and links should be far enough apart that you can tap the intended one.

Use this practical check:

  1. Tap the phone number and confirm the destination.
  2. Open directions and check they lead to the right customer entrance.
  3. Follow the main contact link and submit a test inquiry.
  4. Read the hours and location as if you knew nothing about the company.
  5. Check that policy and social links open the intended pages.

Remove decorative content that gets in the way of these tasks. Tiny gray text, a large slogan, or repeated service keywords do not help someone contact you. Keep genuine credentials current and avoid badges that imply certifications you do not hold.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to publish my home address?

Do not add your private home address merely to fill a footer template. If you travel to customers and do not receive visitors, state your service area clearly. Ask a lawyer about any separate business disclosure requirements that apply to you.

Should I put every town I serve in the footer?

Use a short, accurate coverage description and link to more detail when needed. A long list can become hard to read on a phone. Do not include towns you would normally refuse to visit just to attract searches.

Is a contact form better than a phone number?

They serve different situations, so you may want both. Someone arranging a future painting project may prefer a form, while someone checking today's repair availability may want to call. Make it clear which option is monitored and when.

Should the footer be identical on every page?

Shared core details make updates easier and reduce contradictions. Location-specific pages may need their own relevant contact details, clearly labeled. Whatever the arrangement, avoid showing different phone numbers or hours without explaining which location or service they belong to.
