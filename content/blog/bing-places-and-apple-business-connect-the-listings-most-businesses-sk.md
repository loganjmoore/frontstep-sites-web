You have dealt with your Google listing, so do Bing Places and Apple Business Connect need attention too? Yes, if they show customers your business details, those details should be right. Start with a simple check for old addresses, missing websites, and calls going to the wrong number.

## Know which service you are opening

Bing Places manages business listings for Bing search and Bing Maps. Its current entry point is [Bing Places for Business](https://www.bing.com/forbusiness/). Microsoft moved the service from bingplaces.com and described improvements to importing business information in its [Bing Places announcement](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2025/10/Introducing-the-New-Bing-Places-for-Business-Built-for-Business-Owners%2C-Powered-by-Research/).

Apple's naming has changed too. As of September 2026, the brand and location tools previously called Apple Business Connect are part of Apple Business. Apple's [platform announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/03/introducing-apple-business-a-new-all-in-one-platform-for-businesses-of-all-sizes/) explains the change. Start at [Apple Business](https://business.apple.com/) when following current instructions.

You do not need to explore every feature either platform offers. For this task, concentrate on how customers identify the business, reach you, and find an eligible physical location. A brake repair shop gains nothing from a polished profile if its directions lead to the old garage.

## Search before you create anything

Search Bing and Apple Maps for your business name and town. Also try your phone number or previous address if the business has moved. Save the listing links and note what needs correcting.

An existing entry may be accurate but unmanaged, or it may belong to a former owner. A similarly named company might be a completely different business. Check the address, phone, website, and photos before deciding that an entry is yours.

Prepare a reference sheet with:

- The business name customers recognize.
- Your main business phone and website.
- Current hours, including customer pickup or reception hours.
- The real location, if customers visit it.
- Your actual service area if you travel to customers.
- A few clear, current photos you have permission to use.

Use our [business information consistency checklist](/blog/nap-consistency-why-your-name-address-and-phone-must-match-everywhere/) if the details already disagree across your website and directories. Sorting them out first avoids importing the same mistake into another service.

## Claim and complete your Bing listing

Sign in through the official Bing Places site using an account the business can keep access to. Look for the existing business and follow the claim process offered. If no matching entry exists, follow the process for adding your business.

Bing supports importing information from Google, but review what you import. Check that you selected the right business and location. An old holiday schedule, former website address, or outdated category should not become the starting point for another public listing.

Complete the verification steps shown for your account. Do not assume the method or timing will match someone else's experience. If the process stalls, keep a record of the error and use the support route in the official service.

For a carpet cleaning company, make the description useful: carpet cleaning, upholstery cleaning, and the places you genuinely serve, if those reflect the work you do. For a repair shop, use photos that help customers identify the entrance. Specific, accurate details are more useful than repeating “best local business” in every field.

## Check Apple eligibility before adding a location

Apple distinguishes business branding from physical location listings. Its [Apple Business introduction](https://support.apple.com/guide/business/axm7909096bf/web) describes support for businesses without physical premises, while Maps place cards are associated with physical locations. An account for your company is not a promise of a public Maps location for every business model.

If you operate a customer-facing shop, search for it in Apple Business and follow the process for claiming or adding the location. Apple's [location setup guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/business/add-a-single-location-abcb98816a34/1/web/1) covers details such as the map position, category, contact information, and verification.

For a shop with a side entrance, check the pin against where customers actually arrive. A customer bringing a mower for repair should not be directed to the loading gate behind a locked fence.

If you are a mobile locksmith or a cleaner working from home, pause before entering a residential address as a visitable shop. Review Apple's current eligibility instructions or ask its support team which features fit your business. Do not invent a storefront, borrow an address, or assume Google's service-area setup transfers directly to Apple.

## Review the listing like a customer

Once a listing is published or updated, open the public version. The information visible in your management account is not the whole customer experience.

Work through these checks on a phone:

1. Confirm the business name identifies the right company.
2. Tap the phone link and confirm it reaches your business.
3. Open the website link and check that the page works.
4. Review hours against the times customers can actually get help.
5. Check the entrance and directions for a customer-facing location.
6. Read the services for anything outdated or misleading.

A garage might accept after-hours key drops without staffing the reception desk all night. Explain that distinction clearly where the platform allows, and make the website's instructions match. Do not mark the business continuously open just because a key box exists.

If your website makes calling difficult, fix that connection too. The guide to [working click-to-call links](/blog/click-to-call-making-your-phone-number-work-on-every-page/) covers the handoff from reading to calling.

## Give someone responsibility for keeping it current

Keep a record of who manages Bing and Apple, which account owns the information, and where recovery messages go. Use the platforms' access options when a provider helps, and confirm that the business retains access when that relationship ends.

Add both services to your checklist when you move, change phone systems, adjust seasonal hours, or stop offering a service. A landscaping company that stops taking irrigation repair calls should update the relevant descriptions, not just its homepage.

Ask new customers how they found you and note useful answers. Do not assume a claimed listing guarantees calls or that every customer remembers the precise route they took. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does managing Google automatically update Bing and Apple?

Do not assume it does. Bing offers a Google import process, but you should review imported details and confirm any ongoing update behavior in your account. Manage Apple information through its own service or an authorized provider.

### Is Apple Business Connect gone?

Its brand and location tools are now part of Apple Business. Older guides may still use the Business Connect name, so follow Apple's current instructions when the screens differ.

### Can a home-based service business get an Apple Maps listing?

Do not treat eligibility for an Apple Business account as automatic eligibility for a Maps place card. Check Apple's current requirements for your specific business model before publishing a home address or adding a physical location.

### Should I create a second listing if I cannot access the first?

Start with the platform's claim, ownership, or support process for the existing entry. A second listing can leave customers choosing between conflicting records without solving the access problem.

## The short version

Check Bing and Apple for your business, claim the correct existing records where eligible, and complete verification. Keep contact details and hours accurate, then test the public listing from a phone. Treat home-based service eligibility carefully instead of pretending every business has a storefront.
