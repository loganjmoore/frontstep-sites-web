Should you build your business website yourself or pay someone to handle it? A done-for-you website is a site that a service designs, builds, and maintains for you while you supply the business details. Either can work, but the right choice depends on who will finish the job and keep the information correct afterward. Start with the work your site needs to do, then decide who has the time and skills to do it.

## Write down the job before choosing the tool

A small business website needs a clear purpose. For a gutter installer, that might mean explaining repair and replacement services, showing completed work, listing the service area, and getting estimate calls. For a restaurant, it might mean keeping the menu and opening hours easy to find.

List the information and actions your customers need before looking at templates or asking for quotes. This prevents a simple information site from growing into a project nobody has time to finish.

- Which services or products need their own pages?
- What must a visitor know before contacting you?
- Will customers call, send a request, or complete another task?
- What information changes regularly?
- Does an existing booking or ordering system need to be involved?

If you need a complicated customer portal or a specific business-system connection, describe the complete task to any prospective provider. Do not assume that a basic website package or a builder includes it. Ask to see the exact process demonstrated before making it central to your decision.

## Count the work you still have to provide

Building it yourself means choosing the layout, writing the pages, preparing photos, arranging navigation, and checking the result. You also need to confirm that contact buttons and forms reach you. The tool may help, but somebody still makes the decisions.

A done-for-you service moves some of that work to another person. It does not remove your responsibility for the facts. You still need to explain what you do, where you work, which pictures you can use, and what promises the business can keep.

For example, a painter should supply the difference between interior painting, cabinet refinishing, and wallpaper removal. A provider cannot safely guess which preparation steps are included or whether the crew accepts occupied commercial jobs after hours. Your contribution is business knowledge, even when someone else writes the page.

## Choose DIY when direct control is useful to you

DIY fits an owner who enjoys editing and will use that control. You can make a wording change when it occurs to you, try a different photograph, and learn how the site is put together. That can be valuable when your business details change frequently.

A cafe owner who already prepares weekly menus may prefer handling the website as part of that routine. Someone who finds choosing layouts frustrating may not get the same benefit. Be honest about your interest, rather than assuming you will enjoy it once the subscription starts.

Try a small, representative task before committing. Create a draft service page with your own text and photographs, then look at it on your phone. Check whether you can change the hours, make the number usable, and understand how to publish an update.

If that feels manageable and you have a maintenance routine, DIY may be the better fit. If the draft sits unfinished while customer work takes priority, treat that as useful evidence about your available time, not a personal failure.

## Choose done-for-you when handing off the work helps

A done-for-you service fits an owner who can provide accurate information and review changes but does not want another editing tool to manage. The advantage is having a defined way to request work. That advantage depends on clear scope and a response schedule that suits the business.

Ask how changes are submitted and what happens after a request. A useful request names the page, states the replacement wording, includes any approved photo, and gives the date the information should apply. Keep a copy so you can compare the published result with what you asked for.

Make the agreement fit your actual schedule. A business that changes availability throughout the day needs a different arrangement from a contractor who updates project photos occasionally. Ask what happens when a request exceeds the included allowance or needs action outside normal business hours.

Delegating also requires review. Read the finished pages and test the contact routes yourself. Nobody outside your business should decide that you offer emergency attendance, service a distant town, or provide a particular guarantee without your approval.

## Check ownership and plan for the next year

Make sure you know who controls the domain account and where renewal notices go. Then ask separately about your text, photographs, website files, and any third-party tools. Keeping a domain is not the same thing as being able to move an entire working site.

Request a plain explanation of what you can take away, what might need rebuilding, and whether there are cancellation or transfer charges. If contract terms are unclear, ask a lawyer to review them. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is DIY always cheaper?

No, compare the actual recurring costs and the extras your site requires. Also consider whether the work fits your available time, even if you do not assign that time a dollar amount.

### Do I need to know how to code?

You can build a basic website using a visual editing tool without writing code. You still need to learn that tool and check the finished pages, links, and contact routes.

### Can I hire someone later to improve a DIY site?

You can ask for help later, but confirm that the person works with your chosen system. They may be able to improve the existing site, or they may recommend rebuilding part of it.

### Does done-for-you mean I can stop thinking about the website?

No, someone in your business still needs to report changed hours, services, and contact details. You should also approve factual claims and check that customer messages reach the right place.

## The short version

Choose DIY when you want editing control and can reliably maintain the site. Choose done-for-you when handing off the work fits your schedule and the change process meets your needs. Check the full cost, your responsibilities, and the exit terms before choosing either route.
