## A crowded, price-sensitive market

Appliance repair is a large but fragmented business. IBISWorld counts 37,453 appliance repair businesses in the U.S. in 2026, in an industry that generated $7.4 billion in revenue that year and grew at a compound annual rate of 2.8% from 2021 to 2026, with no single company holding more than 5% of the market. That fragmentation cuts both ways: there's room for a small local shop, but a customer searching for repair help has dozens of similar-looking options to pick from.

Cost expectations are set before the customer ever calls you. HomeAdvisor's 2026 pricing guide puts the average appliance repair around $170 per machine, with service call fees typically running $75 to $125 per visit, and labor averaging about $24 an hour according to ZipRecruiter data cited by This Old House. Your customer already has a rough number in their head. A site that hides pricing entirely, with no range at all, reads as evasive rather than premium.

## Why brand and model pages earn their keep

Appliance repair customers search differently than most trades: they search by brand and symptom, not just by service. Someone typing "Samsung refrigerator not cooling" or "LG washer won't spin" is closer to booking than someone typing a generic "appliance repair near me" search, because they've already diagnosed part of the problem themselves. If your site only has one general repair page, you're invisible to that specific, higher-intent search.

A well-built site usually includes:

- **A page per appliance type** (refrigerators, washers, dryers, dishwashers, ovens and ranges), since the failure modes and typical costs differ enough that lumping them together loses detail customers are searching for.
- **A visible list of the brands you service**, since a customer with a specific brand wants to know you've worked on it before they call, not after.
- **Honest same-day or next-day availability messaging.** BLS reports 29,950 people employed as home appliance repairers nationwide as of May 2023, with a median annual wage of $47,170, or about $22.68 an hour. That's a limited pool of technicians relative to the country's appliance count, which is exactly why a specific, honest answer about how fast you can actually get there matters more than a vague "fast service" banner.

## The repair-versus-replace decision your site can influence

A meaningful share of your potential customers are deciding between calling you and just buying a new appliance. Consumer Reports found that among people whose major appliance broke in the past five years, 58% chose to replace it and 36% chose to repair it, with 70% saying cost was the main factor in that decision. That means part of your website's job is making the case for repair before the customer defaults to replacement: a rough price range, a note about how often repairs actually solve the problem, and clear same-day availability all push that decision in your direction.

## Getting quotes without overpaying

Write your real scope before requesting prices: home page, a page per appliance type you service, your brand list, and a quote-request form. Send that same list to every freelancer or agency you talk to, since a quote for one general page and a quote for five appliance-specific pages are pricing different projects, not disagreeing on the price of the same one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate page for every appliance type I repair?

If you service more than two or three appliance types, yes. Customers search by appliance and often by brand, and a single generic page makes it harder for those specific searches to find you.

### Should I list the exact brands I service?

Yes. A customer with a specific brand wants to confirm you've worked on it before they pick up the phone, and it costs you nothing to list the brands you already service.

### How much does a custom appliance repair website cost from a freelancer?

Typical freelancer pricing for a small business site often runs a few thousand dollars, but it varies with scope and experience. Get a written page list and compare quotes against that same scope.

### Should I put same-day availability on my homepage?

Only if it's true for your actual scheduling. State it specifically, for example same-day service in your core service area, rather than a vague promise you can't back up on a busy day.
