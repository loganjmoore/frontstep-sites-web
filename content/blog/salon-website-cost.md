How much should a salon or barbershop website cost in 2026? Start with the booking decisions your clients need to make. Showing a clear haircut menu and linking to an existing calendar is a smaller job than building a system that chooses stylists, schedules consultations, and coordinates several locations.

## Define the appointments the website needs to support

List your real services before comparing providers. A barbershop may need clear information about cuts, beard work, walk-ins, and appointments. A salon may need to explain color consultations, new-client bookings, styling services, and differences between service options.

Use the questions your front desk already answers. Can a new client book a color correction directly? Does a listed color appointment include a cut or finish? Can someone choose any stylist, or do certain services require a particular team member?

A useful starting scope includes service information, staff details where relevant, location, hours, appropriate photos, and a clear booking route. Include your actual policies and consultation requirements rather than assuming the scheduling page explains everything.

The [salon website essentials](/blog/website-salon/) can help you write a brief. Send the same brief to each provider so you compare the cost of solving the same client problems.

## Compare a DIY builder with your available time

As of September 2026, Wix Light costs $17 a month billed yearly, or $204 a year. Squarespace Basic costs $19 a month as of September 2026. These are the verified subscription prices used here, not a complete quote for every salon booking arrangement.

Check what your proposed setup requires before buying. Ask about any separately quoted parts and do not assume a particular booking connection, email arrangement, or payment workflow comes with the advertised website price.

A builder can suit an owner who wants to arrange the pages and make changes. You will still need to write service descriptions, prepare staff information, choose photos, and check the mobile experience. A template cannot decide which appointment a new color client should select.

Think about maintenance during a busy week. Who removes a departing stylist's booking link or updates a changed service price? The subscription may be manageable, but the website also needs someone with time and responsibility to keep it accurate.

## Know when a custom project has a clear purpose

Typical freelancer and agency website projects can range broadly from a few thousand dollars to many thousands. Treat that as a planning range, not a quote. The actual proposal should explain the pages, content work, design, connections, and support you receive.

Custom work may make sense for several locations with different teams and menus. It may also fit a salon with a specific client intake requirement or a carefully defined connection to an existing booking system. Ask what problem the added development solves.

Compare what you must supply. One provider may expect finished writing and photographs, while another includes interviews and content preparation. A smaller quote is not necessarily incomplete, but you need to understand the work still assigned to you.

Avoid paying for custom features simply because they look impressive in a demonstration. If your current booking system already handles staff availability and deposits appropriately, a clear website link may be enough. Rebuilding the same functions adds another system to maintain.

## Separate the service menu from the booking software

Your website should help a client choose the right next step before opening the calendar. "Color" may be too broad if your team distinguishes root services, highlights, major changes, and consultations. Explain the choices in language clients understand.

When asking a provider to quote booking work, describe the real cases:

- A new client needs a consultation before a major color change.
- A returning client wants the same stylist and an appropriate service length.
- A barbershop accepts walk-ins but also offers selected appointments.
- Some team members offer services others do not.
- Different locations use different menus, staff, or booking destinations.

Ask whether the proposed solution is a link, an embedded booking page, or a custom connection. These involve different work and responsibilities. Request an explanation of what happens when the outside booking system is unavailable or its page changes.

Keep payment and sensitive intake information within the appropriate system. An ordinary website inquiry form should not become an improvised card collection or detailed health-history form. Gather only what is needed for the next conversation.

## Include upkeep and a real booking check in the comparison

Ask each provider for the initial price, recurring costs, included changes, and any separately quoted work. Confirm domain ownership, access, and what can move if you leave. Keeping your domain and moving a complete website are different questions worth asking explicitly.

Plan for photos and staff changes. Use images you have permission to publish, describe whose work they show accurately, and remove outdated team information promptly. A client should not choose a stylist from a page that has been abandoned.

Before launch, follow the booking path on a phone. Check the location, service choice, stylist options, and any consultation instructions. Stop before creating an unwanted appointment, or arrange a controlled check with your team if confirming the full process is necessary.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a new booking system with a new website?

Not if your current system works for your clients and team. Ask the website provider to explain how it will connect clients to that existing process before pricing a replacement.

### Should each stylist have a separate page?

Use individual pages when they help clients understand relevant specialties, services, or booking choices. A concise team page may be enough when the distinctions do not require more explanation.

### Can a barbershop start without online booking?

Yes, if the site clearly explains how walk-ins or phone appointments work. Keep hours, location, service information, and the next step accurate so clients can plan their visit.
