How much should you spend on a website for your house or office cleaning business? Start by deciding whether you need a clear quote request or a system that sells and schedules cleaning automatically. Those are different projects, even if both proposals call the main button "Book now."

## Price the cleaning jobs you want to attract

List the work you want customers to request: recurring house cleaning, first-visit deep cleaning, move-out work, or office cleaning after business hours. Each needs enough explanation for someone to decide whether your crew fits the job.

A homeowner may need to know whether you bring supplies and clean inside ovens. An office manager may want a walkthrough, a written scope, and information about access outside working hours. Putting both visitors into one unexplained form can create extra phone calls rather than save time.

For a straightforward launch, ask for service pages, an honest service area, contact information, a quote request, and relevant photos. Include your actual exclusions, such as laundry or exterior windows, where customers would otherwise assume they are covered.

The [cleaning website essentials](/blog/website-cleaning/) can help you turn that list into a written brief. Give the same brief to every provider so their estimates describe comparable work.

## Compare builder subscriptions with the work you supply

As of September 2026, Wix Light is $17 a month billed yearly, or $204 a year. Squarespace Basic is $19 a month as of September 2026. Those are the verified price references for this comparison; check the complete proposed setup before deciding what you will spend.

A builder can fit an owner who wants to write the pages, arrange the photos, and maintain the site. The subscription does not remove those jobs. You still need to explain the difference between a maintenance visit and a first clean in words your customers understand.

Ask what your planned booking or payment workflow requires instead of assuming it comes with the quoted plan. Compare the total arrangement, including any separately quoted services, without treating the lowest advertised figure as your finished website budget.

Be honest about your available time. If you spend evenings organizing keys, supplies, and tomorrow's route, a site you must finish yourself may keep slipping down the list. DIY is useful when you can actually maintain it.

## Know when custom work is worth considering

Typical freelancer and agency project costs range broadly from a few thousand dollars to many thousands, depending on the work. This is a planning range, not a quote. Ask for a written scope that explains what your business receives and what remains your responsibility.

A custom project may make sense if you need separate customer journeys for residential accounts and commercial contracts. It may also fit an established operation with several branches, different coverage areas, or a specific connection to existing business software.

Ask whether the proposal includes writing, photography, revisions, mobile checks, and support after launch. A lower quote that requires finished copy from you may still be the right choice, but only if you have time to provide it.

Do not buy custom development just to show a service checklist or collect a few quote details. Ask the provider to explain what cannot be handled with a simpler page and why that limitation matters to your customers.

## Separate a quote form from automated scheduling

A quote form can ask for a town, property type, approximate size, cleaning frequency, and the work requested. You review the request and confirm scope, price, and availability. That is a manageable starting point for businesses whose jobs vary.

Automatic booking has more decisions to handle. Consider how the system would deal with:

- A first deep clean that takes longer than a recurring visit.
- An empty apartment versus an occupied home with belongings throughout.
- Travel between appointments and the crew size needed for the work.
- Optional tasks such as oven interiors or inside cabinets.
- An office that needs a walkthrough before you can price the contract.

Write out these cases before asking for an integration. Decide what happens when a customer enters incomplete information or chooses the wrong service. A booking tool that creates unsuitable appointments can add work for your office.

Keep keys, alarm codes, and detailed access instructions out of an ordinary initial quote form. Arrange the necessary handover after you have established the job.

## Compare the cost of keeping the site useful

Ask each provider for initial costs, recurring costs, included changes, and the process for adding pages. Confirm who owns the domain, who can access the website, and what can be moved if you leave. Domain ownership and website portability are separate questions.

Check how the provider handles real updates: a new office-cleaning service, revised holiday availability, or a changed contact person. Ask for clear terms rather than relying on "support included" without an explanation.

Before launch, submit a test inquiry from a phone and confirm it reaches the person responsible for estimates. Read the confirmation message: it should say whether the customer has requested a quote or reserved a visit. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need instant cleaning quotes on the website?

Not if you normally assess condition, access, or scope before quoting. A clear request form can explain what you need and let you confirm a suitable price and appointment.

### Should house and office cleaning have different pages?

Usually they benefit from separate explanations because customers are buying different arrangements. You can still keep them on one website and route each inquiry to the right person.

### Is a custom website always better for growth?

No, the useful question is whether it solves an actual operating or customer problem. Clear service information and a working inquiry process can remain useful as your business adds crews.
