Use this checklist as an audit, not a universal licensing script. Requirements depend on the operation and jurisdiction: interstate movers, brokers, and intrastate movers can face different federal, state, and local rules. Confirm current credentials with FMCSA and the applicable state or local regulator before publishing them.

| Business identity and profile | Real-world business name, staffed location or service-area setup, customer-facing phone, hours, categories, and profile ownership | The profile and website show the same current facts, and each profile represents a real eligible location | Correct the source facts first, then update the profile, website, and active listings |

## Verify the business identity and Google profile

Start with facts a customer can check. Google's current Business Profile guidance says the business name should match the name used in the real world, the address or service area should be accurate, and categories should be limited to the fewest specific choices that describe the core business. Do not add services, towns, or hours as keywords in the business name.

If customers do not visit the business address, use a service-area profile and remove the address from public display. A service-area business normally uses one profile for its central office or location. A business with separate real locations, separate staff, and separate service areas can be eligible for a profile at each location; serving another city by itself does not create another eligible location.

Google currently requires a service area to use cities, postal codes, or other named areas rather than a radius. It allows up to 20 service areas and says the overall boundary generally should not extend beyond about two hours of driving time from the business, although a larger area can be appropriate for some operations. List only coverage the company can actually serve.

## Verify the mover or broker credentials that apply

First identify what the business does. A mover transports the household goods; a broker arranges transportation by a mover. For interstate household-goods moves, the [FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/movers-vs-brokers) says movers must have a USDOT number and be registered with the agency, while interstate brokers must also be registered. FMCSA operating authority can also apply depending on the activity, so do not treat a USDOT number and an MC number as interchangeable.

FMCSA's household-goods consumer rules apply to interstate moves. An intrastate carrier may still need a USDOT number or other credentials under state rules, and local licensing can add another layer. Check the current federal record and the responsible state or local agency. Publish only the role and identifiers that match those records; a broker should not present itself as the company that will transport the shipment.

## Separate services and describe real coverage

Give each moving service customers can actually book a clear destination. Local moves, interstate moves, packing, storage, labor-only work, and commercial moves should not appear as available unless the company has confirmed that scope. A focused service page should explain what is included, what the customer needs to provide, and how to request an estimate.

Create a location or service-area page only when the business has distinct services, access rules, scheduling, or other verified operating details to explain there. A regional coverage section is better when several towns share the same service and process. Do not produce a set of pages that changes only the city or ZIP code.

## Test the mobile quote path

Use a phone on mobile data and start from the page a searcher is most likely to land on. Tap the phone number, open the quote form, complete every required field, submit a test request, and confirm that it reaches the monitored inbox or dispatch system. Check that the confirmation tells the customer what happens next without promising a response time the team cannot meet.

Repeat the test for the main services and a representative coverage page. If calls go to voicemail or a form is reviewed only during set hours, state that accurately instead of implying immediate dispatch.

## Use a neutral review process

Choose a repeatable milestone after a completed move when the customer has enough experience to comment. The right timing depends on the operation, the communication channel, and any applicable consent rules, so a universal one-day or two-day schedule is not appropriate.

Offer the same neutral invitation to comparable eligible customers. Do not ask only customers who signal that they are happy, request a particular star rating, provide review language, or offer an incentive. Google's review policy prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews and other rating manipulation. Keep service recovery separate from the public review invitation.

Track whether the invitation was sent so the process can be checked without using the star rating as a staff target. A single optional follow-up may fit the business's normal communication policy, but it should not pressure the customer.

## Check the technical basics

- Open the site on a phone using mobile data and confirm the main pages load and remain readable.
- Confirm the sitemap includes every service and coverage page that should be indexed.
- Click through the navigation and quote paths looking for broken links to retired services or areas.
- If the site uses business schema, check that it matches current public facts and does not invent a rating, review count, credential, or service.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is local SEO for movers different from local SEO for other trades?

The regulatory trust check is unusually important. Interstate movers and brokers have FMCSA registration requirements, while intrastate and local requirements depend on the state and operation. Customers should be able to match the business's stated role and identifiers to the current applicable records.

### Should my moving company have a Google Business Profile for each city it serves?

No, not just because the company serves another city. A service-area business normally has one profile for its central office or location. A separate profile can be eligible for each real location only when it has separate staff and a separate service area under Google's current guidance.

### When should a moving company create a coverage page?

Create one when the business can explain a real difference in services, access, scheduling, or the moving process for that area. Use one regional coverage section when the same service and process apply across several nearby towns.

### How should a moving company request reviews?

After an eligible completed job, send an optional, neutral invitation that does not screen customers by satisfaction or ask for a particular rating. Use the same rule for comparable jobs and keep any service-recovery conversation separate.

### How often should I revisit this checklist?

Recheck it whenever the company's role, credentials, services, coverage, phone, hours, or quote path changes. A periodic audit can catch an expired credential, old listing, or broken request route before a customer does.
