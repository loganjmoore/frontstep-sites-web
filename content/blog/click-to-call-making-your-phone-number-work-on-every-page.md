Can a customer tap the phone number on every page of your website and reach your business? A number that is visible is not necessarily a working phone link. Check both the placement and what actually happens after the tap, especially on the service pages customers visit directly.

## Turn the number into a real telephone link

A phone link uses a destination beginning with **tel:** rather than a normal web address. On a supported device, activating it offers a way to call the number. The exact behavior depends on the device and installed calling apps, as described in [MDN's telephone link documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/a#linking_to_telephone_numbers).

You do not need to write HTML to request this change. In your website editor, select the phone text or call button and look for a phone-link option. If someone manages the site for you, ask them to make the visible number a telephone link and confirm the actual destination.

Keep the displayed number readable with familiar spacing or hyphens. The link destination should use your complete number, including the appropriate country code. For a US business, that means the +1 country code followed by the area code and local number.

Check for a mismatch between the label and the destination. It is possible to replace the number a customer sees while leaving an old number inside the link. A visual inspection alone will miss that mistake.

## Put calling options at the decision points

Use a consistent phone link in the site header so visitors can find it without learning a new layout on each page. Include it in the footer too, where people may look after reading. Do not rely on the contact page as the only place a call can start.

Add a relevant call option near the end of service explanations. Someone reading about a water-heater replacement or a garage door that will not close should not have to return to the home page to continue. A short label such as "Call about a repair" can explain the purpose.

Show the actual number alongside or within the call control. Desktop visitors may want to dial from a separate phone, and some customers may prefer to copy the number. A tiny telephone icon alone gives them less information.

If you use a fixed call bar on mobile, check that it does not cover prices, form controls, or other important content. The [phones-first website guide](/blog/why-your-small-business-website-should-be-built-for-phones-first/) explains how to review the whole page around that action.

## Make the link clear and comfortable to use

Give the phone link enough space that a thumb can select it without accidentally opening a neighboring menu. Make its text readable against the background. Avoid placing it inside a busy photograph where the number disappears into the image.

Use wording that matches what happens. "Call the office" should start a call route, while "Request an estimate" might open a form. Do not label a button as an immediate call if it first opens a sales popup or asks for an account.

Keep the link available to keyboard users and people using assistive technology. A real link with descriptive text is a sound starting point, and it should have a visible focus state when selected by keyboard. Ask your website provider to check this if you do not manage the page structure.

Avoid making every contact option compete equally. A restaurant may need reservations, a salon may need booking, and a repair business may prioritize calls. The [website accessibility basics guide](/blog/website-accessibility-basics-for-small-businesses/) covers practical checks that help people use these options.

## Explain who answers and when

A working button does not mean someone is available at all times. Put honest contact hours near important call options, especially where the page uses words such as urgent or emergency. Explain what customers can expect outside those hours.

If calls go to voicemail while you are on a job, make the greeting useful. Identify the business and ask for the details you need to return the call. Do not promise an immediate callback unless you have a process that supports it.

Provide a separate alternative for someone who cannot call. A short inquiry form may suit a customer at work or a person planning a future project. The [contact form guide](/blog/contact-forms-that-actually-get-filled-out/) explains how to keep that route manageable.

If you have multiple genuine locations, make the number appropriate to the page. A customer reading about one branch should not reach another without explanation. Check after-hours routing and any menu options as part of the same customer journey.

## Test the full route and measure it honestly

Choose a time when your team expects a test call. Open the home page and a few service pages on a phone, tap the link, inspect the number offered, and complete a call. Confirm that it reaches the intended person or voicemail rather than stopping at the dialer check.

Repeat on another available phone type when possible. Also check the desktop experience; a computer may offer an installed calling app, so keep the readable number available regardless of whether that app is configured.

If your analytics records phone-link clicks, label that measurement accurately. A tap might open the dialer without the customer completing the call. Someone may also read the number and dial manually, leaving no phone-link click to count.

For a simple business check, record actual inquiries and ask callers how they found you when it fits the conversation. If you use dedicated call-tracking numbers, ask how routing, number ownership, and cancellation work. Keep the displayed number and link destination aligned, including when a tracking tool changes them.

Do not assume that tracking requires recording conversations. Start with the information needed to understand whether inquiries arrive and lead to useful discussions. The [website customer measurement guide](/blog/how-to-tell-if-your-website-is-getting-you-customers/) helps distinguish activity from actual business outcomes.

Repeat the call check after changing phone providers, forwarding rules, website templates, or tracking tools. A link that worked at launch can become outdated when the surrounding setup changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does tapping a phone link always place the call immediately?

No, the behavior depends on the device and its calling setup. Many devices present a confirmation or calling option, so test the experience rather than promising an identical result everywhere.

### Should I hide the number behind a Call now button?

Keep the number visible as well as providing a clear action. That helps customers who want to dial from another device or who cannot use the calling option their browser offers.

### Can I count phone-link clicks as leads?

Treat them as signs of intent rather than confirmed inquiries. Compare them with calls actually received and work actually discussed before drawing conclusions about the website's results.
