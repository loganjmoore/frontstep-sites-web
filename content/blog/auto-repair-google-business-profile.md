You want someone searching for "brake repair near me" to find the right information about your shop. How should you set up and maintain your Google Business Profile? Concentrate on the details that help a driver decide whether you service the vehicle and understand what happens when they contact you.

## Start with the correct business and account

Search for the shop before creating anything new. There may already be a listing under your business name, especially if the shop has operated at the address for some time. Follow the available ownership and verification process for the existing business where appropriate, rather than creating a duplicate.

Use an account the business can keep access to. If a service adviser or outside helper manages information, make sure access does not depend solely on that person's personal arrangements. Store recovery details securely and review access when roles change.

Check the business name, address, map position, phone number, and website link. Use the name customers know from your real business, and avoid adding a string of service keywords. [Google's business guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) explain how to represent the shop accurately.

If you operate a mobile repair business instead of a customer-facing shop, describe that model truthfully. Do not advertise an address as a place where drivers can arrive for service if you do not receive customers there.

## Choose categories and services that match the bays

Choose the closest accurate business category from the options available in your profile. Do not guess at category names or select unrelated ones just because they sound popular. Review the choices in the actual interface and use what reflects the work your shop performs.

Then describe services in customer language. Depending on your real capabilities, these might include:

- Brake inspection and repair.
- Check-engine-light diagnosis.
- Oil changes and scheduled maintenance.
- Steering and suspension repair.
- Air conditioning diagnosis and service.
- Tire work or wheel alignment, if provided.

Make vehicle scope clear on your website and in appropriate profile information. If you specialize in certain makes, say so accurately. Do not imply that you handle diesel, hybrid, or electric vehicle work simply because you perform general auto repair.

Distinguish diagnosis from a promised repair. A customer searching "car AC blowing warm" needs an assessment, not a guarantee that one recharge solves every problem. ## Make hours and arrival information practical

Publish hours that match when customers can reach staff and use the shop as described. A key drop does not mean the service counter is staffed all night. Explain after-hours drop-off separately through your website and normal customer communication.

Review holiday hours, temporary closures, and changes to Saturday availability. Check that the website and phone greeting agree. A driver arranging a ride should not discover at the locked door that the hours on your profile were outdated.

Make the entrance easy to recognize. A shop in a shared industrial building may need a clear exterior photo and written arrival directions on its website. If the customer entrance differs from the bay doors, show that distinction.

Use caution with details that belong in private instructions. Do not publish alarm information, lockbox codes, or customer keys in photographs. Towing and drop-off arrangements should reflect your actual process, including whether they need confirmation before a vehicle arrives.

## Add photos that answer a driver's questions

Use recent photographs of the actual shop, sign, customer entrance, service counter, and team where appropriate. These help a first-time customer recognize the place and understand the setting. A polished stock photo of an unrelated workshop cannot do that job.

Photographs of work should have a clear purpose and customer permission where needed. Remove license plates, vehicle identification numbers, repair orders, screens, and other identifying details before publishing. Do not present a customer's vehicle as an endorsement without permission.

Choose a manageable set of useful images. A driver planning to leave a vehicle may care more about locating the entrance than seeing another close-up of a brake rotor. If you show specialist equipment, explain the service it supports without claiming capabilities the shop lacks.

Review the photos when the exterior, signage, or customer area changes. The guide to [business photography with a phone](/blog/how-to-take-photos-for-your-business-website-with-a-phone/) offers practical ways to collect images during normal shop operations.

## Use updates and questions to clarify service

Where posting features are available, use updates for current, useful information. A change in scheduling, a genuine service announcement, or a link to an explanation of your diagnostic process can help. Do not publish repetitive sales copy simply to fill a posting calendar.

Keep time-sensitive information current. An expired promotion or old Saturday-hours announcement can confuse a customer even if the main hours are correct. Remove or clarify outdated material when it no longer represents the shop.

If customer questions or a Q&A surface are available on your listing, review what is visible and answer through the supported options. Features can vary, so keep essential answers on your website regardless. Common topics include vehicle makes, appointment requests, diagnostic fees, towing arrivals, and after-hours drop-off.

Avoid answering a symptom with a remote diagnosis. You can explain that the shop assesses warning lights without promising a particular fault or repair cost. Invite the customer to contact the service desk with the relevant details.

## Keep reviews and upkeep tied to the customer experience

Read reviews for questions your service process should answer earlier. If customers repeatedly misunderstand diagnostic charges or authorization steps, review your counter explanation and website wording. Treat the pattern as information you can act on.

Reply respectfully without revealing repair records or private vehicle details. A short acknowledgement and an invitation to discuss a concern through the shop can be more useful than a public argument over every line of the invoice.

Build a neutral review invitation into your normal completion process. Let customers choose whether and what to write. The [auto repair review guide](/blog/auto-repair-get-more-reviews/) covers timing and sample invitations without pressure or incentives.

Assign one person to check the profile after changes to hours, staffing, services, or location. Also test the website link and main phone number periodically. Review information from a customer's perspective, not only while signed into the management account.

Keep a short list of corrections and confirm they appear as intended. An edit submitted in an interface is not the same as verifying what a driver actually sees.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I list every make of vehicle?

Describe the vehicles your shop can genuinely service. If capabilities differ by repair type, explain the limitation instead of implying complete coverage. Customers should be able to confirm fit before arranging a tow or a ride.

### Can I show a key drop as being open all night?

Do not confuse drop-off access with staffed business hours. Explain the drop-off process separately and keep published shop hours accurate. Customers need to know when someone can answer questions and receive them in person.

### What if my profile does not have a questions feature?

Use the features available to your business without inventing a workaround. Put important questions and answers on your website and direct customers there when appropriate. The information should remain accessible even when a platform feature changes.
