You want Google to bring cleaning inquiries you can actually turn into scheduled work. A weekly house clean, an empty apartment before handover, and an office that needs evening service are different jobs. Your online information should help each customer recognize the right service and contact you with enough detail to begin.

## Start with accurate details and realistic expectations

Your Google Business Profile, website, and reviews are practical places to improve, but they are not a complete formula for rankings. Google describes local results in terms of relevance, distance, and prominence in its [local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). You cannot promise to appear first across every town your crews visit.

Check the information a customer sees before calling. Use your real business name, a working phone number, the right website link, and hours when someone can handle inquiries. Do not imply that late-night office cleaning means a person answers the sales phone all night.

Choose business categories and service descriptions that fit your actual work. If you clean homes but do not take commercial contracts, make that clear. If your team handles both, explain the difference in plain language.

Keep the service area realistic. A job beyond your normal route might require a different minimum or scheduling arrangement. Describe that during quoting instead of presenting a wide map as a promise that every address receives identical service.

## Build pages around the job being hired

A single sentence saying "We clean everything" makes it difficult for customers to know what to request. Give your main services distinct explanations. Separate pages are useful when they answer different questions, not merely because you want more pages on the website.

For recurring house cleaning, cover the normal room checklist, visit frequency, supplies, and how the first visit is assessed. Customers may be trying to keep bathrooms and floors manageable between work and school. Explain what they should pick up beforehand and how you handle access when they are out.

For move-out cleaning, explain whether the property must be empty, whether utilities need to be on, and which tasks are additional. Oven interiors, refrigerator interiors, inside cabinets, and carpet cleaning should not be left to assumption. Do not promise a returned security deposit.

Office cleaning needs its own details: work hours, restroom and breakroom scope, trash handling, building access, and the process for a walkthrough. The [cleaning homepage copy guide](/blog/cleaning-homepage-copy/) shows how to introduce these options without overwhelming the first page.

## Answer the searches behind the service names

Customers may search for "biweekly house cleaning in Madison," "move-out cleaners this week," or "evening office cleaning near me." Use phrases like these naturally when they accurately describe your work. They are examples of customer needs, not a reason to repeat a string of keywords in every heading.

Start with the question behind each search. Someone looking for a move-out cleaner may need a quote before a lease ends. Put the availability request near the service explanation, while making clear that a date is confirmed only after you accept the job.

Someone looking for regular cleaning may worry about who enters the house and whether the same checklist is followed each visit. Explain your actual team arrangements, communication process, and how customers flag priorities. State insurance or screening claims only when you can support them.

You can also use a service-area section to explain where crews work. Create individual town pages only when you have useful local information to add. ## Show evidence without exposing a customer's home

Real photos can make your service more understandable. Show a completed kitchen, a cleaned office breakroom, or your organized supplies when you have permission. Remove addresses, family photos, paperwork, entry codes, and other identifying details from the frame.

Caption the work accurately. If the photograph shows a deep clean before recurring visits begin, say so. Otherwise a customer may expect that same scope in every maintenance appointment. Do not use a sparkling oven photo to sell a standard visit if oven interiors are an additional service.

Ask for honest reviews as part of a consistent follow-up process after completed work. Invite customers to describe their experience in their own words. Avoid discounts for reviews and do not ask only the customers who first tell you they are happy.

Reply without revealing private household details. A disagreement about access or job scope should move to direct contact, where you can review the agreement. ## Remove friction between the search and the quote

Open your website on a phone and try to request cleaning as a new customer. The form should distinguish a home from an office and ask for the basics needed to respond. Do not require a long inventory of every room before someone can ask a question.

For a home, useful starting details include the town, approximate home size, service type, and preferred timing. For an office, ask about the building size, cleaning frequency, and suitable contact time. You can collect access arrangements later through an appropriate private conversation.

Make the next step explicit. "We will contact you to review the property and confirm availability" is clearer than a vague thank-you screen. Use a response window only if you can meet it.

Track the outcome of inquiries in a simple list. Record the requested service, location, source when known, and whether it became a quote or booking. If calls keep asking for carpet extraction you do not offer, correct the wording that creates that expectation before chasing more traffic.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should house cleaning and office cleaning share a page?

They can share a short homepage introduction, but separate service pages help when the quoting process and scope differ. An office manager needs access and scheduling details that may not matter to a homeowner.

### Should I advertise same-day cleaning to get more calls?

Only describe availability you can honestly offer. If urgent work depends on the crew schedule, invite customers to ask about openings and confirm the date before treating it as booked.

### Is a starting price enough for a cleaning quote?

A starting price can help if you clearly define the property and scope it covers. Explain what else affects the quote, such as condition, visit frequency, additional tasks, and access.
