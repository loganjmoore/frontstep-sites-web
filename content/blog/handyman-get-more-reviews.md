How do you get more Google reviews when your handyman jobs range from a loose door handle to a full day of repairs? Make the request part of a clear job closeout. The customer should understand what you finished and what remains before you invite them to describe the experience.

## Make the punch-list walkthrough your starting point

At the end of a visit, review the agreed tasks with the customer. Open the adjusted cabinet door, point out the drywall patch, or show the assembled furniture. Explain any instructions or follow-up work that belongs with the completed job.

Be clear when a task remains open. Perhaps the customer supplied the wrong hinge, a replacement part has not arrived, or painting was outside the agreed scope. Record what happens next rather than treating the entire list as finished because you are leaving for the day.

Choose a consistent completion point for your review invitation. For a job requiring an agreed return visit, that may be after the return work is complete. For an independently completed repair, the normal handover can be enough. The rule should follow the job, not your guess about the customer's rating.

This closeout also protects against avoidable confusion. A customer may be satisfied with a patch but unhappy because they thought the price included repainting the whole wall. A review request cannot fix an unclear scope.

## Invite honest feedback across your completed jobs

Give customers the same optional opportunity to review their experience. Do not ask only people who praise you, pay immediately, or have used you for years. A new customer with a small repair should receive the same respectful treatment as a property manager with regular work.

Google prohibits review incentives and selectively requesting positive reviews. Keep rewards and satisfaction screening out of the process, as explained in its [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en).

Avoid asking "Were you happy?" and providing the public link only after a favorable answer. You can ask how the job went and invite a review without making public feedback conditional on praise. A direct contact option should be available to everyone as well.

Keep any correction or goodwill adjustment separate from reviews. If you need to return to realign a door, arrange that because you are addressing the work. Never make the visit depend on a customer changing a rating or deleting a complaint.

## Use language that fits an ordinary home visit

You do not need a sales speech. After the walkthrough, you could say: "Thanks for having me out. If you'd like to share your experience, there's a Google review link with your receipt." Let the customer decide whether and when to use it.

For an agreed follow-up message, adapt this example: "Thanks for booking the repair visit. If you'd like to leave an honest review, here's our Google link. Please contact me with any questions about the work or the next steps we discussed."

Do not write the review for them or ask for phrases such as "best handyman near me." The customer can mention punctuality, communication, or the repair itself if those details matter to them. Their wording should remain their own.

Use your actual review link, checked on a phone. Google provides a [review link and QR code option](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en-GB). Put it somewhere easy to find on the receipt without making the invitation more prominent than the job details.

## Handle landlords, tenants, and repeat customers thoughtfully

Rental jobs may involve several people, but they do not all have the same experience of your business. The landlord might arrange and pay for the work while a tenant coordinates access. Do not ask someone who never dealt with you to describe a repair they did not see.

Keep the request tied to a real experience, and avoid turning one visit into a campaign aimed at everyone connected to the property. Do not ask a property manager to arrange reviews from uninvolved staff or other tenants.

For repeat customers, track whether you have already sent an invitation. A homeowner who calls you for several small jobs should not receive the same reminder after every invoice. Continue asking about the work directly without pressuring them to publish repeated praise.

Use only an appropriate, agreed contact channel. Keep access arrangements, tenant names, and the property address out of the review request unless needed for the private service conversation. ## Respond to complaints with the job record in mind

Read the review and check the agreed scope before replying. A complaint about an unfinished room may involve work that was never included, but the public reply is not the place to publish an invoice or argue line by line.

You could respond: "I'm sorry the result did not match your expectations. Please contact me so we can review the agreed tasks and discuss what still needs attention." Use wording that fits the actual situation and do not claim to have investigated before you have.

Keep addresses, photos of private rooms, payment details, and tenant information out of public replies. Even if you believe the customer is wrong, exposing their home details creates another problem. You can be clear and professional without litigating the whole visit online.

If you believe a review violates Google's rules, use the reporting process. Do not assume that disagreement about workmanship qualifies for removal. Address legitimate concerns through your normal service process regardless of the review's status.

## Build a routine you can maintain between jobs

Add a review-invitation check to the same record you use for closing work and sending receipts. Keep it simple: completed job, invitation sent, and any follow-up task. Do not create a rating prediction or mark customers as safe or unsafe to ask.

Check new reviews regularly and look for repeated practical issues. If several people mention unclear material charges, improve the estimate explanation. If customers are unsure whether you take small jobs, clarify your minimum visit and accepted work.

Use the [handyman Google Business Profile guide](/blog/handyman-google-business-profile/) to keep your service details accurate. Review the [handyman website mistakes guide](/blog/handyman-website-mistakes/) when feedback points to a gap between your page and the actual visit.

Measure whether your process is being followed and whether customer concerns are resolved. A particular number of reviews cannot guarantee a place in search results or a full calendar.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I ask after a very small repair?

Yes, a customer who hired you for a small job has a real experience to share. Keep the invitation optional and use the same standard you apply to larger completed jobs.

### Should I wait until every return visit is finished?

Use a consistent rule based on the agreed job scope. If a return visit is part of finishing that job, complete the handover before treating it as closed for your request routine.

### Can I offer a discount on the next visit?

Do not tie a discount to posting, changing, or removing a review. Any ordinary promotion should remain independent of whether the customer publishes feedback.

Close out the work clearly, invite an honest review, and let the customer choose whether to respond.
