## What does website hosting pay for?

Website hosting pays for the service that makes the site's files available when a customer opens its address. The domain points people to the site, while SSL lets the browser use HTTPS. Our guide to [website hosting in plain English](/blog/what-website-hosting-is-in-plain-english/) separates those parts and explains why a domain bill and a hosting bill can come from different companies.

A four-page trade website may be technically simple, but it still needs a dependable public address. A plumber's customer may open a service page from a phone beside a leaking fixture. A tree-service customer may need the contact route after a storm. Hosting delivers those pages. Writing a new service description or correcting a phone number is maintenance work.

That difference matters when a proposal says "hosting included." Ask where content changes, broken contact routes, domain renewal, and support appear in the written scope. A low hosting price can be accurate while leaving those jobs with the owner.

## What does website maintenance cover?

Website maintenance covers agreed work that keeps the published site accurate and working. For this plan, that includes handling renewals and publishing up to 2 requested changes each month within 2 business days. A request might update Saturday hours, replace a project photo, or add a short service detail.

Technical upkeep depends on the site's software. A static site has fewer moving parts than a plugin-heavy content management system, but its forms, links, domain settings, and published pages still need checking. The National Institute of Standards and Technology calls patching a critical part of preventive maintenance and says it helps prevent compromises, data breaches, operational disruptions, and other adverse events in its [enterprise patch management guidance](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/40/r4/final). NIST's guidance explains why software updates belong in a maintenance discussion, though it does not endorse any website company or define what this plan maintains.

Content maintenance is easier to see. If a landscaping company stops offering weekly mowing, the website should stop advertising it. If the estimate email changes, someone needs to update the contact route and test it. ## How is a website subscription different from a one-time build?

A website subscription keeps recurring responsibilities inside an ongoing service agreement. A one-time build pays for a defined project, after which hosting, domain renewal, edits, and repairs may move to the owner or a second provider.

The label alone leaves out important details. A subscription can have tight change limits, and a one-time project can include a support period. Compare the written handoff, recurring charges, response time, ownership, and exit terms.

| Question | Subscription plan | One-time build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who hosts the site? | Often the same provider | Owner or a separate host after handoff |
| Who renews the domain? | May be included | Usually the owner unless stated otherwise |
| Who publishes routine changes? | Provider within plan limits | Owner or hired help |
| What happens after launch? | Service continues for the term | Project closes unless support is added |
| What can the owner take away? | Must be stated in the agreement | Must be stated in the handoff |

A redesign is a different job from ordinary maintenance. The guide to [what a website redesign should cost](/blog/what-a-website-redesign-should-cost-and-when-you-need-one/) helps separate a new structure or visual direction from routine upkeep.

## What should a trade business ask before paying?

A trade business should ask for a written list of recurring work, limits, and ownership before paying for hosting or maintenance. Use the same questions for a $99 plan and a much larger proposal.

- Is the domain included, and whose name appears as the registrant?
- Does hosting include SSL and renewal handling?
- How many content changes are included, and how quickly are they published?
- Who tests the phone link and contact form after a change?
- Are backups, software updates, and restores relevant to this site's technology?
- What costs extra, and does the provider quote it before starting?
- What files and domain access does the owner receive when leaving?

Keep the answers beside the invoice. That gives the owner a practical maintenance checklist and a record to use if the service changes later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is website hosting the same as website maintenance?

No. Hosting makes the site's files available online. Maintenance covers agreed checks, renewals, fixes, and content changes. Some plans bundle both, while other providers bill them separately.

### Does $99 a year include the domain?

Yes. The Front Step Sites Launch plan includes the domain, and the site says it is registered in the customer's name. The three-year example therefore uses three $99 subscription payments with no added domain line.

### Are unlimited website changes included?

No. Launch includes 2 change requests a month completed within 2 business days. A business that needs more frequent changes should compare a larger plan or another maintenance arrangement.

### Can a one-time website build have ongoing costs?

Yes. A one-time fee can cover design and launch while leaving hosting, domain renewals, software care, and future edits as recurring costs. Ask for the first-year and later-year responsibilities in writing.
