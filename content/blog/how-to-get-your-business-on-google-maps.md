Want your business to appear when someone opens Google Maps and looks for help nearby? Start with a Google Business Profile that describes your actual business, then verify that you manage it. The main decisions are whether a profile already exists and whether customers visit you or you travel to them.

## Look for your business before adding it

Search Google Maps for your business name and town. Try your phone number and an older business name if you have changed names. A listing may already exist even if you never created it.

If the correct listing is there, follow the claim process. If someone else manages it, use Google's ownership request process instead of adding another version. A former employee or marketing company may still have access, so check your records before assuming the listing belongs to a stranger.

If there is no listing, start a new Business Profile. Adding or claiming a profile has no Google fee. Google's [add or claim instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en) explain both routes.

Use a Google Account your business controls. Keep its recovery information current, and avoid making a temporary helper's personal account your only way in. Save the profile link with your other business account details so you can find the correct listing later.

## Decide whether customers come to your address

An auto repair shop that receives vehicles at its garage has different needs from a mobile detailer who works in customers' driveways. The garage needs an accurate public destination. The detailer needs clear coverage information without sending drivers to a private home.

Google distinguishes businesses that receive customers from service-area businesses that visit customers. If you do not serve customers at your business address, hide that address from the public profile and describe the areas you serve. A business that does both may qualify for a hybrid setup. Check [Google's service-area guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en).

Do not rent a mailbox and describe it as a staffed shop. Google says virtual offices where you do not operate are not eligible business locations. Its [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) explain the location rules.

Write down the towns you can reliably reach for the work you offer. A lawn care route and an emergency drain call may have different practical boundaries. Your [website service-area pages](/blog/service-area-pages-when-they-help-and-when-they-hurt/) should explain those differences honestly.

## Prepare your details before starting verification

Collect the information you want a customer to see before filling out the profile. This reduces rushed guesses and makes it easier to spot a wrong phone number or old website address.

- Your business name as customers know it.
- Your main customer phone number.
- Your website address, if you have one.
- The services you actually offer.
- Your real customer-facing hours.
- Your public location or the areas you visit.

Choose a business category that describes the main work you do. A tree removal company should not choose a landscaping category merely because it sounds broader. Describe supporting services separately, using words customers recognize, such as stump grinding or storm cleanup.

Keep a reference copy of these details for your website and other listings. Matching the real business facts matters more than forcing every sentence to be identical. ## Complete the verification method Google offers

Google determines which verification methods are available to your business. Follow the choices shown in your profile rather than assuming you can request a postcard or choose a phone call. Available methods and requirements can differ between businesses. Use [Google's verification instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) for your situation.

If you are asked for video evidence, read the prompts before recording. Prepare to demonstrate the business, its location, and your authority to manage it. A mobile operator might need different evidence from a shop with a customer entrance. Follow the actual instructions instead of copying another owner's recording.

Set aside time when you can access the required equipment or premises. Ask anyone helping you to explain the process, but keep account passwords and verification codes private. Do not assume a caller promising instant approval is acting for Google.

After submitting, check the profile for its status or further requests. Avoid announcing that everything is live until you have checked the public result. If there is a problem, use the support route connected to your profile rather than repeatedly creating new listings.

## Check the listing as a customer would

Once you can manage the profile, inspect what the public sees on a phone. Look for your business by name and open the listing itself. Check the contact details against your reference copy.

For a storefront, test the route to the entrance customers should use. An auto shop's service driveway may be around the corner from its mailing entrance. Make that distinction clear on your website, especially if towing companies or after-hours customers need special instructions.

For a business that travels, check that your private address is not being presented as a walk-in destination. Confirm that the phone reaches the right person and that the website explains how to request work.

If customers visit you, you can also [add a Google Map to your website](/blog/how-to-add-a-google-map-to-your-website-the-right-way/). Test that map separately, since a working Business Profile does not prove your website points to the correct place.

## Keep the profile useful after setup

Give one person responsibility for checking hours, services, photos, and contact details when the business changes. Put that task beside the website update so both sources stay accurate. Remove services you no longer offer instead of leaving them online to attract unsuitable calls.

Being verified and ranking well are different things. Google describes local results in terms of relevance, distance, and prominence, and says you cannot pay for a better organic local ranking. See its [local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

Focus on useful maintenance rather than searching your name repeatedly and worrying about every position change. Ask new customers where they found you and which service they needed. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website to get on Google Maps?

A website is not a prerequisite for adding an eligible business profile. A useful website still gives customers room to check services, project photos, and how your estimates work before calling.

### Can I appear without showing my home address?

Yes, an eligible service-area business can hide its address from customers. Provide accurate information for verification and avoid publishing your home as a place people can visit.

### Why is my business listed but I cannot edit it?

An existing listing may be unclaimed or managed through another account. Find out which situation applies, then claim it or request ownership through Google's process.

### Will verification put me first for searches nearby?

No, verification does not reserve a ranking. Keep your information accurate and judge results by suitable inquiries, rather than expecting every nearby search to show you first.
