An AI answer gives out your old phone number or says you are open on Sunday when you are not. You changed your website months ago, so where is that information coming from? Start by checking the public sources for conflicting facts, then correct the places you control.

## Understand the problem without guessing how AI ranks you

If your website says one thing and a directory says another, anyone researching your business has a contradiction to resolve. A customer can call to ask. An AI answer may simply present information that does not match your current operation.

There is a documented reason to check beyond your own website. Google says its [business information comes from multiple sources](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2721884?hl=en), including public websites, licensed data, and user contributions. That explains how Google's local information can include details you did not enter yourself. It does not reveal the source or decision process behind every AI assistant's answer.

The practical conclusion is narrower: removing incorrect public facts reduces the material that could mislead a customer or a system consulting those pages. Do not confuse that sensible housekeeping with a proven AI ranking boost.

## Write down your current facts before editing listings

Make one working document with the business details you want staff and website providers to use. Keep it somewhere you can update easily. Date it when something changes, and name the person responsible for maintaining it.

Include:

- The real business name customers know.
- Your main customer phone number and website address.
- Your customer-facing location, or the area where you travel to customers.
- Normal customer-facing hours and any appointment restrictions.
- The services you currently offer, plus important exclusions.
- Holiday closures and seasonal changes.
- Any former business name, location, or phone number that needs cleanup.

For example, a mobile auto detailer might record that interior detailing is available at customers' homes, but paint correction is handled only at an approved workspace. "Mobile detailing" alone leaves a customer guessing about what can happen in their driveway.

The document is your reference for editing, not a public collection of sensitive information. Keep account passwords and private verification documents elsewhere.

## Separate hours, response times, and service availability

One of the easiest contradictions to create is using "open" to mean several different things. Your office might answer calls during the day while a technician handles previously booked evening appointments. A form may accept requests all night without anyone responding until morning.

Explain those differences in plain language. A garage door company could say that normal appointments run Monday through Friday and that after-hours emergency availability must be confirmed by phone, if that reflects its actual operation. Do not let a leftover "24/7" banner override that explanation.

Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) call for accurate names, locations or service areas, and appropriate customer-facing hours. They also tell service-area businesses to hide their address from customers. Consistency does not mean exposing a home address everywhere just to fill an empty field.

Decide what each published hour means. Then compare the homepage, contact page, footer, booking form, voicemail, and listings. Your staff should give the same practical answer when someone calls.

## Correct the sources customers are likely to encounter

Start with your own site and the listings you actively use. Search for your business name with the city, then search for the old phone number or address. Look for abandoned social profiles, outdated chamber listings, supplier pages, and copies of old promotional material.

Keep a small correction list with the page address, wrong detail, correct detail, and status. Mark a change complete only after the public page shows it. An email requesting a correction is progress, but it is not evidence that the listing changed.

Prioritize errors by customer impact. A disconnected number, wrong city, or incorrect "permanently closed" label deserves attention before punctuation differences in an otherwise correct address. Focus on whether the information identifies the right business and gets people to the right place.

## Make service changes explicit on your website

Business information includes what you do, not just how to reach you. If you stop offering weekly mowing and focus on landscape installation, a phone number cleanup will not fix pages still inviting customers to book lawn maintenance.

Review service navigation, page headings, old FAQs, and downloadable brochures. Decide whether each old page should be updated, replaced with a helpful explanation, or removed with help from your website provider. Avoid leaving a live booking invitation for work you no longer accept.

For a renamed business, a brief, truthful note about the former name can help existing customers recognize you. For a move, explain the new customer entrance and whether appointments or pickup procedures changed. Match any behind-the-scenes business information to what the public page says.

## Recheck after changes and keep a maintenance habit

Once the source pages are correct, repeat the questions that produced the wrong answer. Save the response and any linked sources. If it still shows the old detail, you have evidence of an unresolved answer, not proof that the source correction failed.

Google also explains that [Business Profiles can receive updates from other sources](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3480441?hl=en). Review profile changes instead of assuming that a previous cleanup settles the information permanently.

Put these checks beside ordinary business events: changing phone providers, adjusting winter hours, moving, hiring someone to answer calls, or adding a service. Check public information when the business changes, with an occasional review between changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Must every listing use identical punctuation?

Prioritize accurate meaning and a recognizable business identity. Standardizing your preferred spelling and address format makes maintenance easier, but a wrong phone number deserves attention before a harmless abbreviation.

### Should I publish my home address for consistency?

Do not publish a private address merely to match other listings. Follow each platform's rules for service-area businesses and explain where you serve customers without presenting your home as a walk-in location.

### Will fixing my listings make AI answers correct immediately?

You cannot promise an immediate change. Verify the source corrections first, then repeat the original questions later and record what remains wrong.

### What if the assistant gives a wrong fact without a source?

Ask where the fact came from, but verify any link it supplies yourself. If you cannot find supporting evidence, record the statement as unsupported and use the assistant's feedback option where available.

## The short version

Keep one current record of your business facts and use it to correct public pages. Pay particular attention to phone numbers, service availability, hours, and changes of location. Accurate sources are useful even when an AI answer remains unpredictable.
