ChatGPT is an AI assistant that can search the web and summarize an answer, including a mention of local businesses, when someone asks it a question. How do you get your business mentioned when someone asks ChatGPT for a plumber, roofer, or other local service? Start by making your business details clear, current, and accessible on the web. Treat that as useful groundwork, not a promise that any assistant will choose you over another company.

## Put the essential facts in plain sight

Your website should answer a few basic questions without making someone interpret slogans. What do you do, where do you work, when can someone contact you, and how do they arrange service?

Write those answers as ordinary page text. Do not leave your phone number only inside a logo image or your service area only inside a map. A person reading the page should be able to check each fact directly.

Start with this list:

- Your actual business name and main phone number.
- The services you currently offer.
- The towns or neighborhoods you serve.
- Your normal contact hours and any separate emergency availability.
- How customers request work and what happens next.

Be specific about limits. "Residential drain cleaning in these three towns" gives a clearer picture than "Complete solutions across the region." If you do not offer overnight visits, do not let an old emergency-service banner suggest otherwise.

This is practical clarity, not a verified formula for ChatGPT placement. For more examples, see [writing website content AI assistants can quote](/blog/writing-website-content-ai-assistants-can-quote/).

## Check whether search crawlers can reach your pages

A crawler is software that visits web pages. OpenAI identifies OAI-SearchBot as the crawler used to surface websites in ChatGPT search. Its documentation recommends allowing that crawler in your site's robots.txt file and allowing requests from its published network addresses. See the [official OpenAI crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

You do not need to edit these settings yourself. Send that documentation to your website provider and ask them to check access to your public service pages. They should also check whether security software is blocking the crawler.

OpenAI treats search crawling and model-training crawling separately. Allowing OAI-SearchBot does not require allowing GPTBot, the crawler associated with training. Ask your provider to preserve your preferences when reviewing access.

Passing this check does not establish that your business will be recommended. It removes an access issue. Also open your site while signed out and confirm that visitors can read the services and contact details without a login or a broken page.

## Make your business listings agree

Compare the information on your website with the business profiles you maintain on Google, Bing, Apple, Yelp, and relevant local directories. Check names, phone numbers, website addresses, hours, and service descriptions. Focus first on listings that already exist for your business.

Look for meaningful contradictions. An old phone number is a problem. So is a directory that says you offer commercial roofing when you now handle residential work only. Cosmetic punctuation differences matter less than facts that send someone to the wrong place.

Keep a simple record of where you made corrections and which ones still need attention. If a listing requires verification, follow that platform's process. Do not invent a storefront or add locations where you have no actual presence.

Consistent facts give readers fewer conflicting details to sort through. That is a sensible reason to do this work, even without knowing how an assistant weighs each source. ## Answer the questions customers ask before calling

Write down the questions you answered on recent calls. Those are good starting points for service pages and FAQs because they reflect actual decisions customers need to make.

A homeowner might ask, "Who repairs tankless water heaters in my town?" Another might ask, "Can a roofer inspect a leak around my chimney?" Your page should say whether you do that work and explain how to arrange an assessment.

Put the direct answer first, then add useful detail. For example, if it matches your business, a page could explain that you service tankless units, list the brands you handle, and ask customers to have the model information ready when calling. Do not copy that claim if you only install new units.

Keep answers tied to real operations. Explain fees, booking steps, and service boundaries where relevant. Avoid publishing dozens of nearly identical town pages or filling paragraphs with "near me." The goal is an accurate answer a customer can use, not a page written around a guessed ranking trick.

## Build evidence and label it accurately

Use real project descriptions, current credentials, and genuine customer feedback to support what your site says. A roofer can describe a completed flashing repair. A landscaper can show a permitted project photo and explain the work included.

Ask customers for honest reviews after completed work. Let them describe the experience in their own words. Do not buy reviews, write them on customers' behalf, or ask only satisfied customers while steering unhappy ones away from public feedback.

Avoid claims that a certain review count will trigger an AI recommendation. Treat feedback as information that helps people assess your business. Which reviews a particular assistant can access, and how it uses them, should not be assumed.

Structured data is another way to label factual information on your site. Google documents LocalBusiness markup for details such as a business's hours. Ask your provider whether appropriate markup is present, accurate, and tested against the [official LocalBusiness guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business).

That Google documentation does not establish a ChatGPT ranking benefit. Keep structured data consistent with visible content, and do not add invented locations or ratings. See [structured data for AI search](/blog/structured-data-for-ai-search-what-small-businesses-need/) for a plain-English explanation.

## Check answers and fix the underlying sources

Set aside a short monthly check rather than repeatedly asking until you get an answer you like. Use questions that resemble actual customer requests and include the town and service. When testing ChatGPT search, ask it to search the web and inspect the sources it provides.

Record the question, date, answer, and linked sources. Then look for actionable errors: a wrong phone number, outdated hours, a service you no longer offer, or confusion with another business.

Correct the source you control and request updates elsewhere when appropriate. Do not assume that correcting one chat changes future answers for everyone. A missing recommendation is also not proof that your site is broken.

Track actual inquiries alongside these checks. Ask callers how they found you, and record their answers without guessing. Our guide to [checking what AI assistants say about your business](/blog/how-to-check-what-ai-assistants-say-about-your-business/) provides a repeatable review approach.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can someone guarantee my business will appear in ChatGPT?

Treat that promise cautiously. A provider can commit to specific work, such as correcting listings or making pages accessible. That is different from controlling whether an assistant recommends your business for a particular request.

### Do I have to allow my website to be used for training?

OpenAI documents separate controls for search and training crawlers. You can allow OAI-SearchBot while disallowing GPTBot. Have your website provider check that your settings reflect your preferences.

### Will adding structured data put me ahead of competitors?

Do not assume it will. Accurate markup labels information, but it is not evidence of a guaranteed ChatGPT ranking improvement. Fix missing or contradictory visible details first.

### How soon should I expect a recommendation?

There is no dependable timetable you can use to promise a recommendation. Check that your changes are live, then monitor answers and actual customer inquiries over time. Avoid treating one favorable answer as a lasting position.

## The short version

Make your services, location, availability, and contact details easy to verify. Keep listings accurate, publish useful answers, and check access to your public pages. These are practical improvements with value even when an AI recommendation remains uncertain.
