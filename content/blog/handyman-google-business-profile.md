What should a handyman put on a Google Business Profile to attract suitable jobs? Be specific about the work you take and the places you reach. Someone searching for "handyman to fix a sticking door" needs a clearer answer than "all home improvements, no job too big or small."

## Get the business identity and service area right

Search for your existing listing before making a new one. If the business already appears, follow Google's ownership process and use an account you control. Keep access organized so you can still change your phone number if a previous marketing helper leaves.

Use your real business name and an accurate service area. If customers do not visit your operating address, hide it under the appropriate service-area setup. Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) explain these requirements.

Think about the route you can actually work. A loose cabinet hinge across town and a full day's punch list at the edge of your coverage are different scheduling decisions. Describe practical travel arrangements on your website rather than listing every distant town you might theoretically visit.

Do not create a separate supposed location for each neighborhood on your route. Your public information should describe the business customers actually hire. Keep the name and phone details consistent with your estimates and website.

## Describe jobs in the words customers use

Choose the closest available primary category for your main work. Review the current options and add categories only when they describe real parts of your business. Categories should identify your business, while service descriptions explain individual jobs.

Write about tasks customers recognize. Depending on your actual work, that might include:

- Adjusting interior doors that rub or do not latch properly.
- Repairing cabinet hinges and drawer hardware.
- Patching small areas of damaged drywall and discussing finish expectations.
- Installing shelves, curtain rods, or other household fixtures you accept.
- Assembling furniture from the manufacturer's parts and instructions.
- Completing an agreed list of minor repairs before a move.

Do not advertise electrical, plumbing, structural, or other regulated work just because it appears on another handyman's page. Check with your state or local authority about the work you may offer. Describe exclusions clearly when customers could confuse your service with a specialist trade.

## Make the estimate process clear before the first call

Explain what you need to assess a job. A useful starting request includes the customer's general location, a short list of tasks, and photos when appropriate. Ask whether parts or fixtures have already been purchased if that affects your planning.

For a sticking door, you may need a description of the problem and a photo of the area. For shelving, the customer might need to explain the intended location and what they expect the shelf to hold. Use this information to decide what needs an on-site assessment, not to promise every job can be priced from an image.

Clarify any minimum visit, estimate arrangement, or material-purchasing policy you actually use. A person with one loose handle should not discover your minimum only after setting aside an afternoon.

Distinguish an inquiry from a booking. State when you confirm scope and availability, and explain how additions to a punch list are handled. Keep these details on the linked website even if the profile has limited room.

## Set hours that reflect how customers reach you

Keep published hours aligned with how your business operates. If you are on a ladder or using tools, you may not answer immediately. Explain your callback process on the website without presenting yourself as continuously available for urgent repairs.

Do not label ordinary handyman service as emergency coverage unless you actually provide that service. A customer with an active water leak may need a different professional and cannot rely on a voicemail you hear the next morning.

Update planned closures, holidays, and extended absences. Check the booking or contact page at the same time. Conflicting information makes customers wonder which version to trust.

For landlords and property managers, explain how to request access coordination rather than publishing tenant details or codes. A profile should help start the conversation, not expose the arrangements used to enter a property.

## Show workmanship through clear project photos

Use photos that let people understand the job: repaired trim, aligned cabinet doors, a finished patch, or an assembled item in its intended location. Include enough context that the viewer knows what changed. An extreme close-up of a screw tells them very little.

For before-and-after examples, keep the angle and lighting reasonably consistent. Explain the actual scope if the work stopped before painting or another trade completed the finish. Do not show a whole-room transformation as your work when you only installed one fixture.

Get permission before photographing inside a customer's home. Keep faces, documents, addresses, family photos, and valuables out of the image. Avoid revealing how a lock or entry system is arranged.

## Keep questions, updates, and reviews useful

Where posts are available, share specific updates such as a new service you now accept or a confirmed scheduling change. Keep them factual. A weekly stream of "best handyman in town" claims gives a customer less useful information than a clear explanation of your repair visits.

Answer visible customer questions when the feature is available, and keep recurring answers on your website too. Common subjects include supplying materials, combining several small tasks, working in occupied rentals, and whether an estimate requires a visit.

Reply to reviews in a way that respects the home and its occupants. Thank customers for details they chose to share. If a complaint involves an unfinished task, invite a direct conversation and check the agreed scope before responding further.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I list every repair I might consider?

List the work you actually want and are equipped to accept. Use the website to explain that unusual jobs need assessment rather than turning the profile into an unlimited promise.

### Can I use photos from a former employer's projects?

Only use material you have permission to use and describe your role accurately. Your own recent work is a clearer way to show customers what your current business provides.

### Do I need separate profiles for different towns?

Do not create locations that are not real eligible business locations. Describe your actual service area and check Google's guidance if your business has a more complex operating setup.
