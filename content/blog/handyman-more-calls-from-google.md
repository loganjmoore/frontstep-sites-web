How do you get more handyman calls from Google without filling the day with jobs you cannot take? Start by describing your work more precisely than "no job too small." Someone with a sticking door, loose trim, or a list of rental repairs needs to know whether you handle those tasks and how to get an estimate.

## Name the jobs you want on the calendar

A handyman business can cover many kinds of work, but your website should reflect your actual services. Write down the jobs you want more of, such as door adjustments, drywall patches, furniture assembly, shelving, or small trim repairs.

Use customer language. "Door won't latch" describes a problem someone recognizes. "Residential maintenance solutions" does not explain much. Include natural search phrases in useful sentences rather than stacking town names and repair terms at the bottom of every page.

Be clear about limits. If you do not do roofing, structural work, or specialist electrical and plumbing jobs, say so. Check with your state about requirements that apply to the work you offer, and do not imply credentials you do not hold.

The [handyman homepage copy guide](/blog/handyman-homepage-copy/) can help turn your actual job list into readable service descriptions.

## Check that your Google profile describes your business

Confirm the phone number, website link, hours, and services. Use your real business name and choose an appropriate available category. Do not add a string of repair keywords to the name just to try to appear in more searches.

If you travel to customers, your public information should explain where you work and how to contact you. Follow [Google's business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) for service-area businesses. Do not list a location as a customer-facing office unless it actually functions that way.

Keep business hours realistic. A phone in your pocket does not mean you can safely answer every call while working. Explain how customers can leave job details and when you normally review inquiries.

Use the [handyman business profile guide](/blog/handyman-google-business-profile/) for a focused pass through the listing. Fix missing or contradictory facts before worrying about posting more often.

## Explain how small jobs get estimated

The first inquiry should collect enough information to judge fit without making the customer write a building report. Ask for the job location, a short task list, a few useful photos if available, and a way to reply.

Help people send photos that answer real questions. For a damaged door frame, ask for a wider view and a close view of the problem. For shelves, ask about the intended location and what the customer wants to store. Do not present photos as a substitute for an inspection when one is needed.

Publish any minimum visit policy and explain how you discuss materials. If bundling several small tasks into one visit makes sense for your business, invite customers to send the whole list. That can prevent several separate conversations about work at the same property.

A request is not a confirmed appointment. Say how you review the details, decide whether a visit is needed, and agree on the scope before scheduling.

## Show completed work people can recognize

Use photos of the repairs you actually want to repeat. A neat drywall patch, rehung cabinet door, or assembled storage unit may be more useful than a dramatic renovation you rarely take on.

Give the photo a factual caption. "Adjusted the interior door and replaced damaged trim" tells a customer what you did. Avoid calling a cosmetic repair a structural fix or implying that every similar problem has the same cause.

Get the customer's permission and keep addresses, family photos, access codes, and other private details out of view. If a property manager arranged the job, make sure you have appropriate permission before sharing images from an occupied rental.

Ask for an honest review after the customer has checked the work. Keep the request simple and avoid rewards or pressure. Google's [review request guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) explains the use of review links and its rule against incentives.

## Build pages around meaningful differences

You do not need a separate page for every screw you tighten. Group related work into services a homeowner understands, such as door and trim repairs, wall repairs, or home assembly jobs. Explain scope, limitations, and the next step on each useful page.

Keep your service area realistic. Travel can overwhelm a short repair visit, so clearly state where you work and how you handle jobs farther away. Do not imply that you have a branch office in every town on the list.

If you create local pages, add real information that changes the customer's decision. Copying the same repair paragraph beneath a different town heading does not explain your service any better.

## Find the gap between discovery and booked work

Track the source, general location, requested tasks, and outcome of inquiries. A basic note is enough. You want to see whether people need work you accept and whether they can move from asking to scheduling.

If callers mainly request full bathroom remodels while you want punch-list visits, review your photos and headings. If people leave because they did not expect a minimum visit charge, explain that policy earlier. If good inquiries sit unanswered, fix the follow-up routine before adding more traffic.

Check the phone and form on a real mobile device. Confirm that the form reaches you and that the customer sees accurate next-step instructions.

Google's [local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) describes relevance, distance, and prominence as major factors. Your information can improve clarity, but it cannot guarantee a position or a certain number of jobs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I advertise every repair I know how to do?

Advertise the work you can reliably and appropriately accept. A long list that includes rare or unsuitable jobs can create wasted inquiries. Keep specialist and regulated work boundaries clear, and check applicable requirements with your state.

### Are before and after photos necessary?

They can help, but a clear completed-work photo with an accurate caption is useful too. Show the types of repairs customers can actually request. Never create a misleading comparison just to make the result look dramatic.

### Should I answer calls while on a job?

Only when it is safe and practical. Give customers a reliable way to leave details and set an honest expectation for your reply. A consistent follow-up routine is better than promising immediate attention you cannot provide.
