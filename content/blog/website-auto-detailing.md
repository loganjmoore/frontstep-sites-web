Your auto detailing website should help someone decide whether you can handle their vehicle and how to book the right service. A parent with crushed crackers in the back seat needs different information from someone asking about paint correction on a weekend car. Make those routes obvious before you ask either person to call.

## Put the service and location before the shine

Your first screen should say what you do, where you do it, and whether customers bring the vehicle to you. A headline such as "Mobile interior and exterior detailing in your driveway in Cedar Park" gives a visitor something useful immediately. Treat that as an example and substitute your actual services and town.

Follow it with one clear action: "Request a detailing quote." Put a working phone number beside it for people who would rather discuss a stubborn smell or damaged finish. If you accept texts, say so. Do not label a number "Call or text" unless both reach someone who can reply.

For a shop, give the address and explain drop-off arrangements. For mobile work, list your normal towns and any location restrictions. An apartment resident should learn early whether you need permission to work in the parking lot or access to a suitable wash area.

Avoid filling that first screen with a large video. A recognizable photo of your van, workspace, or finished vehicle can do the job without delaying the information people came for.

## Explain packages in terms of what gets cleaned

Package names such as Gold and Platinum mean little without a work list. Give each package a plain description before you add a brand name. "Interior detail" is a useful starting point, but customers still need to know whether that includes upholstery extraction, leather cleaning, or only vacuuming and wiping surfaces.

Separate the work into understandable choices:

- Maintenance clean for a vehicle you already keep in good condition.
- Interior detail for seats, carpets, mats, and interior surfaces.
- Exterior detail for washing, decontamination, and the protection you actually apply.
- Paint correction or coating work that needs its own assessment.

Show exclusions beside the package, not in a distant policy page. Pet hair, excessive sand, smoke odor, and heavily soiled upholstery can change the work involved. Explain how you review those conditions before confirming a price.

If you publish starting prices, attach them to a vehicle size and defined condition. A compact car with light dust is not the same job as a three-row SUV after a beach trip. ## Use photos that prove the kind of work you want

Choose photographs that answer a customer's question. Show the carpet under a removed floor mat, a cleaned cupholder, or a corrected panel under similar lighting. Include ordinary daily drivers if those are the cars you want to book. A gallery made entirely of exotic cars can leave a minivan owner wondering whether you take family vehicles.

Pair before-and-after images of the same area at a similar angle. Avoid filters that exaggerate the result. A short caption should name the service and any important limit, such as "Interior detail with additional pet hair removal."

Do not promise that every stain or scratch will disappear. A photo shows the result on that vehicle, with that condition and service. Explain that you inspect the car before advising what is realistic, especially for scratches, damaged upholstery, and persistent odors.

Get permission to use customer vehicles in your marketing, and remove identifying details when appropriate. ## Answer the questions that can stop a booking

Mobile detailing introduces practical questions that a shop website may not need to address. Tell customers whether you supply water and power, what parking space you require, and how weather affects appointments. Describe your actual process rather than assuming every mobile operator works the same way.

For interior work, explain what customers should remove beforehand. Personal belongings, valuables, and child seats need a clear plan. State your own policy on handling child seats instead of leaving parents to guess what will happen when you arrive.

For a shop visit, explain whether customers leave the vehicle, wait on site, or arrange a ride. Give an honest time estimate or say that you confirm timing after reviewing the service and condition. If wet upholstery may need drying time, explain how you discuss readiness for use.

Coating customers may also need aftercare information. Keep the homepage brief and link to the service details you use when quoting. Do not turn general marketing copy into a guarantee about durability or a substitute for the product instructions you follow.

## Make the quote request useful on both sides

Ask for the information you need to start a real conversation: name, contact method, vehicle type, desired service, and town. An optional photo upload can help with pet hair, stains, or a panel concern. Keep the full street address for the stage when you actually need it.

A sensible request form can follow this order:

1. Identify the vehicle and the main problem.
2. Choose mobile service or shop drop-off if you offer both.
3. Provide a reply method and preferred timing.
4. Show a confirmation explaining that the appointment still needs approval.

Test the form from your own phone and confirm the request arrives. Then reply as you would to a customer. A successful submission is only useful if someone receives it and follows up.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should every detailing package have a price?

Publish a price when you can define what it covers. If condition changes the quote, explain the starting scope and what you need to review before confirming the total.

### Do I need separate pages for ceramic coatings and interior detailing?

Separate pages help when the services raise different questions and you have useful details for each. Coating preparation, assessment, and aftercare deserve different explanations from crumbs, upholstery, and pet hair.

### Can customers book without calling first?

Yes, if your booking process handles the service, vehicle, location, and time correctly. If you need to inspect photos or confirm access, label the action as a request rather than a confirmed appointment.
