Your Google Business Profile has been suspended, and you need to know what to do next. Start by finding the stated reason and checking your information against Google's rules. A careful correction and a supported appeal give Google something concrete to review; creating replacement listings does not fix the original problem.

## Confirm what has actually been restricted

Sign in with the account that manages the business and read the notice. Save the wording, date, profile details, and any case reference. A rejected photo, a verification request, and a suspended profile need different responses, so do not diagnose the problem from a missing search result alone.

Google's [suspension and appeal instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en) explain that its appeals tool shows the restriction reason and the related policy. If the account itself is restricted, follow Google's account appeal instructions first. Profile appeals may still be needed after the account restriction is lifted.

Write down recent changes: a move, new phone number, ownership transfer, name edit, or a provider gaining access. These are facts to investigate, not proof that one particular edit caused the suspension. Avoid undoing accurate information simply because the timing looks suspicious.

## Review the business against the relevant rule

Open the policy linked to your notice and read the part that applies. Use Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) to check the basics: real-world name, genuine business location, appropriate category, and duplicate profiles.

Examples of issues worth checking include:

- Added search phrases that are not part of your established business name.
- A mailing address presented as an operating location.
- Multiple profiles created for one business without eligibility for separate listings.
- A customer-facing address that does not reflect how the business operates.

A plumber operating from home and visiting customers should not present the house as a staffed plumbing shop. A repair garage with a reception desk is a different situation. Google's [service-area rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) say to remove the public address if you do not serve customers there.

The guide to [getting your business on Google Maps](/blog/how-to-get-your-business-on-google-maps/) explains that storefront-versus-service-area distinction. Fix the actual mismatch before asking a reviewer to reconsider it.

## Gather evidence before opening the appeal

Prepare readable documents that support the business information being reviewed. Google lists business registration, licenses, tax certificates, and business utility bills as examples of possible evidence. Check that the name and address on your evidence match the business you are appealing for.

Open every file yourself before submitting it. A document photographed in poor light or cut off at the edge creates an avoidable problem. Use clear file names so you can identify what you sent later, and keep a copy of the original material.

If you recently moved, look for current records rather than relying on a pile of documents for the former premises. If your customer-facing business name differs from the legal entity name, gather legitimate records that explain the relationship. Do not alter a document to make it appear to say something it does not.

Keep private material in the official support process. Posting tax documents, customer invoices, or account records in a public help forum can expose information that does not need to be public. Follow the official form's requirements for what to include.

## Submit a short explanation with the relevant evidence

Use the official appeals route linked from Google's suspension help page. Follow the current prompts for evidence and any deadline shown. Have the files ready before starting, since the sequence and upload instructions can change.

Keep your explanation factual. Say what the business does, which information was wrong if you found a problem, what you corrected, and how the attached material supports the corrected profile. Avoid guessing about Google's systems or writing a long account of how unfair the interruption feels.

For example, a hypothetical mobile appliance repair business might explain: “We repair appliances at customers' homes and do not receive customers at our operating address. We removed the public address and corrected the service area. The attached current registration and business utility bill support our business details.” Use only statements and documents that are true for your situation.

Save the submission confirmation and your explanation. Google explicitly says not to create another profile for the same business while the appeal is under review. Keep subsequent support requests connected to the existing case where possible.

## Keep customers able to reach you while you wait

An appeal is one task; keeping the business contactable is another. Check your website's phone number, contact form, hours, and service area today. Submit a test inquiry and confirm it reaches the person responsible for replying.

An HVAC customer with a failed furnace needs a working contact route, not an explanation of your listing problem. Keep the website's service availability honest and clear. If you only handle scheduled repairs, do not promise emergency dispatch to compensate for reduced visibility.

Check your other established listings for correct details too. Our [NAP consistency guide](/blog/nap-consistency-why-your-name-address-and-phone-must-match-everywhere/) gives you an orderly audit, while the guide to [handling website leads quickly](/blog/how-to-handle-leads-from-your-website-fast/) helps keep incoming requests from sitting unanswered.

You can tell existing customers how to contact you through communication channels you already use appropriately. Keep the message practical. There is no need to invite customers into a dispute or ask them to post claims about the suspension.

## Track the result and resolve the next concrete issue

Check the appeal's status through the official tool and monitor the managing account's email. Keep a dated note of responses so another employee does not submit a separate account of the same problem.

If Google requests more information, answer that request directly. If the appeal is denied, Google's guidance describes an additional review route where available. Recheck the reason and supply relevant new evidence instead of resending the same explanation unchanged.

After reinstatement, review the public profile again. Confirm the phone, hours, address visibility, and website link. If Google requests verification, complete the method offered rather than assuming an approved appeal finishes every step.

Keep a simple record of future profile changes and who made them. That gives you a clearer starting point if another problem appears, without turning routine listing maintenance into a guessing exercise.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does reinstatement take?

There is no dependable completion date you should promise your staff or customers. Use the status and guidance Google provides for your case, and keep other contact routes working while you wait.

### Should I delete the profile and start again?

No, do not use a replacement profile to work around a suspension. Google's instructions specifically say not to create a new profile for the same business while an appeal is under review.

### Can someone guarantee they will get my profile back?

Treat a guaranteed outcome as a reason to ask more questions. A provider can help review information and organize an appeal, but Google makes the reinstatement decision.

### What if all my information was already accurate?

Explain the business's eligibility and provide relevant supporting evidence through the official process. Do not invent a correction or change truthful details just to make the appeal sound more convincing.

## The short version

Read the restriction, correct any real policy problem, and prepare clear evidence before appealing. Use the official process, keep your records, and respond to the actual decision or request. Maintain a working website and contact route while the case is reviewed.
