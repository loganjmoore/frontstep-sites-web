What should you spend on a website for your pressure washing business? The right budget depends on whether you need a clear place to send homeowners or a custom system for quoting and scheduling different kinds of jobs. Compare the full scope before judging offers by their first invoice.

## Price the website your customers actually need

Start with your work mix. A solo operator cleaning driveways and house siding needs different content from a company handling shopping-center sidewalks and recurring commercial work. Both need clear service descriptions, but they may need different inquiry questions and examples.

Write down the jobs you want the site to help sell. Your first version might need a home page, house washing and concrete cleaning pages, a project gallery, a service-area explanation, and a quote form. Add roof cleaning, deck work, or commercial services only if you provide them.

A useful quote form might ask for the town, surface, approximate scope, and contact information. Photos can help you decide what questions to ask next, but they do not replace an assessment when access or surface condition is unclear. Avoid paying for instant pricing unless you have a reliable way to define what that price covers.

## Compare DIY and an ongoing website service

A DIY builder suits an owner who wants direct control and has time to write pages, organize pictures, and maintain the site. The subscription is only part of the commitment. You still need to learn the editor and test every contact route yourself.

For a permitted price reference, Wix Light is $17/month billed yearly, or $204/year, as of September 2026. Squarespace Basic is $19/month as of September 2026. These are plan prices, not a promise that either price covers every tool or service your particular business will need; confirm your full purchase before paying.

An ongoing service can suit you if you would rather send instructions than edit pages after a day on the truck. [Front Step Sites](/) costs $99 a year with the domain included and no setup fee; the $99 plan includes 2 change requests a month, made within 2 business days.

Compare how updates work, not just the annual number. Ask who changes your seasonal availability, adds a new driveway project, or removes a service you no longer offer. The [builder versus done-for-you guide](/blog/website-builders-vs-done-for-you-websites/) can help you weigh the time commitment.

## Understand what custom work buys

Typical freelancer costs span a wide range, often a few thousand dollars for a custom small-business build and more as the scope grows. Typical agency projects can also run from a few thousand dollars to much more when the work includes extensive content, strategy, or custom systems. Those are broad planning ranges, not quotes or market averages.

A freelancer may fit when you have a clear brief and want a particular design or inquiry process. An agency may fit when the website is part of a larger, coordinated marketing project. Ask who will perform the actual writing, design, and ongoing maintenance in either case.

For pressure washing, extra work might include organizing years of job photographs, separating residential and commercial inquiries, or connecting an established scheduling process. Custom work can be sensible when it solves an existing problem. A feature described as "advanced" is not valuable merely because it appears on a proposal.

Request examples of how a customer moves from a service page to a quote request. A screenshot of a dramatic driveway transformation does not show whether the form reaches your inbox or asks the right questions.

## Identify the details that add work

Surface-specific writing takes thought. A house washing page should address siding and property preparation; a concrete cleaning page should explain how you assess stains and access. A single paragraph repeated across services may be cheaper to produce, but it gives customers little help.

Photo preparation also affects scope. Ask whether someone will select images, reduce file sizes, add useful descriptions, and get your approval. Provide permission-cleared images from your own jobs rather than expecting the provider to invent a portfolio.

Other cost drivers include:

- Moving an existing site while keeping useful page addresses working.
- Building a quote process with separate commercial and homeowner routes.
- Adding photo uploads and explaining where submitted images go.
- Writing substantial service pages from interviews with you.
- Connecting tools your business already depends on.

Check whether each item is included, optional, or billed later. Use the [website hiring questions](/blog/what-to-ask-before-you-hire-someone-to-build-your-website/) when a proposal does not explain those boundaries.

## Compare ongoing costs and ownership before signing

Ask for a first-year total and a normal renewal-year total. Include the domain, hosting, maintenance, support, paid extras, and expected updates. A low build fee can still produce an expensive arrangement if every service change becomes a separate project.

Find out whose name the domain is registered in and who controls the account. Ask what happens to the domain, page content, photos, and forms if you leave. Domain ownership and the ability to move a particular website design are different questions, so get both answered.

Do not buy a larger package solely because someone promises a certain number of calls. A website cannot control weather, seasonal demand, competition, or whether you answer the phone. Judge the deliverables you can inspect: accurate content, usable pages, working contact routes, and clear maintenance arrangements.

Before approving the site, send a test inquiry from your phone. Confirm the message arrives with the surface and town information intact. Then check how you will reply while you are on a job, since a working form is only the beginning of a useful quote process.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need online booking for pressure washing?

Not necessarily, especially when you need to assess surfaces, access, or the job size before confirming a visit. A clear quote request can be a better fit until you have defined which services can be booked without that conversation.

### Is a before-and-after slider worth paying for?

Only if it helps customers understand your work and stays usable on a phone. Two clear, honestly matched photographs can often communicate the result without a special feature.

### Should I pay for a page for every town?

Pay for useful information, not repeated paragraphs with different place names. Separate pages make sense when you have genuine local project details or service differences to explain.

## The short version

Budget for the pages and contact process your pressure washing customers need, then compare setup and ongoing costs together. Add custom features only when they solve a real quoting or operating problem. [Front Step Sites](/) also includes new pages, blog posts, and local SEO setup.
