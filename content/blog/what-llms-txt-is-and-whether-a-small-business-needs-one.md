Do you need an llms.txt file so AI assistants can find your business? For a typical local service website, it is an optional extra rather than an urgent repair. Before paying for one, understand what it describes, who would use it, and how you will keep it accurate.

## Understand the purpose of the file

The [llms.txt proposal](https://llmstxt.org/) describes a readable text guide with background information and links to useful resources. Its name refers to large language models, the systems behind many AI assistants. The proposal uses Markdown, a simple way to organize text with headings and links.

Think of it as a suggested reading list for a tool that supports the format. A tool still has to find it, use it, and follow relevant links. Publishing a file does not establish that a particular assistant consulted it while answering a customer.

Google is explicit about its own service: llms.txt does not help or harm visibility or rankings in Google Search. Its [current AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says Search ignores these files for that purpose. That leaves room for other uses, but removes a reason to treat the file as a mandatory Google fix.

## Do not confuse a reading guide with access controls

Several website features have similar technical names but different jobs. A sitemap helps describe pages available on your site. Robots.txt communicates crawler rules. Structured data labels page facts. The llms.txt proposal describes a guide to selected information, rather than a replacement for those tools.

In particular, publishing a reading guide is not how you decide whether a provider can use your content for model training. For example, OpenAI documents separate controls for its search crawler and its training crawler in its [official crawler guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots). Search discovery and training are different uses.

Ask your provider to explain any access changes separately from the new file. If you also want business facts labeled in your pages, read [structured data for small businesses](/blog/structured-data-for-ai-search-what-small-businesses-need/). You should know what each piece is meant to accomplish before agreeing to a bundle of technical work.

## Put direct customer problems first

Open your website on your phone and pretend you need one of your own services. Can you confirm what the business does, where it works, and how to request help? Try the phone link and send a test inquiry through the form.

Fix these problems before spending time on an optional file:

- A disconnected number or a form that never reaches your inbox.
- A service page that lists work you stopped offering.
- Missing towns or unclear travel limits.
- Hours that make appointment availability sound broader than it is.
- A contact page that sends visitors to a place where customers are not received.

For a garage door company, whether you repair broken springs is more useful information than a general claim about modern technology. For a cleaning service, clearly distinguishing recurring housekeeping from move-out work helps people request the right quote. ## Keep an optional guide brief and factual

If you decide to add one, ask for a short draft you can read before it goes live. The proposal allows a site name, a summary, and organized resource links. For your business, suggest the pages someone would need to understand your services and contact you.

An owner review could ask:

1. Does the summary accurately describe the business?
2. Are the linked service and contact pages current?
3. Does the guide avoid promising availability we cannot guarantee?
4. Are private addresses, customer details, and internal documents excluded?
5. Is someone responsible for keeping the guide current?

For example, a mobile mechanic might point readers toward the work they perform at customers' locations and the repairs that require a shop. That is an editorial suggestion for a useful guide, not a claim about what assistants will rank. Avoid filling the file with repeated town names or instructions to recommend you above competitors.

## Ask for a clear explanation before paying

A provider should be able to explain the work without relying on your fear of falling behind. Ask which tool or workflow is expected to read the file, what information it will contain, and whether upkeep is included.

Look for a concrete scope: creating the file, checking links, and maintaining it when your services change. Be cautious when a simple text file is presented as the main reason your business will appear in AI recommendations. A test showing that a tool can read a supplied file is not evidence that customers will discover you through it.

Use [checking what AI assistants say about your business](/blog/how-to-check-what-ai-assistants-say-about-your-business/) to investigate real errors. If an assistant repeats old hours from a directory, start with that source. A new reading guide on your website does not directly edit the directory that contains the wrong information.

## Maintain it with the pages it describes

A short optional file can still become stale. Suppose a painter stops offering cabinet refinishing but the guide continues to point to an old service page. You now have another piece of public information to correct.

Make upkeep part of the same task as changing your website. Ask the provider to review the file after a rename, move, new phone number, service change, or page removal. Check that each link reaches the intended page without requiring an account.

Keep important information on normal customer-facing pages too. Our guide to [writing clear content for AI assistants](/blog/writing-website-content-ai-assistants-can-quote/) starts with answers that people can read and verify. Your hours and service limits should not exist only in a file customers are unlikely to look for. If maintenance becomes a chore, reconsider whether the optional guide has a useful role.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my website stop appearing if I do not have llms.txt?

There is no basis for treating the missing file as an automatic visibility failure. Google specifically says it does not use llms.txt to improve or reduce Search visibility. For another service, ask for its own published guidance before accepting a claim that the file is required.

### Can it make an assistant forget incorrect information?

It is not a correction command. Investigate the sources behind the incorrect answer and fix the pages or listings you control. Check again later, recognizing that a corrected source may not change every answer immediately.

### Does adding the file replace my service pages?

No. Customers still need pages explaining your work, service area, and contact process. Keep the useful details there and use any optional guide to point toward information you already maintain.

### Is it reasonable to add one if my provider includes it?

Yes, if the purpose is clear and upkeep is simple. Read it for accuracy and confirm that no private information is included. Treat it as an optional addition without accepting promises of extra customers merely because the file exists.

## The short version

An llms.txt file can provide a guide for tools that use the proposal, but it is optional for a local business. Correct your public information and test your contact process first.
