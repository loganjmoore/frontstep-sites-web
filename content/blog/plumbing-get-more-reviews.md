You finish a drain clearing, explain the invoice, and head to the next call. The customer may appreciate the work but never think to review it. Getting more Google reviews as a plumber starts with making an honest request part of your normal job closeout.

## Choose a moment when the visit is actually finished

A review request belongs after the customer understands what happened and what comes next. For a leaking toilet, that might be after the repair is checked, the floor is cleaned, and you explain the bill. For a water heater replacement, finish the handoff and discuss any remaining visit before asking.

Don't ask while someone is still dealing with an active backup or trying to rescue belongings from a wet basement. They need the service conversation first. Build the request into the completed-visit process instead of interrupting a stressful situation.

Be clear about what "complete" means. If you performed a sewer camera inspection and a separate repair is still being quoted, the inspection can be a completed service. Your message should refer to that visit without pretending the underlying sewer problem has been solved.

Use the same trigger for comparable jobs. A technician's guess about who looks delighted shouldn't decide which customers receive the link. A person who had a difficult visit is still entitled to describe their experience.

## Use a short request that doesn't coach the rating

A simple spoken request works: "Thanks for having us out. If you'd like to share your experience, I can send our Google review link." Say it once, then let the customer decide.

For a follow-up through a channel the customer has agreed to use, adapt this example: "Thanks for choosing us for your plumbing visit. If you'd like to leave an honest review, here's the link. We appreciate your feedback." Insert your actual review link before sending it.

Avoid telling customers to mention "emergency plumber," your town, or a technician's name. Those details should come from their experience. Never hand over a prewritten review for them to paste.

You can offer a printed card with a review link or code as an alternative. Test it on your own phone first, and make sure it opens the correct business. A link that leads to a similarly named plumber wastes the request and confuses the customer.

[Google's review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) explains how businesses can share a review link and prohibits incentives for reviews. A free drain treatment, a discounted service call, or a gift card in exchange for posting is still an incentive.

## Make the request fair across your plumbing jobs

Use a small closeout checklist rather than relying on memory between calls. The person finishing the invoice or customer handoff can record whether the request was offered. That keeps the office and technician from both sending the same message.

A useful process looks like this:

1. Confirm the completed service and explain any remaining work.
2. Make sure the customer has the invoice and contact details.
3. Offer an optional review link using your normal communication process.
4. Record that you asked so another employee doesn't repeat it.
5. Respect a decline and stop if the customer doesn't want follow-up.

Don't send unhappy customers to a private survey while sending happy customers to Google. That is review gating. [Google's contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews and discouraging negative ones.

Keep a help channel available to everyone, alongside the same opportunity to review. "Contact us if anything needs attention" should be a service promise, not a detour that hides criticism. This is especially important when a drain backs up again and you need to discuss the limits of the original visit.

## Reply to complaints without arguing about the house

A public complaint about a plumbing bill can tempt you to post the whole job history. Don't disclose the customer's address, payment details, or embarrassing information about the property. Future customers need to see how you handle a disagreement, not inspect your invoice notes.

For a scheduling complaint, you might write: "I'm sorry the arrival communication was frustrating. Please contact our office so we can review the visit and follow up with you." Adapt the wording to facts you know, and avoid admitting a cause you haven't checked.

For a disputed charge, explain your general process briefly: "We'd like to review the estimate and invoice with you directly." Then actually review them. If your office failed to explain a diagnostic fee, improve the explanation for the next customer.

Don't bargain for removal. Resolving a callback, honoring your stated policy, or correcting a billing error shouldn't depend on a changed review. Report content through Google's process only when you believe it violates the rules, rather than because it is unfavorable.

## Let the feedback improve the next service visit

Read reviews for repeated details: late arrival updates, unexplained charges, muddy footprints, clear options, or careful cleanup. These are concrete parts of a plumbing visit your crew can discuss. A pattern of confusion about camera inspection charges calls for clearer estimates, not a new review script.

Use positive feedback carefully on your website, with permission where appropriate and without changing its meaning. Don't turn a customer's praise for one appointment into a claim that every emergency gets the same arrival time. Keep genuine reviews distinct from your own service descriptions.

Your [Google Business Profile](/blog/plumbing-google-business-profile/) needs accurate hours and services alongside the reviews. Your [plumbing website](/blog/website-plumbing/) should explain fees, coverage, and booking so callers arrive with realistic expectations. Together, those details support the practical work of [getting more calls from Google](/blog/plumbing-more-calls-from-google/).

Track the process simply. Note whether eligible completed visits received a request and whether customers found the link confusing. Don't set a star-rating quota for technicians or pressure them to collect reviews while standing in a customer's kitchen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I offer a discount for an honest review?

No, don't exchange a discount or free service for a Google review. Asking for an honest opinion doesn't make the reward acceptable.

### Should I ask after an emergency plumbing call?

You can ask after the visit is complete and the immediate stress has passed. Keep it optional and use the same completed-service process you use for other customers.

### What if the customer already left a review last year?

Don't pressure repeat customers to post again after every faucet repair or maintenance visit. Keep a note of prior requests so your follow-up remains considerate.

### Can I write the review for someone who struggles online?

Let the customer choose their words and use their own account. You can explain where the link goes, but don't take over their review or tell them which rating to select.

## The short version

Ask consistently after completed plumbing visits, share a working link, and leave the rating to the customer. Never buy reviews or filter out dissatisfied customers. Treat recurring feedback as a reason to improve the service itself.
