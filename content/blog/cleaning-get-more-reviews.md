You finish a careful clean, send the invoice, and move to the next property. Asking for a review can easily get forgotten, especially when the customer is not home. A simple follow-up routine can help you collect honest feedback without turning the handover into a sales pitch.

## Ask after the customer can judge the clean

For a first home clean, choose a point after the customer has returned and had an opportunity to look around. A review request sent while the crew is still working asks them to judge an unfinished service. For an empty-property clean, send it after the agreed completion message and any walkthrough.

Recurring cleaning needs a different rhythm. Ask after the client has experienced your normal service rather than attaching a fresh request to every visit forever. Track whether you already asked so a loyal customer is not repeatedly prompted by several team members.

For offices, contact the person who oversees the cleaning and has firsthand experience of the service. The person who pays the bill may not be the person who checks the kitchen and meeting rooms each morning. Do not ask someone to describe results they have never seen.

Apply your timing rule consistently. Do not invite only the people who praised you during the walkthrough. A review process should welcome ordinary and critical experiences too.

## Make the request brief and easy to use

Use the review link associated with your correct business profile, then test it before adding it to a message. It should take the customer toward the right business, not make them search through several similarly named cleaning companies. Keep the request separate from urgent access or payment instructions.

Here are example scripts to adapt, not customer quotes:

- **After a home clean:** "Thank you for having us clean your home. If you would like to share your experience, you can leave an honest Google review using this link. It is completely optional."
- **After a move-out clean:** "Your cleaning visit is complete. Once you have had a chance to check the work, you are welcome to leave an honest review using this link. Thank you for booking with us."
- **For an office contact:** "Thank you for coordinating our cleaning visits. If you would like to describe your experience working with our team, here is our Google review link."

Use a communication channel appropriate to your relationship with that customer. Keep the wording short enough to understand at a glance. The [general review request guide](/blog/how-to-get-customers-to-leave-reviews-on-google-without-being-pushy/) offers more ways to make the request convenient.

## Keep rewards and rating filters out of the process

Do not offer a free oven clean, a discount on the next visit, or entry into a prize draw in exchange for a review. Google prohibits incentives for posting, changing, or removing reviews. Its [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) explains the rule.

Avoid a form that sends happy customers to Google while directing unhappy customers only to a private inbox. You can provide a way to contact your team about a problem, but everyone should have the same opportunity to review publicly. Do not make the public link depend on a satisfaction score.

Never ask for a particular star rating or give customers text to copy as their own opinion. Your staff should not post on their behalf. The customer's account and words should remain theirs.

You can mention that comments about communication or the service itself may help future customers, but leave the content open. An honest account of a routine visit is useful without a list of search phrases.

## Build the request into your actual job routine

Choose who owns the request. For a small crew, it might be the person sending the completion message. For an office cleaning contract, it might be the account contact who handles service checks. Avoid having both the cleaner and the office send overlapping messages.

A simple internal record can include the completed job, request date, and whether a reminder was sent. You do not need to keep screenshots of customer reviews beside private property details. Track the process without collecting unnecessary information.

If you choose to remind customers, use one gentle follow-up and stop if they decline. Do not repeatedly chase someone who has not responded. A review is optional, and silence does not require an explanation.

Make sure your [Google Business Profile](/blog/cleaning-google-business-profile/) has the right name and contact details before you send customers there. A confusing or outdated listing creates doubt at the exact moment you ask someone to support your business.

## Respond without revealing the household's business

A positive reply can simply thank the reviewer for their feedback. Do not add that you cleaned before their vacation, let yourselves in with a key, or handled a difficult family situation. Even a friendly detail can reveal more than the customer intended to share.

For a complaint, acknowledge the concern and offer a direct way to discuss the visit. An example reply is: "Thank you for raising this. We would like to understand what was missed and review the agreed cleaning scope. Please contact our office so we can discuss the visit with you."

Do not post private messages, entry codes, invoice details, or accusations about how the property looked. Check the booking notes privately, listen to the customer, and follow your normal service recovery process. The [bad review reply guide](/blog/how-to-reply-to-a-bad-review/) can help you keep the public response calm.

Fixing a problem should not depend on removing a review. If the feedback identifies a repeated issue, such as unclear oven-cleaning exclusions, improve the service description and staff checklist as well as addressing that visit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I ask every recurring customer after every visit?

You can build review requests into your process without making them relentless. Keep a record of earlier requests and use a sensible cadence for established clients. A service check after a visit does not always need another public review request.

### Should I wait until a complaint is resolved?

Handle the complaint promptly through your normal service process. Do not selectively exclude dissatisfied customers from a review invitation that others receive. Keep service recovery independent of whether someone posts or changes a review.

### Can my cleaners ask in person?

Yes, a brief optional invitation can fit naturally at a completed walkthrough. Give the customer a way to review later rather than watching them do it. Staff should never pressure someone for a rating or take their phone to post.

### What if a review mentions a specific cleaner?

You can thank the customer without adding the employee's schedule or personal details. Share appropriate praise internally according to your team's normal practice. Keep public replies focused on the service experience.
