What should go on your FAQ page if you want it to help customers and make sense in AI search? Start with the questions that hold up real jobs: whether you serve a town, what an estimate includes, or what someone must do before you arrive. A page of honest answers is useful even when a search tool never quotes it.

## Which questions belong on your FAQ?

Collect questions from recent phone calls, estimate requests, and booking messages. Write down the customer's actual uncertainty, without including private details. Choose questions where an answer helps someone decide whether to contact you or prepare for a visit.

An interior painter might hear whether furniture must be moved and whether repairing small nail holes is included. A mobile mechanic might need to explain where a vehicle can be worked on safely. A house cleaner may need to distinguish cleaning inside an oven from wiping its exterior.

Start with a short working list:

- Do you provide the particular service I need?
- Do you travel to my location?
- What does the quoted work include?
- What information do you need to give an estimate?
- What should I do before the appointment?
- How do I change or cancel a booking?

These are prompts for your own decisions, not questions to publish unchanged. Replace them with wording specific to the work you do.

## How do you write an answer that stands on its own?

Give the answer in the opening sentence. Then explain the condition, exception, or next step that matters. Name the service so the paragraph is understandable even when someone arrives directly from a search.

For a fence installer, a sample answer might read: "Removal of the existing fence is included only when it appears on your written estimate. Tell us whether you want the old posts and concrete removed so we can inspect the site and price that work." Use that wording only if it matches your business's process.

Keep related information together. A carpet cleaner answering whether furniture is moved should explain which items the team will move and what the customer must move beforehand. Don't put the yes on one page and the important exception in a separate policy the customer is unlikely to find.

For help turning spoken explanations into readable paragraphs, see [writing website text when you are not a writer](/blog/how-to-write-website-text-when-you-re-not-a-writer/).

## Should questions live on a service page or one FAQ page?

Place a question where the customer needs its answer. A page about drain clearing should explain whether a camera inspection is included, if customers commonly ask. Someone reading that page should not have to search a general FAQ to understand the service.

Use a central FAQ for policies shared across the business. Service area, appointment changes, payment process, and how to request an estimate may fit there. Link between the service page and the shared answer when necessary.

Choose one main place to maintain each policy. If your cancellation process appears in several places, record those locations so changes stay consistent. Contradictory answers create more work for the person answering the phone.

Our guide to [writing a service page that helps customers decide](/blog/how-to-write-a-service-page-that-ranks-and-converts/) explains how to fit questions around the service description. A good FAQ supports that page instead of repeating the whole thing.

## What does AI search need from the answers?

Write for a person trying to solve a problem. Clear wording gives a reader a usable answer and provides information a search system can potentially summarize. It does not create a right to be quoted or recommended.

Google's [current AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says there is no special writing format or ideal page length required for AI search. So do not force every answer into an arbitrary word count or repeat the same phrase across several headings.

Do make qualifications explicit. "We offer evening appointments by arrangement" says something different from "open evenings." If an assistant summarizes your answer, you want the condition close to the promise. The same clarity helps a customer reading quickly on a phone.

Use information your business can verify. Avoid calling your service the safest, cheapest, or best unless you can substantiate the claim. Specific details about what happens during a visit are more useful than a broad claim of superiority.

The guide to [writing content AI assistants can quote](/blog/writing-website-content-ai-assistants-can-quote/) covers clear, self-contained explanations in more detail.

## Does adding FAQ schema help?

FAQ schema is a way to label questions and answers in a page's code. The [Schema.org FAQPage definition](https://schema.org/FAQPage) describes the type. Those labels are separate from the readable FAQ your customer sees.

As of September 2026, Google's FAQ rich results are discontinued. Google's [documentation update log](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) records that they stopped appearing on May 7, 2026. Older advice saying a small FAQ addition will earn expanded questions in Google results should not guide your decision.

That change does not make clear answers useless. It means a specific search display feature is no longer a reason to buy FAQ markup. Nor should anyone promise that adding this markup will make an AI assistant quote your page.

If your website already includes FAQ schema, ask whoever maintains it to keep it accurate. Do not add extra claims in code that customers cannot read on the page. Our guide to [structured data for AI search](/blog/structured-data-for-ai-search-what-small-businesses-need/) separates useful labeling from unsupported promises.

## How do you keep the FAQ useful after publishing?

Give each answer a quick reality check before publishing. Ask the person who schedules work whether the answer matches what they tell customers. For pricing or booking questions, compare it with the current estimate and confirmation process.

Then open the page on your phone and follow the customer's next step. If an answer says to request an estimate, make sure the form works and asks for the details you actually need. If customers must call about urgent work, make the correct number easy to find.

Revisit answers whenever a policy, service area, or booking process changes. Remove seasonal promises when they expire. Add a new question when it repeatedly causes confusion, and shorten answers that bury the decision under background information.

Ask your office staff whether callers still get stuck on the same point. If they do, try clearer wording or a better location on the site.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions should I start with?

Start with the questions that most often delay a booking or cause a misunderstanding. A short page with useful answers is easier to maintain than a long list written to fill space. Add questions when customers show you a real gap.

### Can I copy another company's FAQ?

Use competitor pages only to notice topics you may have overlooked. Write your answers from your actual services and policies, because another company's preparation requirements or estimate process may differ. Copying their promises could leave you explaining work you never intended to offer.

### Should I put prices in FAQ answers?

Include pricing information when you can state it accurately and explain what is covered. For work requiring inspection, describe what affects the quote and what information you need. A clear explanation is better than publishing a starting price that almost no customer qualifies for.

### Can the answers guarantee a place in AI search?

No format or FAQ label guarantees a citation or recommendation. Write answers that help customers, then check whether public descriptions of your business are accurate. Treat any search appearance as an outcome to observe, not a promise the page can make.

## The short version

Build your FAQ from real questions and put the direct answer first. Keep conditions beside the answer, place service questions on the relevant pages, and review policies when they change. FAQ markup does not guarantee AI exposure or restore Google's discontinued FAQ rich results.
