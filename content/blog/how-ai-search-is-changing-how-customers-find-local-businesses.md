Are customers going to ask an AI assistant for a contractor instead of looking through websites? They can already use search tools that summarize answers and help compare options. Your job is to make your business easy to understand and contact, while treating claims about guaranteed AI recommendations with caution.

## Understand the different ways customers can search

Google AI Overviews summarize some searches, while AI Mode supports more involved questions and comparisons. Both can show links to supporting websites, but they do not necessarily return the same answers or sources. Google's [explanation of its AI search features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) describes those differences.

ChatGPT also has web search. When it uses that tool, it can show sources in the conversation, according to the [official OpenAI documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/web-search). A spoken request is another way to ask a question, rather than evidence that every voice assistant uses the same information.

For your business, this means a customer might arrive with a shortlist and a specific concern. Ask what they have already read. You may need to confirm the service they want or correct an assumption before discussing the appointment.

## Think about the questions behind the call

Imagine a homeowner asking which nearby company repairs older boilers and offers appointments after work. Another might want a landscaper who handles drainage problems rather than weekly mowing. Those are different buying decisions, even if both customers start with a broad search.

Build a short list from actual phone calls:

- Which equipment or materials do you work on?
- Do you repair existing installations, replace them, or both?
- Which towns can you reach for this type of job?
- What happens before you can provide a firm price?
- What should someone have ready when contacting you?

The useful part is the answer, not guessing the exact sentence a customer will type. If you service gas furnaces but not oil furnaces, say so. That distinction helps someone decide whether to call and gives your staff fewer unsuitable requests to sort through.

## Make your basic facts agree

Check your website alongside your main business listings. Compare the public business name, phone number, regular hours, services, and area covered. A changed number left on your contact page is a direct customer problem, regardless of how someone finds the page.

Separate facts that sound similar but mean different things. Answering messages on weekends does not mean you dispatch technicians on Sundays. Serving a town does not mean you have an office there. A repair company taking future bookings is not necessarily available for an emergency visit tonight.

Keep one approved list of these details for whoever updates your website and listings. Our guide to [consistent business information for AI answers](/blog/why-consistent-business-information-matters-for-ai-answers/) explains how to handle that work. Accuracy also makes it easier to investigate an incorrect answer because you can compare it with a clear source.

## Give each important service a useful explanation

A page headed "Our Services" with a long list of nouns leaves the customer to guess. A useful water heater page could explain whether you handle tank and tankless units, how an assessment works, and what information helps with scheduling. An auto repair page could distinguish diagnostic appointments from an immediate repair promise.

Add a real example when you have permission to share it. Describe the problem, the work performed, and a relevant limitation. Leave out customer addresses and other private details.

Write a direct answer near the beginning, then explain what changes the answer. Our guide to [writing website content AI assistants can quote](/blog/writing-website-content-ai-assistants-can-quote/) covers that approach. Treat it as clear communication, not a secret writing formula. A useful page should still earn its place if an assistant never cites it.

## Check access before buying an AI add-on

Ask your website provider whether search systems can reach your public pages and read their main content. Also ask whether any previous privacy or crawler settings prevent the kind of search visibility you want. Request an explanation in ordinary language before changing those settings.

Search access and model training are separate questions. OpenAI documents independent controls for OAI-SearchBot, used for search, and GPTBot, associated with training. Allowing one does not require allowing the other. See its [crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

For Google, special AI files and special schema markup are not required. Its [current optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says llms.txt does not affect Google Search visibility or rankings. Read [whether a small business needs llms.txt](/blog/what-llms-txt-is-and-whether-a-small-business-needs-one/) before paying for a package built around that file.

## Measure useful inquiries and check wrong answers

Set aside time each month to ask a few realistic questions about your business and services. Record the question, date, answer, and any sources shown. If an assistant lists the wrong hours, follow its source links and correct the information you control. Keep a note of changes so you can check again later.

Do not treat one favorable answer as a permanent position. Likewise, one missing mention does not tell you that your website has failed. These checks are useful for finding factual problems, but they are not a complete picture of customer demand.

Track inquiries alongside booked work. Ask callers how they found you, and distinguish a mention from an appointment you could actually serve. ## Frequently asked questions

### Should I stop working on ordinary Google search?

No. Google says the basic practices used for search remain relevant to its AI features. Keep useful pages, accurate listings, and a clear way to contact you in place while you evaluate new sources of inquiries.

### Can someone guarantee an AI assistant will recommend me?

Treat that promise as a reason to ask for specifics. Require a written description of the work, how results will be measured, and what the provider actually controls. A screenshot of one answer does not establish ongoing customer demand.

### Should I rewrite every page as questions and answers?

Start with pages that leave real customer questions unanswered. A short explanation, service list, or appointment guide may be clearer than a long FAQ. Choose the format that helps someone decide whether your business fits their job.

### What if an assistant says I offer something I do not?

Check any cited pages, including old versions of your service descriptions and listings. Correct those you control and request corrections elsewhere when appropriate. Make the limitation clear on the relevant service page, then check again without expecting an immediate change.

## The short version

AI search adds ways for people to investigate your business before making contact. Clear services, current facts, and useful answers are practical work you can do today. Watch for inaccurate claims and measure the inquiries that turn into suitable jobs.
