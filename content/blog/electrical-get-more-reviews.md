You can finish an excellent electrical job and still leave with no public feedback. Customers may be relieved that the circuit works or pleased with their new lighting, but they don't automatically think about Google reviews. A clear, optional request at the right point in the job makes it easier for them to share their experience.

## Match the timing to the electrical work

Different electrical jobs finish differently. For a troubleshooting visit, the handoff might include explaining the findings, completed repair, and any recommended next step. For a planned panel upgrade, the project may involve coordination and follow-up beyond the day your crew installs equipment.

Ask when the customer has received the service you are asking them to review. Don't treat a signed estimate as a completed installation. Don't tell a customer the whole project is finished if agreed work or a required handoff remains outstanding.

Use consistent milestones. A completed diagnostic visit can receive a request about that visit, even if the customer chooses not to proceed with a larger repair. A lighting installation request can follow the walkthrough and explanation of the completed scope.

Avoid asking while you are still seeking approval for extra work. A homeowner shouldn't feel that posting a review affects how their repair will be handled. Keep the service decision and the feedback request separate.

## Give the customer a short, neutral invitation

A technician can say: "Thanks for having us out. If you'd like to review your experience, our office can send you the Google link." That makes the choice clear without asking the customer to perform a favor on the spot.

For an approved follow-up channel, use a message such as: "Thank you for choosing us for your electrical service. You're welcome to share an honest review using this link. We appreciate your feedback." Add the actual link and check it before using the message.

Don't request five stars or a particular phrase. A customer who had an EV charger installed might choose to discuss scheduling, explanations, or cleanup. They shouldn't need to repeat "EV charger electrician near me" to satisfy your marketing plan.

Printed cards can help customers who prefer to act later. Keep the wording neutral and make sure any code opens the correct review destination. Don't stand over a customer while they choose a rating, and don't ask to use their phone.

[Google's review request guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) allows businesses to share a review link but prohibits incentives. A free outlet replacement, a discount on future work, or entry into a prize drawing should not be tied to a review.

## Ask fairly across repairs and larger projects

Build the request into your closeout checklist so it doesn't depend on which technician remembers. The office can record that a request was offered, preventing repeated messages from the estimator, electrician, and scheduler.

Use the same basic process for customers who are eligible to review their actual experience. Don't send only customers who praised the crew to Google. Don't use a satisfaction survey to direct criticism privately while routing positive feedback to the public page.

That practice is review gating. [Google's contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) prohibits discouraging negative reviews and selectively asking for positive ones. Keep an accessible support contact for everyone without making it a barrier to posting feedback.

Be thoughtful with commercial projects. A general contractor, property manager, and tenant may all interact with your crew, but don't pressure people with limited firsthand experience to review the business. Direct the optional invitation to the person who actually received or managed your service.

For repeat customers, check your history before asking again. A property manager who books you for several small repairs shouldn't receive the same request after every visit. Consistency means a fair process, not constant reminders.

## Protect customer privacy when replying

Electrical reviews can expose more about a property than the customer intended. Avoid adding details about security lighting, access arrangements, vacant buildings, or the condition of wiring inside a home. Keep your reply focused on the service experience.

For a positive review, a short thank-you is enough. You can acknowledge clear communication or the customer's feedback without expanding on the project. Don't turn every reply into an advertisement for panel upgrades or generators.

For a complaint about an estimate, use calm language: "We'd like to understand the difference between what you expected and what was proposed. Please contact our office so we can review it with you." Then check the actual estimate and notes before responding further.

If someone says a callback went unanswered, investigate the contact trail. Don't publicly accuse them of calling the wrong number or failing to understand the work. A future customer is watching whether your company handles questions responsibly.

Resolve genuine issues without making help conditional on removing a review. If feedback appears to violate platform rules, use the reporting process with supporting facts. A low rating alone isn't a reason to treat a review as invalid.

## Use reviews to improve estimates and handoffs

Look for repeated themes that you can act on. Customers may praise clear circuit labels, careful cleanup, or a straightforward explanation of options. They may also reveal confusion about access needs, appointment changes, or what an estimate includes.

Discuss those themes with your team. If EV charger customers repeatedly misunderstand the assessment process, improve the relevant page and estimate conversation. If remodel customers are unclear about responsibility for wall repairs, make your scope clearer before work begins.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can we ask after a diagnostic visit with no repair?

Yes, if the customer received a genuine service and the request describes that experience accurately. Don't imply a repair or installation happened when it did not.

### Should we wait for every part of a panel upgrade to finish?

Ask about the completed project after its agreed handoff is complete. If you seek feedback on a separate completed visit, make that limited scope clear rather than presenting the whole job as finished.

### Can a customer mention the electrician by name?

They can choose what to share about their experience. Don't require names, keywords, or specific praise as part of your request.

### What if a customer doesn't use Google?

Thank them for considering it and don't pressure them to create an account. You can still listen to their feedback through your normal customer service channel.
