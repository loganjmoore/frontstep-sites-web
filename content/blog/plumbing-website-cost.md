How much should you spend on a website for your plumbing business? Start by pricing a site that helps people book the work you actually want, whether that is drain clearing, water heater replacement, or everyday repairs. The right budget depends more on those needs and ongoing upkeep than on how impressive a sales presentation looks.

## Price the plumbing website you need first

A solo service plumber and a company handling commercial installations do not need the same website. Before requesting prices, write down your main jobs, coverage area, hours, and how you want customers to contact you. This becomes the scope every provider quotes against.

For a residential service business, a sensible starting scope might include a homepage, service information, an about section, and a contact page. Give water heaters or sewer repairs separate pages if those jobs need a fuller explanation. Someone searching "water heater leaking" needs different information from a landlord arranging several faucet replacements.

Ask for a working call button, a short request form, and readable pages on a phone. Include help writing the words if you do not already have them. Our guide to [what a plumber website needs](/blog/website-plumbing/) explains which details deserve space before you pay for extras.

Keep the initial scope written down. "A complete plumbing website" can mean very different things to two providers, especially when one expects you to supply every paragraph and photograph.

## Compare DIY, done-for-you, and custom work

A DIY builder fits if you want to control the layout yourself and have time to write, upload photos, and check the result. The subscription is only part of your investment. You still need to assemble the service pages and maintain the information.

As of September 2026, Wix Light is $17/month billed yearly ($204/year), and Squarespace Basic is $19/month. Those are plan prices, not a quote for a finished plumbing website. Check the current checkout and written terms before buying, and ask what your chosen setup requires beyond the plan.

A done-for-you service can suit an owner who wants to send business details and request changes instead of learning a builder. Compare the actual deliverables, change allowance, and ownership terms. Do not assume every subscription includes the same work.

Freelancer and agency costs vary widely. A typical custom project is often a few thousand dollars, while more involved work can run substantially higher. Treat that as a broad planning range, not a price promise. An agency may fit a company coordinating multiple locations, advertising campaigns, and a larger marketing effort.

The [done-for-you versus DIY guide](/blog/done-for-you-website-vs-building-it-yourself/) can help you decide how much website work you want on your own plate.

## Understand what makes a plumbing quote grow

Useful content takes work. A drain-cleaning page should explain the problems you handle, how you assess the job, and when you discuss charges. A tankless water heater page needs different details. Paying someone to interview you and write those pages is different from buying a blank layout.

Other work that can increase the scope includes:

- Sorting and preparing photos from completed plumbing jobs.
- Rewriting old pages that advertise services you no longer offer.
- Moving an existing site while preserving useful page addresses.
- Connecting a request form to an existing office workflow.
- Building separate information for genuinely different locations.

Ask what is standard and what is extra. A quote that includes copy, photo preparation, and launch checks may be more useful than a lower quote that leaves all three to you.

Avoid buying dozens of town pages just because a provider offers them. A page that changes only the town name does little to help a customer decide whether you can handle a backed-up sewer line there. Start with accurate coverage information and add local detail where you have something useful to say.

## Separate essential contact tools from optional extras

An emergency plumbing customer needs a phone number and an honest availability statement. That does not automatically require a complicated booking system. A direct call may be the better fit when dispatch depends on the job, distance, and crew availability.

For routine work, a short form can collect the town, contact details, and a description such as "replace two bathroom faucets." Explain that submitting it requests a callback, not a confirmed appointment. Ask your provider to test where those requests arrive.

Online booking can make sense when you have predictable appointment types and a schedule someone maintains. Before paying for it, decide how you will handle a drain job that runs long or a water heater replacement needing additional assessment. Technology cannot resolve an unclear scheduling policy.

Skip decorative extras until the basics work. A large video of water flowing through pipes should not slow down the page someone opens while standing beside a leaking kitchen sink.

## Check the ongoing bill and your ownership

Ask for the first-year total and the expected recurring cost in plain language. Have the provider identify the domain, hosting, content changes, and any separate tools. If an item is optional, it should be clearly marked that way.

Then ask practical questions about everyday changes:

- Who updates holiday hours and emergency availability?
- What happens when you stop servicing a particular heater type?
- Are new service pages included or quoted separately?
- How do you submit a change, and when should it be finished?
- What can you take with you if you leave?

Domain ownership deserves its own answer. Know whose name is on the registration and how you regain control if the relationship ends. Do not confuse owning the domain with receiving a portable copy of every part of the website.

Use these [questions to ask before hiring a website provider](/blog/what-to-ask-before-you-hire-someone-to-build-your-website/) to get the important details in writing. A clear recurring arrangement is easier to assess than a cheap starting price with unclear upkeep.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a cheap plumbing website necessarily a bad choice?

No, a small site can do the essential work well. Check whether it explains your services, works on a phone, and delivers inquiries reliably. Judge the scope and ongoing support rather than the price alone.

### Do I need separate pages for every plumbing service?

Start with the services customers regularly ask about and the work you want more of. A water heater replacement page can be useful, while several nearly identical fixture pages may not be. Add pages when you can answer distinct questions.

### Should I pay more for a promise of Google rankings?

Do not use a ranking promise as the basis for your budget. Ask what concrete work is included, such as accurate service information and fixing broken pages. Your spending decision should rest on deliverables you can inspect.

### What should I check before making the final payment?

Open the site on your phone, tap the call button, and send a test request. Confirm your services, coverage, fees, and availability are accurate. Also verify the agreed ownership and access arrangements.

## The short version

Price a clear, useful plumbing website before adding optional tools. Compare the full recurring cost, the work included, and the time you will spend maintaining it. Get ownership and change terms in writing.
